## Directions (all on the tank, with 1 predator against 5 prey sharing one brain)

**A. The full five-rung ladder (recommended)**

| Rung | What learns | Against | New idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | prey | a scripted predator | foraging plus fleeing at once |
| P2 | predator | frozen P1 prey | hunting a learned opponent |
| P3 | both, taking turns (freeze one, train the other, swap every N batches) | each other | non-stationarity you control |
| P4 | both at once | each other | full co-evolution, where cycling is expected |
| P5 | both | a pool of saved past snapshots | the fix for cycling and forgetting |

The closing experiment is the cross-play table for P4 and P5. Why: one idea per rung, and P3 sits between "one side frozen" and "both moving", so a P4 failure can be pinned on simultaneity rather than on a bug.

**B. Four rungs, without P3:** P1 → P2 → P4 → P5. One run shorter, but the step from "one side frozen" to "both learning at once" is a cliff.

**C. Four rungs, without P2:** P1 → P3 → P4 → P5. The predator never learns against a fixed opponent first, so a poor predator in P3 has two possible causes.

## Stress test
- **Time.**
  - P3–P5 train two PPO learners together. Each run fits in an hour only with small networks and the vectorised tank.
  - The **cross-play table is the real cost.** For example, 5 predator snapshots × 5 prey snapshots × 100 episodes = 2,500 episodes. It needs its own time limit, roughly ≤ 15 minutes. The spec will set it.
- **Bars for both sides:**
  - prey must beat a random and a scripted predator (one that chases the nearest prey);
  - the predator must beat random and scripted prey (prey that flee the predator and grab nearby pellets).
  - Bars apply to each rung's *final* snapshots, through the same `null-hypothesis-testing` route.
- **Speeds:** the predator is 1.5× faster than prey, matching the ball, so it can catch them but not trivially. The time cost stops it from waiting.
- **Expected failure:** P4 may cycle or collapse. That's the finding, not a defect, and the cross-play table shows it. The real risk is that **P5 doesn't fix it within the hour**, which would be reported honestly in its note.
- **The shared core** has to support **two learners in one environment**: agents grouped by team, one brain per team. That's the architecture's job. It's the core change the brief currently rules out (below).
- **Dependency:** this builds on M4's shared brain and nearby view. Its stage comes after S2, and its spec waits for M3 as you decided.

## Hidden bets
- Two PPO learners on 6 bodies learn anything at all within an hour on a laptop processor.
- Respawning pellets plus the time cost are enough shaping; no hand-tuned bonuses are needed.
- A pool of 5–10 snapshots is enough to damp cycling.

## Fit check
- **Goals:** this serves Goals 1–5, so **no new goal**.
- **Constraints:** unchanged. It's still a small 2D continuous game under the one-hour limit.
- **The Not Doing list is contradicted.** "Shared pieces cover Snake and the tank, nothing more" rules out the two-learner core change. The proposed rewording is: **"Designing a framework beyond what the planned environments need: shared pieces cover Snake, the tank and predator-prey, nothing more."** That needs your agreement. Without it, the idea gets parked.
- Not a different problem or user.
