I want to learn quant research on US equities properly and find out whether any signal survives honest testing. Ad-hoc research fools itself: delisted stocks go missing, models peek at the future, and most good-looking results are chance. Without trustworthy data and disciplined tests, I can't tell a real finding from a lucky one.

Me: one person doing personal research and simulated paper trading.

1. Every result rests on daily data I can trust: index membership as of each date, delisted stocks included, corporate actions applied, every download validated.

3. No claim without a test: each hypothesis is written down before it is run, tested against chance, and its outcome recorded, failures included.
4. A method earns its place only by beating a simpler benchmark out of sample. Classical risk-based portfolios are judged on realised variance; anything driven by machine learning must beat them.
5. I can explore the dataset and watch download and validation runs live in a local browser app.

- S&P 500 membership history is reproduced, delisted members included.

**Writing rules:**

- **One requirement, one behavior.** If a requirement contains "and", it's probably two.
- **Every requirement has at least one acceptance criterion** in Given/When/Then form. If you can't write one, the requirement isn't clear yet.
- **Describe behavior, not implementation.** "The customer sees their usual order" — not "the app queries the orders table."
- **Use exact numbers.** "≤ 2 seconds", "up to 50 regulars", never "fast" or "many".
- **Every feature names one stage.** Its `**Stage:**` line holds only the ID of the roadmap stage that first builds it. `dt lint` fails a feature with no stage, or one naming no stage row.
- **Requirement IDs are permanent.** `CODE-NN`, numbered within the feature, never reused or renumbered.
- **Quality requirements only when measurable.** Leave out generic wishes ("should be secure"); keep checkable ones ("passwords are never shown in full"). Cost, licence and budget limits are `QR-` items ("data costs ≤ $50 a month"), never `DATA-` or feature requirements.
- **No tech stack, libraries, vendors, schemas, endpoints, or file structure, even when the user names them.** Write the behavior the name stands for. The name itself belongs to `architecture-design`, which reads the idea file and the conversation.
- **Describe the product as it is now, not how it got here.** Requirement text, scope lists and assumptions carry no change notes, provenance tags ("from the field-photo idea") or process notes ("numbers chosen for review"). History goes in one Changelog row per round, and in git.

**Data and ML projects — extra rules:**

- **Specify the data, not the source.** `DATA-NN` requirements say what the data must contain and guarantee ("hourly readings for every meter in the service area, including decommissioned ones, since 2015"; "every record is stamped with the date it became known"). Which API, vendor, or dataset meets them is an architecture decision, made by checking candidates against these requirements. Name a source only when the brief makes it a fixed constraint (our own database, a required benchmark).
- **Write data guarantees as testable criteria.** Leakage, look-ahead, selection bias, and coverage gaps are requirements, not hopes: "Given a forecast issued at time T, when the model requests weather, then no value published after T is returned."
- **Model quality is a requirement with a baseline.** Acceptance criteria name the metric, the held-out data, the threshold, and the baseline to beat: "Given the most recent 6 months of plates (never trained on), when ranked, then reviewing the top 20% catches ≥ 90% of scientist-failed plates, and beats ranking by Z′ alone." Never "the model is accurate".
- **No model types or libraries.** "Gradient boosting" or "PyTorch" are architecture decisions; the spec says what the model must achieve.
- **Learning projects: "build it by hand, then compare with a library" is a valid requirement. Write it without the names.** "The hand-written CNN reaches ≥ 90% of the accuracy of an established reference implementation on the same dataset." "The logistic regression is written from basic array operations; a reference library is used only to check its output." The architecture picks the reference.

# Quant Research Platform — Spec

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [brief.md](brief.md)

## Summary
A personal research platform for daily US large-cap equities. It builds a trusted, point-in-time S&P 500 dataset with delisted stocks and corporate actions, scores every method on one shared walk-forward schedule, records every hypothesis and its test outcome in a ledger, and works up from risk structure and classical portfolios to features, forecasts and trading agents. Each stage has to beat the stage before it out of sample. A local browser app explores the data and shows download and validation runs live. See the [brief](brief.md) for why.

## Users
| User | What they need to do |
|---|---|
| Researcher (the sole user) | Build and refresh the dataset, check its quality, register and run hypotheses, compare methods out of sample, read the outcomes, and explore data and runs in a local browser app |

## Scope
**In scope:** Universe membership, Price & corporate-action ingest, Download validation, Point-in-time data access, Data explorer, Live run monitor, Walk-forward engine, Hypothesis ledger, Simulated portfolio backtest, Factor removal, Correlation denoising, Covariance estimator ranking, Classical portfolios, Candidate features, Feature significance, Return & volatility forecasters, Forecast-driven portfolios, Trading agents, Final verdict

**Out of scope:**
- Real money, brokerage connections or order routing of any kind: simulated trading only
- Intraday data: daily bars only
- Any universe wider than the S&P 500 (including stocks that were members in the past)
- A shared, hosted or multi-user app: it runs locally, for one person
- Return prediction before risk estimation is settled: S3 and S4 start only after S2's covariance ranking is recorded
- Open-ended trading agents: an agent line of work stops when it fails to beat minimum variance after costs within its registered budget
- Fundamental, news or alternative data: features come from daily price, volume and corporate-action data only
- Short selling and leverage in the classical portfolios: they are long-only and fully invested

## Features

### Universe membership (`UNIV`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** For any trading date, the researcher can get the exact set of S&P 500 members on that date, including companies that later left the index or were delisted. Every stock has a permanent identity that survives ticker changes and ticker reuse.

**Requirements**
- **UNIV-01** The system returns the S&P 500 member list as of any trading date in the covered history.
  - Given a trading date D in coverage, when membership is requested for D, then every stock whose membership was effective on D is returned and no other stock is.
  - Given any trading date in coverage, when membership is requested, then the count is between 495 and 510.
- **UNIV-02** Members that were later removed or delisted appear on the dates they were members.
  - Given a company that was removed from the index after a bankruptcy, when membership is requested for a date before its removal, then it is included.
- **UNIV-03** Each stock carries a permanent identifier that never changes and is never reused.
  - Given a ticker that was used by two different companies at different times, when either company's history is requested, then only that company's data is returned.
  - Given a company that changed ticker, when its history is requested by its identifier, then it is one unbroken series across the change.
- **UNIV-04** Membership history is reproduced: it agrees with publicly announced index changes.
  - Given a check set of at least 30 publicly announced index additions and removals spread across the covered history (at least 5 of them delisted companies), when membership is compared with them, then every change takes effect on the announced effective date.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Company with two share classes both in the index → both are members, each with its own identifier
- Membership requested for a non-trading date → returns the membership of the most recent prior trading date, and says which date it used
- Membership requested outside covered history → error naming the covered date range; never an empty list

### Price & corporate-action ingest (`INGST`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** The researcher runs a download that fetches daily prices, volumes, corporate actions and membership changes for every stock that has ever been a member. The first run fetches full history. Later runs fetch only what is new or revised. Every run is recorded and its output is kept as a versioned snapshot.

**Requirements**
- **INGST-01** A full download fetches the complete history for every stock that was ever a member.
  - Given an empty store, when a full download completes, then every identifier returned by UNIV-01 for any covered date has price history covering its membership period.
- **INGST-02** An update download fetches only data newer than the last successful run, plus any vendor revisions.
  - Given a store last updated on day D, when an update runs on day D+3, then only records dated after D or revised since D are fetched.
- **INGST-03** Every download produces an immutable, dated snapshot that later runs never overwrite.
  - Given two successful downloads, when either snapshot is requested afterwards, then it returns exactly the data that run stored.
- **INGST-04** A vendor revision to an already-stored record is kept as a new version with both values on record.
  - Given a stored close that the vendor later revises, when the update completes, then both the old and the new value are retrievable, each with the date it was known.
- **INGST-05** A run that fails partway leaves the last good snapshot untouched and can be resumed.
  - Given a download interrupted after half the stocks, when the researcher resumes it, then only the unfinished stocks are fetched and the previous snapshot is still readable throughout.
- **INGST-06** Every run records its start and end time, scope, record counts, and any errors.
  - Given a completed or failed run, when its record is opened, then all of these fields are present.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Network failure or rate limit → retried with backoff up to 5 times per request, then the stock is marked failed in the run record; the run continues with the other stocks
- Vendor returns no data for a stock that should have it → marked failed in the run record, never stored as an empty history
- Credentials missing or rejected → the run stops before fetching anything, with a message naming the problem

### Download validation (`VALID`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** Every snapshot is validated before anything downstream can read it. Records that fail are quarantined with the reason recorded. A snapshot with blocking failures is never promoted to the current dataset. The data-analysis skill produces a data-quality report for each promoted snapshot.

**Requirements**
- **VALID-01** No snapshot becomes the current dataset until validation passes.
  - Given a snapshot with a blocking failure, when validation completes, then the previous current dataset stays current and the run record says why.
- **VALID-02** Price records are checked for internal consistency.
  - Given a daily bar with a non-positive price, high below low, or close outside [low, high], when validated, then it is quarantined with the failed check named.
- **VALID-03** Every member stock has a bar on every exchange trading day of its membership, or a recorded reason (halt, suspension) for the gap.
  - Given a member missing a bar on a trading day with no recorded reason, when validated, then the gap is reported as a failure.
- **VALID-04** Large daily moves must be explained.
  - Given an adjusted daily return beyond ±40%, when validated, then it passes only if a corporate action, delisting or recorded news-free check by the researcher explains it; otherwise it is flagged for review.
- **VALID-05** Corporate actions are applied correctly.
  - Given a stock with a split or dividend on day D, when its adjusted return for D is computed, then it equals the economic total return (price change plus dividend, split-neutral) within 0.01 percentage points.
- **VALID-06** Membership integrity is checked on every snapshot.
  - Given a snapshot, when validated, then a failure is reported for any date whose member count falls outside 495–510 and for any member with no price data on a membership date.
- **VALID-07** Each promoted snapshot gets a data-quality report produced with the `data-analysis` skill.
  - Given a promoted snapshot, when the report is produced, then it covers coverage by date, missingness, return distributions and tails, and quarantined-record counts by check; it passes only if every quarantined record is listed with its reason.
- **VALID-08** Duplicate records are rejected.
  - Given two bars for the same stock and date in one snapshot, when validated, then both are quarantined and the failure is blocking.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Validation rules change → older snapshots can be revalidated, and the result is recorded against that snapshot
- Researcher overrides a flagged move → the override is recorded with its reason and date, and shows in the quality report

### Point-in-time data access (`PANEL`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** Every later stage reads data through one point-in-time view. Asked "as of date T", it returns only what was known by the close of T, for the stocks that were members then, with delisting returns included and no gaps filled with invented values.

**Requirements**
- **PANEL-01** An as-of-T request never returns information published after T's close.
  - Given a request as of T, when any record is returned, then its known-date is on or before T.
  - Given a membership change announced before T but effective after T, when membership as of T is requested, then the change is not applied.
- **PANEL-02** A returns panel for a date range contains only stocks that were members on each date.
  - Given a returns panel for a range, when a stock joined the index mid-range, then its returns appear only from its effective join date.
- **PANEL-03** A delisted stock's final return, through its last price or delisting payout, is included.
  - Given a stock delisted after a cash acquisition, when its returns are requested, then the last return reflects the cash paid per share.
  - Given a stock delisted after bankruptcy with no final price, when its returns are requested, then the last return is flagged as estimated and a delisting return of −100% is applied unless a final price is recorded.
- **PANEL-04** Missing values stay missing.
  - Given a stock with no bar on a trading day, when returns are requested, then that cell is reported missing, never zero.
- **PANEL-05** Every request can be pinned to a snapshot so results can be reproduced.
  - Given a request pinned to snapshot X, when it is repeated after newer snapshots exist, then the output is identical.
- **PANEL-06** Daily risk-free rates are available for the same dates as prices.
  - Given any trading date in coverage, when the risk-free rate is requested, then a daily rate is returned.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Request for a date before coverage starts → error naming the coverage start
- Request mixing snapshots → refused; one request reads one snapshot

Charts follow the `dataviz` skill. Layout and visual design follow the `frontend-design` skill.

**Requirements**
- **EXPL-01** The researcher can view the member list for any covered date.

- **EXPL-02** The researcher can open any stock ever in the universe, delisted ones included.

  - Given a stock with a split, when its page opens, then the split is marked on the chart on its date.
- **EXPL-04** The researcher can browse validation results for any snapshot.

- **EXPL-05** The researcher can see data coverage across the universe over time.

**Edge cases & errors**

**Requirements**
- **RUNS-01** A running download or validation shows live progress.

### Walk-forward engine (`WFWD`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** One shared schedule defines every training and test period. Every method, from covariance estimators to agents, is fitted only on its training window and scored on the same test periods, with a gap between training and test so that no training label overlaps a test outcome.

**Requirements**
- **WFWD-01** One schedule defines the test periods for every method.
  - Given any two methods scored by the platform, when their results are compared, then they cover exactly the same test periods.
- **WFWD-02** Test periods are consecutive blocks of 21 trading days, and the first starts after at least 756 trading days of history.
  - Given the covered history, when the schedule is built, then test blocks are 21 trading days, non-overlapping, consecutive, and the first begins on or after trading day 757.
- **WFWD-03** A gap separates each training window from its test period: at least 5 trading days, and never shorter than the longest label horizon the method uses.
  - Given a method whose labels look 21 days ahead, when its training data for a test period starting on day T is assembled, then no label's horizon ends on or after T, and the last training date is at least 21 trading days before T.
- **WFWD-04** A method sees only data available by the end of its training window when it is fitted.
  - Given a fit for a test period, when any input dated after the training window's end reaches the fit, then the run fails with a look-ahead error.
- **WFWD-05** The most recent 504 trading days are a held-back lockbox, scored once per hypothesis only after its walk-forward result is recorded.
  - Given a hypothesis with no recorded walk-forward outcome, when lockbox scoring is requested, then it is refused.
  - Given a hypothesis already scored on the lockbox, when lockbox scoring is requested again, then it is refused.
- **WFWD-06** The universe in every test period is the membership as of its start.
  - Given a test period starting on T, when portfolios are formed, then only stocks that were members on T are eligible.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Stock delisted mid test period → its delisting return counts; the position does not vanish from the score
- Method that cannot fit (too little data) for a period → that period is scored as a failure for that method, never skipped

### Hypothesis ledger (`LEDG`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Before any test runs, the researcher registers the hypothesis: the claim, the null, the metric, the test, the significance level, the data snapshot and test periods. Registration freezes it. The `null-hypothesis-testing` skill runs the test, and the outcome, pass or fail, is written back. No result is reported without a registered entry.

**Requirements**
- **LEDG-01** A hypothesis is registered with its claim, null, metric, test, significance level, correction family, snapshot and test periods before it runs.
  - Given a hypothesis missing any of these fields, when registration is attempted, then it is refused with the missing fields named.
- **LEDG-02** A registered hypothesis cannot be edited once its run starts.
  - Given a hypothesis whose run has started, when an edit is attempted, then it is refused; the researcher can only register a new, linked hypothesis.
- **LEDG-03** Every test is run with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill on the registered metric and test periods.
  - Given a registered hypothesis, when it is tested, then the skill receives the registered null, metric, periods and correction family; the hypothesis passes only if the null is rejected at 5% after multiple-testing correction across its family.
- **LEDG-04** Every outcome is recorded, failures included.
  - Given a completed test, when the ledger is read, then the entry shows the statistic, corrected p-value, effect size, and pass or fail.
- **LEDG-05** No comparison, ranking or claim is reported without a registered ledger entry.
  - Given a comparison run with no registered hypothesis, when its result is requested, then it is refused.
- **LEDG-06** The ledger can be listed by stage, feature and outcome.
  - Given the ledger, when filtered to failed S3 hypotheses, then exactly those entries are listed.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Run crashes mid test → the entry is marked incomplete, never pass or fail; re-running uses the same frozen registration
- Researcher abandons a registered hypothesis → it stays in the ledger marked abandoned, and still counts toward its correction family

### Simulated portfolio backtest (`BTEST`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Given target weights from any method at each rebalance, the backtest holds them through the test period on point-in-time returns, charges trading costs on turnover, and reports realised variance, after-cost return, Sharpe ratio and turnover per test period.

**Requirements**
- **BTEST-01** Portfolios rebalance at the start of each test period and drift with returns within it.
  - Given weights set at the start of a 21-day block, when the block is simulated, then daily weights drift with each stock's returns until the next rebalance.
- **BTEST-02** Trading costs of 10 basis points per side are charged on traded value.
  - Given a rebalance trading 20% of portfolio value, when costs are applied, then 0.02% of portfolio value is deducted.
- **BTEST-03** Each test period reports realised variance, after-cost return, turnover, and Sharpe ratio net of the risk-free rate.
  - Given a completed backtest, when its results are read, then all four values are present for every test period.
- **BTEST-04** A held stock that is delisted is closed at its delisting return.
  - Given a position in a stock delisted mid-period, when simulated, then the delisting return is applied and the proceeds are held as cash until the next rebalance.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Weights not summing to 1, or negative weights in a long-only method → the backtest refuses them with the offending total or stock named
- Weight on a stock that is not a member at rebalance → refused

### Factor removal (`FACT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** For each training window, the system extracts the dominant common factors from member returns, starting with the market mode. It chooses how many to keep by comparing eigenvalues with those of pure noise, and produces residual returns. Everything is estimated only on data available at the time.

**Requirements**
- **FACT-01** Factors are estimated only on each training window, never the full sample.
  - Given a test period starting on T, when its residual returns are computed, then factor loadings come from a fit whose data ends before T.
- **FACT-02** The number of factors is chosen per window: those whose eigenvalues exceed what pure-noise returns of the same shape would produce.
  - Given a window of pure-noise returns, when factors are chosen, then 0 are kept in at least 95% of 100 simulated trials.
- **FACT-03** Residual returns show much less cross-correlation than raw returns.
  - Given any training window, when residuals are computed, then the mean absolute pairwise correlation of residuals is at most 50% of that of raw returns, checked with the `data-analysis` skill.
- **FACT-04** Stocks are included only for the dates they have data; nothing is zero-filled.
  - Given a stock that joined mid-window, when factors are fitted, then its missing dates are excluded, not filled.
- **FACT-05** Residual returns are available as of any date for later stages.
  - Given a date T, when residual returns up to T are requested, then they use only loadings fitted before T.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Stock with fewer than 60 return observations in a window → excluded from that window's fit and listed as excluded
- Window where no eigenvalue beats noise → zero factors kept; residuals equal demeaned returns, and this is recorded

### Correlation denoising (`DENOI`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** The correlation matrix of each training window is cleaned: eigenvalues indistinguishable from noise are replaced while the signal eigenvalues and the matrix's trace are kept. The result is offered as one more covariance estimator.

**Requirements**
- **DENOI-01** Noise eigenvalues are replaced and signal eigenvalues are kept.
  - Given a window's correlation matrix, when denoised, then eigenvalues above the pure-noise edge are unchanged and those below it are replaced by their average.
- **DENOI-02** The denoised matrix is a valid correlation matrix.
  - Given any denoised matrix, when checked, then it is symmetric, positive definite, and has a unit diagonal within 1e-10.
- **DENOI-03** Denoising is fitted only on the training window.
  - Given a test period starting on T, when its denoised matrix is built, then it uses only returns dated before the window's end.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Fewer observations than stocks in a window → still produces a positive-definite matrix, and the ratio is recorded

### Covariance estimator ranking (`COV`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Several covariance estimators are compared by building the minimum-variance portfolio from each and measuring its realised variance on the shared test periods. The estimators are the sample estimate, shrinkage toward a structured target, a factor model on FACT's factors, and the DENOI matrix. The data-analysis skill characterises the estimators. Each pairwise claim is a ledger hypothesis.

**Requirements**
- **COV-01** Each estimator is scored by the realised out-of-sample variance of the minimum-variance portfolio built from it.
  - Given an estimator, when scored, then the score is the realised variance over all walk-forward test periods of its long-only minimum-variance portfolio from BTEST.
- **COV-02** Estimators are characterised with the `data-analysis` skill before ranking.
  - Given the estimators on the training windows, when characterised, then the skill reports eigenvalue spectra, condition numbers and stability across windows for each estimator.
- **COV-03** Each estimator's improvement over the sample estimate is a registered hypothesis tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
  - Given an estimator, when compared with the sample estimate, then the skill tests the paired per-period variance differences; the estimator ranks above the sample estimate only if its lower variance is significant at 5% after correction across all estimator comparisons.
- **COV-04** The ranking is published with every estimator's score, effect size and ledger outcome.
  - Given the completed comparison, when the ranking is read, then every estimator appears, failures included.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Estimator that produces a non-positive-definite matrix in a window → that period is a failure for the estimator (WFWD), recorded with the window

### Classical portfolios (`PORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Four long-only, fully invested risk-based portfolios are built each test period from the best-ranked covariance estimator: minimum variance, risk parity, hierarchical risk parity, and equal weight as the simplest benchmark. They are ranked on realised out-of-sample variance, and their after-cost results become the bar that S4 must clear.

**Requirements**
- **PORT-01** Each portfolio is long-only and fully invested, with no stock above 5% weight.
  - Given any portfolio's weights at a rebalance, when checked, then all are ≥ 0, sum to 1 within 1e-9, and none exceeds 0.05.
- **PORT-02** Minimum variance, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity are each built from the top-ranked COV estimator.
  - Given the COV ranking, when portfolios are built, then all three use the estimator ranked first.
- **PORT-03** Each risk-based portfolio's lower variance versus equal weight is a registered hypothesis tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
  - Given a portfolio, when compared with equal weight, then the skill tests paired per-period realised variances; it passes only if significant at 5% after correction across the three comparisons.
- **PORT-04** The ranking reports realised variance, after-cost return, Sharpe ratio and turnover for all four portfolios.
  - Given the completed comparison, when read, then all four portfolios appear with all four metrics and their ledger outcomes.
- **PORT-05** The minimum-variance portfolio's after-cost results are stored as the benchmark for S4.
  - Given S4 comparisons, when they run, then they use these stored per-period results on the same test periods.
- **PORT-06** Risk parity gives each stock equal risk contribution under the estimated covariance.
  - Given a risk-parity portfolio at a rebalance, when risk contributions are computed with the estimator used to build it, then each is within 1% (relative) of the average.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Weight cap makes the minimum-variance problem infeasible (fewer than 20 eligible stocks) → refused with the count named

### Candidate features (`FEAT`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** The researcher defines candidate features from daily price, volume and corporate-action data. Examples are momentum over several lookbacks, short-term reversal, trailing volatility, volume shocks and distance from highs. Each is computed point-in-time for every member on every date.

**Requirements**
- **FEAT-01** A feature value on date T uses only data known by T's close.
  - Given any feature, when its value for T is recomputed with all data after T removed, then it is unchanged.
- **FEAT-02** Features are computed for every member on every date where their lookback has enough data, and are missing elsewhere.
  - Given a stock with 30 days of history and a 63-day feature, when computed, then the value is missing, not zero or partial.
- **FEAT-03** At least 10 candidate features are defined before S3 testing starts, each with a written rationale.
  - Given the start of S3 testing, when the feature list is read, then at least 10 features are listed, each with its lookback and rationale.
- **FEAT-04** Each feature's distribution and stability are characterised with the `data-analysis` skill before testing.
  - Given a feature, when characterised, then the skill reports coverage, distribution and tails, and correlation with the other candidates across time windows.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Two candidates correlated above 0.9 across the history → both stay, flagged as near-duplicates, and both count toward the correction family

### Feature significance (`SIG`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** Each candidate feature is tested for information about future market-removed returns on the shared test periods, as a registered hypothesis, with correction across every feature tested. Features that pass are the only inputs S4 forecasters may use beyond raw price history.

**Requirements**
- **SIG-01** Each feature is tested against the next 5-day residual return from FACT.
  - Given a feature on date T, when its information is measured, then the target is the residual return from T+1 through T+5, with residuals fitted before T.
- **SIG-02** Each feature's information gain is a registered hypothesis tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill on the walk-forward test periods.
  - Given a feature, when tested, then the skill tests the mean per-period cross-sectional rank correlation with the target against zero; the feature passes only if it is significant at 5% after correction across every S3 feature tested.
- **SIG-03** The surviving features are recorded as the only engineered inputs S4 may use.
  - Given S4 training, when a feature not in the surviving list is supplied, then it is refused.
- **SIG-04** Every feature's outcome is recorded, failures included.
  - Given S3 is complete, when the ledger is filtered to S3, then every candidate in FEAT appears with its outcome.

**Edge cases & errors**
- No feature passes → recorded as the S3 outcome; S4 forecasters then use raw price history only

### Return & volatility forecasters (`FCST`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Neural sequence forecasters predict each stock's forward returns and realised volatility over 1, 5 and 21 trading days. Their inputs are kept separate: fixed attributes, values known in advance (calendar), and observed history. The inputs are price history plus S3's surviving features. Every forecaster is compared with simple baselines on the shared test periods.

**Requirements**
- **FCST-01** Forecast targets are forward log return and forward realised volatility over 1, 5 and 21 trading days, counted in trading days, not rows.
  - Given a stock with a halt day inside the horizon, when its 5-day target is built, then the horizon spans 5 exchange trading days and the target is missing if any day lacks a bar.
- **FCST-02** Training windows respect the walk-forward gap for the longest target horizon.
  - Given a forecaster trained for a test period starting on T, when its training samples are assembled, then no sample's target ends on or after T (WFWD-03).
- **FCST-03** Inputs known in advance, observed inputs and fixed attributes are kept separate, and fixed attributes are point-in-time.
  - Given a stock whose sector changed, when samples before the change are built, then they carry the earlier sector.
- **FCST-04** Normalisation statistics come only from each training window.
  - Given a test sample, when it is normalised, then the statistics used were computed on that period's training window only.
- **FCST-05** The volatility forecaster must beat trailing 63-day realised volatility and an exponentially weighted volatility baseline.
  - Given forecasts on all test periods, when compared with each baseline by the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill on per-period mean log-volatility squared error, then the forecaster passes only if better than both at 5% after correction.
- **FCST-06** The return forecaster must beat a zero-return forecast.
  - Given forecasts on all test periods, when tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill on mean per-period cross-sectional rank correlation with realised returns, then it passes only if above zero at 5% after correction; its rank correlation must also exceed the best single surviving S3 feature's.
- **FCST-07** Every training run is reproducible from its recorded snapshot, settings and seed.
  - Given a recorded run, when it is re-run on the same hardware, then test-period forecasts match within 1e-6.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Stock with less than one full lookback of history → no sample, and counted in the run record
- Unseen fixed-attribute category in a test period (a new sector) → mapped to an "unknown" category, never an error or dropped stock

### Forecast-driven portfolios (`FPORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Portfolios built from S4 forecasts are run through BTEST on the shared test periods. Volatility forecasts feed a minimum-variance construction, and return forecasts feed a risk-controlled construction. Each must beat the stored S2 minimum-variance benchmark after costs.

**Requirements**
- **FPORT-01** A volatility-forecast portfolio is judged on realised variance against the S2 minimum-variance portfolio.
  - Given its per-period results, when compared by the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill with PORT-05's stored results, then it passes only if its realised variance is lower at 5% after correction across all FPORT and AGENT comparisons.
- **FPORT-02** A return-forecast portfolio is judged on after-cost Sharpe ratio against the S2 minimum-variance portfolio.
  - Given its per-period after-cost results, when compared by the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill with PORT-05's stored results, then it passes only if its Sharpe ratio is higher at 5% after correction across all FPORT and AGENT comparisons.
- **FPORT-03** Forecast-driven portfolios meet the same constraints as classical ones.
  - Given any FPORT weights, when checked, then they satisfy PORT-01.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Forecaster that failed FCST-05 or FCST-06 → its portfolio may still be run, but the result is labelled as built on a failed forecaster in the ledger

### Trading agents (`AGENT`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Reinforcement-learning agents learn to set portfolio weights by trial and error in a simulated market built from training-window data, paying BTEST's costs. Each agent line has a configuration budget registered in advance. If no configuration beats minimum variance after costs, the line stops.

**Requirements**
- **AGENT-01** Agents train only in simulations built from training-window data.
  - Given an agent trained for a test period starting on T, when its training episodes are inspected, then none contains data dated on or after the training window's end.
- **AGENT-02** Each agent line registers a budget of at most 20 configurations before training starts.
  - Given an agent line with 20 configurations tried, when a 21st is attempted, then it is refused.
- **AGENT-03** An agent is judged on after-cost Sharpe ratio against the S2 minimum-variance portfolio.
  - Given its per-period after-cost results, when compared by the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill with PORT-05's stored results, then it passes only if its Sharpe ratio is higher at 5% after correction across all FPORT and AGENT comparisons, including every configuration tried.
- **AGENT-04** An agent line whose budget is used up with no pass stops, and is recorded as failed.
  - Given a line with every configuration failed, when another training run is requested for it, then it is refused and the ledger shows the line as stopped.
- **AGENT-05** Agent actions are valid portfolios.
  - Given any agent action, when applied, then the weights satisfy PORT-01.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Training diverges (non-finite losses) → the configuration is recorded as failed and still counts against the budget

### Final verdict (`VERD`)
**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** At the end of S4, one report answers the project's question: does any signal beat the classical portfolios after costs? It lists every forecaster, forecast-driven portfolio and agent against the benchmark, with its ledger outcome and lockbox result, and states the conclusion plainly, "none does" included.

**Requirements**
- **VERD-01** The verdict lists every S4 method with its walk-forward outcome, lockbox outcome and effect size versus the benchmark.
  - Given S4 is complete, when the verdict is produced, then every FPORT and AGENT ledger entry appears, failures included.
- **VERD-02** A method is declared to beat the classical portfolios only if it passed walk-forward and was not significantly worse on the lockbox.
  - Given a method that passed walk-forward but whose lockbox Sharpe ratio is lower than the benchmark's at 5% significance, when the verdict is written, then the method is not declared a winner.
- **VERD-03** The verdict states one conclusion: which methods beat the benchmark, or that none does.
  - Given the verdict, when read, then its first line names the winning methods or says "none".

**Edge cases & errors**
- S4 stopped early (all agent lines stopped, all forecasters failed) → the verdict is still produced, with "none"

## Data requirements

  - Given the dataset, when the earliest date with complete membership is read, then it is at least 15 years before the latest date.
- **DATA-02** Daily open, high, low, close and volume for every stock ever a member, from 252 trading days before its first membership through its last trading day, delisted stocks included.

  - Given a stock known to have split, when its corporate actions are read, then the split appears with its ratio and effective date.
- **DATA-04** Every record carries the date it became known, as well as the date it refers to.
  - Given any stored record, when read, then both dates are present.
- **DATA-05** The US equity exchange trading calendar for the covered history.
