Nothing is written yet. These are the workflows I think you'll run during S1. Confirm them first, and then I'll write `docs/packages/rllab/pipelines.md`.

**Candidate pipelines (all in stage S1)**

1. **Train a run.** Trigger: `rllab train <settings.toml> --seeds 0-4`. It loads the settings, builds the environment and the agent (table-based, DQN, or an ablation), then trains with learning-curve points and progress lines. It saves the checkpoint and recorded games. The same pipeline trains the corridor, which is how `CORE-02`–`CORE-05` get tested. Covers SNAKE, AGENT, ABL-01–03, RUN, CORE and QR-01–03.
2. **Train the reference.** Trigger: `rllab train <reference.toml>`, sent here when the settings say `agent = "reference"`. Stable-Baselines3 DQN trains through the adapter, using the same settings mapping and evaluation-based curve as pipeline 1. Covers REF-01.
3. **Evaluate.** Trigger: `rllab eval <run>…`, or a baseline name such as `scripted`. It reloads checkpoints, plays 200 held-out-seed games per seed with no exploration, and prints the mean and spread for each seed and overall. Covers EVAL-01, DATA-01, BASE-03, RUN-05 and QR-04.
4. **Report.** Trigger: `rllab report <report.toml>`. It evaluates every listed player and runs each claim through `compare` (the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill). It writes `report.md`, the learning-curve charts with baseline lines, and the ablation charts. Covers EVAL-02–06, REF-02, REF-03 and ABL-04.
5. **Replay.** Trigger: `rllab replay <run> [--game N | --new --seed S]`. It opens a recorded game, or records a new one from the checkpoint, in the interactive viewer. Covers REPLAY-01–03.

**My recommendation: all five.**

**One choice I'm making, so you can override it:** the report re-evaluates players itself rather than reading saved scores. The architecture has nowhere to store score sets, so saving them would mean reopening the approved contract. An evaluation of 1,000 games is capped at 5 minutes, and I expect it to take about 10–20 seconds per player, so re-running it is cheap. `rllab eval` is then just a quick standalone check.
