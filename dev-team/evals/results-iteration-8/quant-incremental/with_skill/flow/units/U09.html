26	    """

34	                       price_col: str = "adj_close") -> pd.Series:
35	    """Realised volatility of daily log returns over (t, t+horizon], aligned to date t."""
36	    p = prices.sort_values(["ticker", "date"]).copy()
37	    p["log_ret"] = log_returns(p, price_col)

- **Purpose:** Builds forward-looking training labels (forward log return and forward realised volatility over several horizons, per ticker) for a return/volatility forecasting model.
- **Key parts:** `log_returns(prices, price_col="adj_close") -> Series`, `forward_return(prices, horizon, price_col) -> Series`, `forward_volatility(prices, horizon, annualise=True, price_col) -> Series`, `make_targets(prices, horizons=(1,5,21), price_col) -> DataFrame`, `demean_cross_section(targets, cols)`, `drop_incomplete(targets)`, constant `TRADING_DAYS = 252`.
- **In → Out:** A long DataFrame of daily closes with one row per (ticker, date) and columns `ticker`, `date`, `adj_close` → a DataFrame with `ticker`, `date` and one `fwd_ret_{h}d` and one `fwd_vol_{h}d` column per horizon h. Volatility is annualised by default.
- **Use for:** Building the label columns and planning the label-alignment and leakage tests for a sequence model (such as a TFT) that forecasts returns or volatility. It could also help as a cross-check on next-month (21-day) forward returns.
- **Caveats:** Targets are future data by design: a label at t is only known at t+h, so train/test splits need an embargo of at least max(h) rows. Horizons count rows, not trading days, so a gap in a ticker's dates silently stretches the window. `drop_incomplete` silently drops each ticker's last max(h) rows, which loses the returns of delisted names (survivorship bias). `demean_cross_section` takes the mean over whatever tickers are in the frame, not point-in-time members, and is crude, not a factor adjustment. There are no checks for prices at or below zero, duplicate (ticker, date) rows or missing columns. No instructions addressed to an AI were found.
