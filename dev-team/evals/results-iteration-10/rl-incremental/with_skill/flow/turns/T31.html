# Idea: Fish tank

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a second environment, a circular tank with a bouncing ball, so that the learner can write policy-gradient methods themselves, up to PPO, and watch several short-sighted fish learn to stay alive with continuous movement.

## The problem
Snake teaches value learning: discrete moves, one agent, a whole-board view. It teaches nothing about learning a policy directly, about continuous movement, or about several agents that each see only part of the world. Those are the next ideas to understand, and each one needs a world built to show it.

## Who it's for
The brief's one learner, once Snake is done, writing a second family of algorithms on a world where every result can be watched.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 1 (write the algorithms yourself, one piece at a time), Goal 2 (random and scripted baselines), Goal 3 (explained results), Goal 4 (replays), Goal 5 (the shared core makes the second environment cheaper)
- **Touches:** RUN-08 (the second environment becomes real); REPLAY-01–REPLAY-07, especially REPLAY-03 (3 move scores don't exist for continuous movement); EVAL-01–EVAL-07 (scores become time survived, comparisons reused); DATA-01 and QR-01 (worded for Snake); the Snake spec's out-of-scope line "a general framework beyond what Snake needs"
- **Brief changes:** None
- **Stage:** New S2 (roadmap stage on acceptance)

## Recommended direction
**The tank.** A circular tank holds one ball that moves at constant speed and bounces off the tank wall. The fish move continuously, choosing how to swim at every step. The ball touching a fish removes it; walls and other fish are harmless. A tank game ends when every fish is gone or a fixed time cap is reached. A fish's score is how long it survived, and a perfect fish's score is the cap.

**What a fish sees.** Only things within a fixed radius: the ball's position and velocity relative to the fish, the distance to the tank wall, and the nearest other fish. Beyond the radius it senses nothing. These are compact hand-made facts, never pixels.

**One brain.** Every fish runs the same learner on its own view, and all their experience trains it.

**The ladder (direction A).** On a single fish, four rungs, each adding one piece to the one before: REINFORCE → + a learned baseline → actor-critic → PPO's clipped update. Then PPO with 5 fish (one fish vs several). Then 5-fish PPO with a wide view vs the short view, which shows what limited sight costs. That makes 6 learner setups × 5 seeds = 30 runs, the same scale as Snake, each with its before-and-after comparison.

**Baselines.** A random swimmer, and a scripted bot that swims straight away from the ball whenever it sees it.

**Done.** The 5-fish, short-view PPO fish beat the random swimmer and beat or tie the scripted bot. An explained tie counts, as for Snake.

The learner wants the method to suit policy gradients, PPO and continuous control, and wants the Snake shared core reused: environment interface, training loop, logging and replay viewer.

## Data
- No outside data: fish create their own experience in the tank, from seeds.
- Evaluated on fixed held-out test tanks that are never trained on, scored as mean time survived per fish.
- Each comparison is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill across 5 training seeds, as for Snake.

## Alternatives considered
- Short ladder, REINFORCE → PPO only — PPO's gain would blend three ideas into one.
- Every rung at both 1 and 5 fish — about 50% more training for a side question.
- Shared brain vs a separate learner per fish — left out for now.
- Shaped survival reward, a ball that speeds up, a ball that learns to hunt — left out.

## What success looks like
- The PPO fish beat random and beat or tie the scripted bot, with every result explained.
- Each ladder rung, the one-to-five-fish step and the view step have a measured before-and-after comparison.
- Any trained school of fish can be watched as a replay.
- The Snake core took the second environment with changes, not a rewrite.

## What we're betting on
- A small 2D tank with 5 fish simulates fast enough that a PPO run fits in an hour on the laptop.
- Lower rungs may lose to the scripted bot. Only PPO gates done.
- Tank games replay exactly from a seed on the same laptop.
- The core built through Snake bends to several agents and continuous moves without a rewrite.

## Not doing
- Separate learners per fish — one shared brain keeps the ladder about the algorithm.
- Shaped rewards for the tank — survival time is the only reward.
- A ball that speeds up, more balls, or a learning hunter — each adds a different lesson.
- Library policy-gradient algorithms — written in the project, as for Snake.

## Open questions
- Tank size, ball speed, fish top speed, view radius, the wide view's radius, and the time cap.
- What a fish's move is exactly: swim direction and speed, or turn and thrust.
- What the replay shows instead of 3 move scores: for example, the fish's chosen direction and how sure it was.
- How often fish choose a move: the step length in tank time.
- Whether a fish removed early still counts in the game's score until the end.
- Whether the one-hour limit and test-game count stay as for Snake (assumed yes).
