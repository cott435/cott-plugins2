The constant `TRADING_DAYS = 252`.
- **In → Out:** In: a long DataFrame with `ticker`, `date` and `adj_close`, one row per (ticker, date). Out: a DataFrame with `ticker`, `date` and float columns `fwd_ret_{h}d` and `fwd_vol_{h}d` for each horizon h. Rows come back sorted by ticker then date.
- **Use for:** Building labels for the NN return and volatility forecasters and their baselines. Also gives the residual-return target (`demean_cross_section`) for testing features, and can feed golden-case tests that check how the forward windows line up (the volatility window is t+1..t+h).
- **Caveats:**
  - These columns look into the future by design. Never use them as features. Train/test splits need a purge or embargo of at least max(h) days.
  - Horizons count rows, not calendar days, so a missing date stretches the window without any warning.
  - `drop_incomplete` drops a row if any horizon is NaN. That costs the 1-day target the last 21 rows of every ticker. Delisted names also lose their final returns, which adds survivorship bias.
  - `demean_cross_section` subtracts a crude equal-weight mean over whichever tickers have that date. The result depends on whether it runs before or after `drop_incomplete`, and it is not a PCA market removal.
  - Volatility is RMS (root mean square) with no mean subtracted, and annualised with a hard-coded 252.
  - There is no input validation: missing columns, or prices at or below zero (log gives -inf/NaN), are not handled.
  - Only depends on numpy and pandas. No instructions aimed at an AI were found in the text.
- **Stage guess:** S4, because it makes the targets for the sequence-model return/vol forecasts. It is also partly useful for S3's residual-return targets.
