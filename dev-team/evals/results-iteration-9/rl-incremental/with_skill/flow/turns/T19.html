# Idea: Fish Tank — Policy Gradients

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a second environment, a circular tank where fish learn to swim away from a bouncing ball, so that I can learn policy-gradient methods for continuous control (REINFORCE, then PPO) by building them myself.

## The problem
Snake teaches value learning, which picks the best of a few discrete moves. It can't easily steer with a continuous direction and speed. To understand policy gradients I need a game where actions are continuous, where the gap between plain REINFORCE and PPO is visible, and where I write both algorithms myself.

## Who it's for
Me, the learner, once Snake is done. I'll know value learning, and I'll be ready for the second named concept of the lab.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 1 (one named concept: policy gradients for continuous control), Goal 2 (hand-written PPO against a library PPO), Goal 3 (beats random and scripted fish), Goal 4 (replay of the school), Goal 5 (the shared core, deliberately extended)
- **Touches:**
  - **CORE-01 and CORE-02:** the environment and agent interfaces must carry continuous actions and several agents per step.
  - **SNAKE and the corridor:** both are updated to the revised interface without changing their behaviour.
  - **EVAL, RUN, REPLAY, REF:** reused with survival time as the score.
  - **The spec's out-of-scope line "multi-agent or competitive games":** narrowed to competitive or cooperative multi-agent learning. Independent fish sharing one policy are allowed.
- **Brief changes:** none
- **Stage:** new S2 (roadmap stage on acceptance)

## Recommended direction
**The tank.** A circular tank holds one ball, which moves at a constant speed and reflects off the wall. Each fish chooses a continuous swim direction and speed, up to a cap, every step. A fish touching the ball is out. Each step a fish survives earns a reward, so the score is survival time. Everything is seeded and reproducible, as in Snake.

**The ladder,** each rung adding one idea:
1. **One fish, REINFORCE.** The plain policy gradient, with its high variance visible in the curves.
2. **One fish, PPO.** The clipped objective and a learned value baseline. This rung is compared against a library PPO.
3. **A school of fish sharing one PPO policy.** The fish are independent copies: no competition, no cooperation, and no collisions between fish. More fish just means more experience per step.
4. **The school with limited vision.** Each fish sees only what's within a set radius (the ball, the wall, and optionally its neighbours), instead of the whole tank.

An optional rung 0 could restrict swimming to 8 fixed directions so the S1 value learner runs as a reference point, showing why continuous control calls for policy gradients. Whether it's worth building is an open question.

**The shared core is revised deliberately,** not bent. The environment interface learns to carry continuous actions and several agents per step. Snake and the corridor move to the new interface, with their golden cases still passing unchanged. Learning that a first-draft core needs one honest revision is part of the lesson.

**Tool preference:** my own PPO is built from basic tensor operations, as the S1 agents are. The library comparison uses the same library family as S1's reference agent (Stable-Baselines3's PPO), on the single-fish rung only, because the library doesn't handle several agents sharing one tank.

## Data
- All experience comes from the tank itself; no outside data.
- Survival time is measured over many games on held-out seeds, never seeds used in training, with 5 training seeds per rung, as in S1.
- **Baselines:** a random swimmer, and a scripted fish that always swims straight away from the ball at full speed.
- **Claims** ("beats random", "beats scripted", "PPO beats REINFORCE", "not worse than the library") are tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, as in S1.

## Alternatives considered
- **Competing or cooperating fish, or a learned ball:** multi-agent learning dynamics would muddy the policy-gradient lesson and reverse the scope decision.
- **Starting with the full school:** debugging PPO with many fish at once hides whether a bug is in PPO or in the school setup.
- **Library comparison on the school:** the library doesn't support several agents in one tank, so the comparison would be unfair or bent.

## What success looks like
- Each rung's survival-time curve rises above both the random and scripted fish, measured over many games.
- PPO clearly beats REINFORCE on the single-fish rung.
- My PPO reaches at least 90% of the library PPO's mean survival time on the single-fish rung.
- A replay shows the school scattering away from the ball.
- Snake and the corridor still pass their golden cases on the revised core.

## What we're betting on
- A small policy network learns a sensible escape strategy within an hour of laptop CPU training per rung.
- Sharing one policy across independent fish speeds up learning rather than confusing it.
- The core revision stays small: continuous actions and several agents per step, without rewriting training, evaluation or replay.

## Not doing
- Competitive or cooperative multi-agent learning — a separate lesson.
- A learned or adaptive ball — that makes it adversarial.
- Fish colliding with or blocking each other — physics complexity that isn't the lesson.
- Library comparison on the school rungs — the library can't run them fairly.
- Learning from pixels — the fish receive positions and distances directly.

## Open questions
- Is the optional 8-direction rung worth building, or does REINFORCE on one fish already make the point?
- With limited vision, do fish see their neighbours, or only the ball and the wall?
- What tank size, ball speed, fish speed cap, vision radius and school size? These set how hard the game is, and spec-writing should propose numbers.
- Does survival time stop at a cap (for example 1,000 steps), so a perfect fish's game still ends?
- Does a school's game end when the last fish is caught, or at the step cap, and is its score the mean survival time per fish?

- **Competing or cooperating fish, or a learned ball:** a different lesson, and it reverses a scope decision.
- **Starting with the full school:** bugs in PPO would hide behind the school setup.
- **Library comparison on the school:** the library can't run several agents in one tank fairly.

- Every rung beats the random and scripted fish on survival time.

- A replay shows the school scattering from the ball.

- A small policy network learns to escape within an hour of laptop CPU training per rung.
- One shared policy across independent fish speeds learning up rather than confusing it.
- The core revision stays small, with training, evaluation and replay unchanged.

## Not doing
- Competitive or cooperative multi-agent learning — a separate lesson.
- A learned or adaptive ball — adversarial.
- Fish colliding with each other — physics that isn't the lesson.
- Library comparison on the school — not supported fairly.
- Learning from pixels — the fish get positions and distances directly.

## Open questions
- Is the optional 8-direction rung worth building?
- With limited vision, do fish see their neighbours, or only the ball and the wall?
- Tank size, ball speed, fish speed cap, vision radius, school size: spec-writing proposes numbers.
- Is there a survival cap (for example 1,000 steps) so a perfect fish's game ends?
- Does a school's game end at the last catch or at the cap, and is its score mean survival per fish?
