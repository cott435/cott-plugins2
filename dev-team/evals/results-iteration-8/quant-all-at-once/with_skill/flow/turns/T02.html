# Quant Research Platform

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17

## The problem
It's easy to fool yourself in stock research. Data missing failed companies, test periods peeked at during tuning, and "edges" that are really chance all make results look better than they are. I want a personal research setup that keeps me honest, from the data up to trading agents.

## Who it's for
Me: one researcher testing ideas on large US stocks.

## Goals
1. Trust the data. Daily prices for large US stocks as known each day, including companies that later left the index or stopped trading.
2. Believe only what survives chance. Every edge passes a null-hypothesis test, corrected for how many things were tried, before anything builds on it.
3. Compare everything on one scoreboard. Every portfolio is scored on the same held-out periods and trading costs: Sharpe ratio after costs, with realised variance alongside.
4. Earn complexity. A method replaces a simpler one only by beating it out of sample; a clear "it didn't" is a result.
5. See the data and the runs. Explore data and results visually, and watch download and validation runs live.

## Constraints
- One person, local laptop; a rented GPU now and then.
- Data: my existing Sharadar subscription; anything else free or under $50 a month.
- Universe: S&P 500 members with point-in-time membership, at least 15 years of history, covering 2008-09.
- Paper trading only, on daily data.
- A TypeScript and React app over a Python backend, with typed contracts.

## What success looks like
- A validated dataset from 2008 to today, cross-checked against a second source, every mismatch explained.
- Covariance estimators and classical portfolios (minimum-variance, risk parity, hierarchical risk parity) ranked out of sample.
- Every feature claim recorded with its corrected p-value, failures too.
- A verdict on held-out years: do forecast-driven portfolios and trading agents beat the classical ones after costs? Either answer is fine.

## What we're betting on
- Sharadar includes delisted index members with final prices and corporate actions. If not, Goal 1 needs rethinking.
- About 500 stocks over 15+ years is enough to tell real effects from noise.
- A cheap second source overlaps enough to catch errors in the main one.

## Not doing
- Real money or live broker trading — paper only.
- Small caps, non-US stocks, other assets or intraday data — cost and noise.
- Return prediction before variance is done right — risk first.
- Touching held-out years before a final verdict — that's how results become fiction.
- Hosting, logins or other users — personal tool.

## Open questions
- Which second source to cross-check against? (Architecture.)
- How many final years to hold out? (Spec.)
