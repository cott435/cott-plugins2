## Index template: `docs/references.md`

````markdown

- **Caveats:** <what not to reuse as is, or "none found">

## Retired
````

- **Hash** stays `—` until `dt references --stamp` writes the first 8 hex digits of the file's sha256. A later mismatch puts the file under `references_changed`.
- **Kind** is `code` or `doc`.
- **Milestones** holds stage IDs `S<n>` (or `M<n>` for one slice of a cut stage; comma-separated), `all`, or `—`.
- **Retired** holds one struck line per removed file: `- ~~RF-04: Old loader~~ Retired 2026-12-01: file removed; superseded by RF-05`. Omit the heading until the first file is retired.

**RF-01: PCA market-mode removal.** File `references/market_decomposition.py` · Hash b3993e95 · code · Milestones: not assigned yet
- **Purpose:** removes the top-k principal components (the market mode) from daily returns, keeps the stock-specific residuals, and measures how much cross-correlation is left.
- **Key parts:** `demean_returns`, `standardise`, `pca_factors(returns, k)`, `residualize`, `remove_market`, `residual_correlation_check`
- **Caveats:**
  - **Look-ahead:** it fits on the full panel.
  - **Dropped columns:** stocks with fewer than `min_obs` observations disappear without a warning.
  - **Gaps filled with 0 before demeaning:** non-member days become fake zero returns, which biases the means, variances and correlations.
  - **No rule for k:** k is hard-coded, with no Marchenko–Pastur cutoff.
  - **Sign fix:** it zeroes any eigenvector whose loadings sum to 0.
  - **Hidden NaNs:** `nanmean` hides NaNs from zero-variance stocks.
  - **Scale mismatch:** the factors are on the correlation scale but the betas are on the raw-return scale.

## Stage 2 idea: understanding it

**How might we** find the variance-minimising portfolio construction for point-in-time S&P 500 members by comparing covariance estimators and allocators out of sample, before any return forecast exists?

This stage builds on S1's `UNIV-01/02` (members as of a date) and `RET-01/04/07` (total returns, as-of correctness, gaps). Those gaps are exactly where RF-01's zero-fill goes wrong.

**One thing to push back on now.** The market factor should be removed for structure (to see the correlation clusters and to feed HRP's clustering), not from the covariance that min-variance optimises. Most of a long-only portfolio's variance is market variance. If you drop it before optimising, the estimate looks great in-sample and gets realised variance wrong out of sample. I'd keep two outputs: a detoned correlation for clustering, and a full covariance for weights.

**Questions** (my default in brackets):
1. **Estimation setup.** Should each rebalance use a trailing window over that day's members? [504 trading days, monthly rebalance, walk-forward from 2000. With N≈500 and T=504, q = N/T ≈ 1. That's where denoising should matter most, and the sample covariance is close to singular.]
2. **How many factors to remove.** Fixed k, or the eigenvalues above the Marchenko–Pastur edge λ₊? [Use the MP edge, and report fixed k=1 next to it. RF-01's residual-correlation check is the sanity test.]
3. **Portfolio rules.** Long-only fully invested, or should min-variance be allowed to short? [Long-only for all three, so they're comparable, with turnover reported but no cost model.]
4. **What "right" means.** [A table of realised out-of-sample volatility for every estimator × allocator pair, with 1/N and sample-covariance min-variance as baselines. Plus a test of whether the best pair beats the baseline by more than chance (null-hypothesis-testing, with a multiple-comparison correction across the 9 pairs).]
5. **Where it lives.** [A new Python package with its own CLI that reads from `eqdata` and writes a report you can open in the existing app. The brief's Not Doing currently rules out research views in the app, so the alternative is a report file outside it.]

**Variations considered:**
- **Detoning only for HRP clustering:** keeps the full covariance for min-variance and risk parity.
- **A factor-model covariance (k PCs + diagonal residuals) as a fourth estimator:** a cheap, standard competitor to denoising.
- **Ledoit–Wolf with two shrinkage targets (identity vs constant correlation):** the target choice often matters more than shrinkage vs denoising.
- **Denoise, then shrink:** the two methods combine.
- **Estimator stability first:** compare estimators before any portfolio, using eigenvector stability and condition number across windows.
- **The simplest useful version:** min-variance only, three estimators, one window length. The allocators come after that.
- **Window sensitivity:** run 252, 504 and 1008 days, since q drives everything here.
