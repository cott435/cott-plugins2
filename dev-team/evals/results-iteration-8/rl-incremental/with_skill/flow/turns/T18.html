# Idea: Fish tank

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a second environment, a round tank where fish learn to dodge a bouncing ball, so that I can learn policy-gradient methods for continuous movement, one idea at a time.

## The problem
Snake teaches value learning: a table, then a network that scores each of three moves. It never shows a learner that picks its actions directly, actions that are continuous rather than a short list, or several agents at once. Those are the next ideas in RL I want to understand. Without an environment built for them, I'd be back to tutorials with finished code.

## Who it's for
Me, after Snake: comfortable with value learning and my own training loop, new to policy gradients and continuous control.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 1 (one concept: learning a policy directly), Goal 2 (a rung per new idea), Goal 3 (random and scripted fish as bars), Goal 4 (replays of the tank), Goal 5 (the first real test of reusing the shared core)
- **Touches:** the shared core's agent and environment interfaces, which assume one agent, one integer move and three move values (CORE-01, the network learner's NET requirements); the replay viewer and saved replays, which assume grid frames and three move values (WATCH-01–WATCH-07); the bar tests (EVAL-05–EVAL-07), reused as they are
- **Brief changes:** Constraints: "a small 2D grid game" becomes "a small 2D game, on a grid or continuous"
- **Stage:** New S2. Its spec starts only after M1's exit, once the shared core is proven on Snake

## Recommended direction
A round tank holds one ball that bounces off the wall and moves about 1.5 times faster than a fish, so fleeing in a straight line isn't enough. A fish touched by the ball is removed. Every fish earns +1 for each step it stays alive. An episode ends when every fish is gone, or at a time limit. Fish don't collide with each other.

The environment is climbed as a ladder, one new idea per rung:

| Rung | Fish | Sees | Moves | Learner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | one | everything | 8 compass directions | REINFORCE |
| T2 | one | everything | continuous steering | REINFORCE |
| T3 | one | everything | continuous | PPO |
| T4 | several, one shared brain | only what's nearby | continuous | PPO |

T1 isolates learning a policy directly. T2 adds continuous actions. T3 shows why PPO is steadier than REINFORCE. T4 adds a crowd and limited vision. Several fish share one brain ("one brain, many bodies"), so the learner acts on a batch of fish rather than becoming many learners.

REINFORCE and PPO are written by hand, as Snake's learners are. PyTorch supplies only the network's arithmetic and gradients. The replay shows each fish's chosen direction and how sure it is, plus the learner's estimate of how well things are going, in place of Snake's three move values.

## Alternatives considered
- Straight to PPO with continuous moves — faster to the tank, but PPO would arrive as a recipe without REINFORCE's noisy learning to explain it
- Many fish, limited vision and PPO all at once — a flat curve would say nothing about which of four new pieces is wrong
- One fish against several balls — keeps the crowd out, but drops the multi-agent idea that motivated the tank
- Learning to be the ball — a natural later environment, not this one

## What success looks like
- Each rung's fish clearly beats the random fish and matches or beats the scripted fish, which swims straight away from the ball
- At least one rung beats the scripted fish by not getting cornered against the curved wall, and I can show it in a replay
- I can explain from T2 against T3's curves why PPO learns more steadily
- The tank reuses the training loop, logging, evaluation, bar tests and viewer with changes only to the shared interfaces, not copies

## What we're betting on
- A small network learns the tank within the one-hour laptop limit, given the tank's simple physics and small observations
- A ball 1.5 times faster than a fish makes the scripted fish beatable, but not trivially
- The shared core generalizes to vector actions and batches of fish without becoming a framework

## Not doing
- Separate brains per fish — one shared brain keeps T4 a single learner; separate brains are a later experiment
- Fish colliding with each other — extra physics with no new RL idea
- Several balls, moving obstacles or a growing tank — difficulty curricula stay out, as in the brief
- Changing the shared core before M1's exit — it would collide with M1's frozen interfaces

## Open questions
- How many fish T4 uses, and how far a fish can see
- The time limit per episode, and whether a removed fish's +1 stream simply stops
- What exactly the scripted fish does at the wall
- Whether "matches the scripted fish" keeps Snake's 10% margin
