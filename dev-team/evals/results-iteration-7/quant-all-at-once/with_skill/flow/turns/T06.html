# Quant Research Platform — Spec

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [brief.md](brief.md)

## Summary
A personal research platform for daily US large-company stocks, built in four stages. It starts with a point-in-time S&P 500 dataset and a browser explorer. Next comes risk structure with classical portfolios, then features tested against chance, then neural forecasters and a reinforcement-learning trading agent. Every result is judged out of sample, after costs, against simpler baselines. Why: see the [brief](brief.md).

## Users
| User | What they need to do |
|---|---|
| The researcher (sole user) | Build and refresh the dataset, explore it, run each stage's experiments, and read verdicts they can trust. |

## Scope
**In scope:** point-in-time universe, ingest and cleaning, validation, as-of loader, exploratory analysis, explorer app (S1); factor removal, covariance estimation, walk-forward evaluation, classical portfolios (S2); feature library, feature testing (S3); neural forecasting, forecast-driven portfolios, PPO trading agent, paper trading (S4).

**Out of scope:**
- Real money or any live brokerage connection: paper only.
- Intraday data, options, futures, crypto, non-US markets.
- Stocks outside the S&P 500 (as of each date), and the full US market.
- Company financials and alternative data: features come from prices and volume only.
- Starting S4 when S3 found no surviving feature.
- Hosted, multi-user or logged-in use of the app.
- Scheduled or automatic data updates: updates are started by hand.

## Common definitions
These apply to every requirement below.
- **Trading day:** a day the US stock exchanges are open.
- **Universe on day D:** the stocks that were S&P 500 members at the close of D.
- **Total return:** return including splits and reinvested dividends. **Price return:** splits only.
- **Decision timing:** a decision for day D uses data up to and including the close of D. Its trades are filled at the close of the next trading day, D+1.
- **Cost model:** 10 basis points (0.10%) of traded value per side. Every "beats" claim is also re-checked at 20 basis points (2× costs).
- **Rebalance:** monthly, on the last trading day of each month.
- **Locked holdout:** the most recent 2 years of trading days, counted back from the most recent close in the dataset. The holdout start date is fixed when the first holdout evaluation is made, and never moves after that.
- **Development period:** everything from 2005-01-03 up to the holdout start date.
- **Out of sample:** fitted only on data before the period it is scored on.

### S1 — Data + explorer

### Point-in-time universe (`UNIV`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Behavior:** For any trading day since 2005-01-03, the researcher can ask which stocks were in the S&P 500 on that day. They can also see every entry to and exit from the index, with its date. A stock that left the index keeps its history.

**Requirements**
- **UNIV-01** The system returns the S&P 500 membership as of any trading day from 2005-01-03 to the latest close.
  - Given a date D, when membership is requested, then the stocks returned are exactly those that were members at the close of D.
  - Given any trading day in the range, when membership is counted, then the count is between 495 and 510.
- **UNIV-02** Membership matches published records.
  - Given 10 dates spread across the range, when membership is compared with published index lists for those dates, then every difference is listed and explained. Unexplained differences: 0.
- **UNIV-03** Each stock has a permanent identity across ticker changes.
  - Given a stock that changed its ticker, when its history is requested under either ticker, then one continuous history is returned under one identity.
- **UNIV-04** Every entry to and exit from the index is listed with its effective date.
  - Given a date range, when changes are requested, then each entry and exit in the range is returned with stock, date and direction.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A date that is not a trading day → membership for the most recent trading day before it, and the response says which date was used.
- A date before 2005-01-03 or after the latest close → a clear "out of range" error. No empty result.
- A company with two share classes in the index → both classes are members, each with its own identity.

### Ingest & clean (`ING`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Behavior:** The researcher runs a full download once, then add-on updates by hand. Each run downloads prices, volume and corporate actions for every stock that was ever in the universe, cleans them, computes returns and stores the result locally. Runs can be stopped and resumed.

**Requirements**
- **ING-01** A full run downloads daily open, high, low, close and volume for every stock that was a member at any point since 2005-01-03, covering its membership period plus the 3 years before it entered.
  - Given an empty store, when a full run completes, then every past and present member has daily rows covering its membership period, apart from what is flagged under VAL.
- **ING-02** The system stores splits and dividends, each with its ex-date and amount or ratio.
  - Given a stock with a known split, when its corporate actions are requested, then the split appears with its ex-date and ratio.
- **ING-03** The system computes daily price returns and total returns from the stored prices and corporate actions.
  - Given a day with a 2-for-1 split and no price move, when returns are computed, then the price return and the total return are both 0 (within 1e-9).
  - Given a stock paying a dividend of 1% of the price on its ex-date, with the close otherwise unchanged, when returns are computed, then the total return is 1% higher than the price return (within 1e-6).
- **ING-04** A stock's final return before delisting is kept.
  - Given a stock that stopped trading, when its returns are requested, then the history runs up to its last trading day, and the move to the final price is included.
- **ING-05** An update run adds only the new trading days since the last stored close.
  - Given a store complete to day D, when an update runs on day D+5, then only days D+1 to D+5 are downloaded, and earlier stored rows are unchanged unless the source corrected them.
- **ING-06** Runs are resumable.
  - Given a run interrupted part-way, when it is started again, then it continues from the last completed stock, and the final store is identical to one produced by an uninterrupted run.
- **ING-07** Every run records what it did.
  - Given a completed run, when its record is opened, then it shows start and end time, stocks attempted, stocks succeeded, stocks failed with reasons, and rows added or changed.

**Edge cases & errors**
- The source fails or limits requests → retry with increasing waits, up to 5 times per stock. After that the stock is recorded as failed and the run continues.
- The source corrects past data → the change is stored, and the run record lists it (stock, date, old value, new value).
- No network → the run stops with a clear message, and the store is unchanged.

### Validation (`VAL`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Behavior:** After each ingest run, and on demand, the system checks the stored data. Failing data is kept but flagged. The loader leaves flagged data out by default, and the app lists the flags.

**Requirements**
- **VAL-01** The system flags each member-day with no price row.
  - Given a member that is missing a trading day while a member, when validation runs, then that stock-day is flagged as "missing".
- **VAL-02** The system flags non-positive prices and inconsistent bars: high below low, or close outside the high–low range.
  - Given a row with high < low, when validation runs, then it is flagged as "inconsistent bar".
- **VAL-03** The system flags large moves with no corporate action to explain them: an absolute daily price return above 40%, with no split on that day.
  - Given a +60% price return with no split, when validation runs, then it is flagged as "unexplained jump".
  - Given a −50% price return on a 2-for-1 split's ex-date, when validation runs, then it is not flagged.
- **VAL-04** The system flags stale prices: 5 or more trading days in a row with an identical close and zero volume.
  - Given 5 such days, when validation runs, then each of the 5 is flagged as "stale".
- **VAL-05** The system flags duplicate rows for the same stock and day.
  - Given two rows for one stock-day, when validation runs, then both are flagged as "duplicate".
- **VAL-06** Flagged data is stored, never deleted.
  - Given flagged rows, when the store is read, then the rows are still present, each carrying its flag types.
- **VAL-07** Each validation run produces a report.
  - Given a run, when the report is opened, then it shows counts by flag type, by stock and by year, and the overall share of member-days that are flagged.
- **VAL-08** The share of member-days that are missing or flagged is at most 0.5% across the whole dataset.
  - Given the full dataset, when the validation report is produced, then the flagged share is ≤ 0.5%. If it is higher, the report lists the stocks that contribute the most.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A trading halt with zero volume for fewer than 5 days → not flagged as stale.
- An exchange closure, such as a market-wide closure day → not counted as missing for any stock.

### As-of loader (`LOAD`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Behavior:** Research code requests data as it was known on a date. It gets back prices, returns, volume and membership, with nothing from after that date and with flagged data left out by default.

**Requirements**
- **LOAD-01** Every request carries an as-of date, and nothing dated after it is returned.
  - Given as-of date D, when prices, returns, volume, corporate actions or membership are requested, then no value dated after D is returned.
- **LOAD-02** The loader returns a panel of returns for the universe as of each day. A stock appears only on days it was a member, unless history before entry is requested.
  - Given a range, when a member-only returns panel is requested, then a stock's returns are present only on days it was a member.
  - Given a request for 3 years of history before each stock's entry, when the panel is returned, then that earlier history is included and marked as before entry.
- **LOAD-03** Flagged data is left out by default and can be included on request.
  - Given a flagged stock-day, when data is loaded with default settings, then that value is missing in the result. When flagged data is requested, it is present, with its flags.
- **LOAD-04** The loader returns either total returns or price returns, as requested.
  - Given a dividend day, when total and price returns are both requested, then they differ by the dividend yield.
- **LOAD-05** Requests that touch the locked holdout are refused outside final-evaluation mode (see EVAL-06).
  - Given the holdout is locked, when a normal request covers any holdout day, then it fails with an error naming the holdout start date.

**Edge cases & errors**
- An as-of date with no stored data yet → an error saying the latest stored close.
- A stock requested on days it was not a member, using member-only mode → missing values, not an error.

### Exploratory analysis (`EDA`)
**Serves:** Goal 4, Goal 3
**Behavior:** The researcher runs a standard analysis of the dataset and gets a report with plots, made with the `data-analysis` skill.

**Requirements**
- **EDA-01** The analysis is run with the `data-analysis` skill on the development period of the dataset. It is given daily total returns, membership and the validation flags.
  - Given the dataset, when the analysis runs, then the report covers: summary statistics of returns, distribution and tail shape, missingness by stock and year, membership turnover per year, the average pairwise correlation over time, and the eigenvalue spectrum of the correlation matrix.
- **EDA-02** The report states the share of variance explained by the top eigenvalue in each calendar year.
  - Given the report, when the yearly table is read, then each year from 2005 on has one value.
- **EDA-03** Each finding in the report names the period and the stocks it was measured on.
  - Given any figure in the report, when it is read, then its caption states its date range and universe.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A year with fewer than 200 trading days of data → shown, with a "partial year" note.

**Behavior:** A local browser app for one user. In S1 it lets the researcher look at the universe, any stock's history, the validation flags and the EDA results, and start ingest and validation runs and watch them live. S2–S4 each add views of their results.

**Requirements**

  - Given a stock, when it is chosen by ticker or name, past tickers included, then its history chart loads with splits, dividends, flags and membership periods marked.
- **APP-03** The app lists validation flags, filterable by type, stock and year.

- **APP-04** The app shows the data-coverage picture: the share of member-days present, by year and by stock.
  - Given the coverage view, when it loads, then the coverage by year is shown and the stocks with the lowest coverage are listed.
- **APP-05** The app shows the EDA report's figures and tables.
  - Given a completed EDA run, when the EDA view is opened, then its figures and tables are shown, with their captions.
- **APP-06** The researcher can start a full ingest, an update or a validation run from the app.
  - Given no run in progress, when "update" is started, then a run begins and its live progress view opens.
  - Given a run already in progress, when another is started, then it is refused with "a run is already in progress".
- **APP-07** Live progress of ingest and validation runs is pushed to the browser as it happens, without reloading. Progress shows the run type, phase, stocks done out of total, the current stock, failures so far and the estimated time remaining.
  - Given a running ingest, when progress changes, then the view updates within 2 seconds of the change.

  - Given a run in progress, when the page is reloaded, then progress resumes showing the current state within 2 seconds.
  - Given a run that has finished, when the view is open, then it shows the run record (ING-07) or the validation report (VAL-07).
- **APP-09** (S2) The app shows the eigenvalue spectrum next to the random-matrix noise band, per window, and the out-of-sample comparisons of covariance estimators and portfolios.
  - Given a completed S2 evaluation, when its view is opened, then the spectrum, the realized-variance comparison table and the cumulative return curves are shown.

  - Given a completed S4 evaluation, when its view is opened, then the loss comparison against baselines and the after-cost Sharpe and variance comparison against S2 portfolios are shown.
  - Given paper trading is running, when its view is opened, then each day's orders, fills and running results are listed.

**Edge cases & errors**

### S2 — Risk structure + classical portfolios

### Factor removal (`FACT`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Behavior:** For each walk-forward window, the system finds the market factor and other dominant factors from past returns only, and removes them to give residual returns. The number of factors kept is chosen by how far their eigenvalues stand above random-matrix noise.

**Requirements**
- **FACT-01** Factors are fitted only on returns inside the estimation window that ends at the decision date.
  - Given a window ending at D, when factors are fitted, then changing any return after D leaves the factors, loadings and residuals for days up to D unchanged.
- **FACT-02** The number of factors kept equals the number of correlation eigenvalues above the Marchenko–Pastur upper edge for that window's shape, unless a fixed number is requested.
  - Given a window, when factors are chosen, then the count and the noise edge are recorded with the window.
- **FACT-03** The system returns residual returns: each stock's return minus its exposure to the kept factors.
  - Given residual returns for a window, when their correlation matrix is computed, then the mean absolute off-diagonal correlation is lower than that of the raw returns.
- **FACT-04** The method recovers a planted structure.
  - Given simulated returns with 1 planted market factor plus 2 planted sector factors, 500 stocks and 756 days, when factors are fitted, then 3 factors are kept, and each planted loading vector correlates ≥ 0.95 with a recovered one.
- **FACT-05** Out-of-sample residuals are produced for later stages. Exposures fitted on a window are applied to the returns of the following holding period.
  - Given a window ending at D, when residuals for D+1 to the next rebalance are requested, then they use exposures fitted only on data up to D.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A stock with less than 90% of returns present in the window → left out of that window's fit, and the count of stocks left out is recorded.
- Missing returns are never filled in as 0 for fitting. The handling used is recorded with the window.

### Covariance estimation (`COV`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Behavior:** For each window, the system estimates the covariance of stock returns three ways: the plain sample estimate, a shrinkage estimate and a random-matrix denoised estimate. The `data-analysis` skill compares them on real data.

**Requirements**
- **COV-01** The system produces a sample covariance estimate per window.
  - Given a window with T days and N stocks, when the estimate is made, then it is symmetric and matches a direct calculation within 1e-10.
- **COV-02** The system produces a shrinkage estimate that pulls the sample covariance toward a structured target, with the shrinkage amount estimated from the data.
  - Given a window, when the shrinkage estimate is made, then the shrinkage amount (between 0 and 1) is recorded, and the estimate is positive definite.
- **COV-03** The system produces a denoised estimate. Correlation eigenvalues inside the Marchenko–Pastur noise band are replaced by their average, keeping the trace. The result is then turned back into a covariance.
  - Given a window, when the denoised estimate is made, then eigenvalues above the edge are unchanged, the trace of the correlation matrix equals N within 1e-8, and the estimate is positive definite.
  - Given simulated pure-noise returns, when denoised, then the denoised correlation matrix is closer to the identity (smaller Frobenius distance) than the sample one.
- **COV-04** The estimation window length defaults to 756 trading days, and 504 and 1260 can also be chosen.
  - Given a window length setting, when estimates are made, then each window uses exactly that many trading days.
- **COV-05** The estimators are compared on real data with the `data-analysis` skill. It is given each estimator's out-of-sample minimum-variance portfolio variance per rebalance, and its out-of-sample covariance forecast error per window, over the development period.
  - Given a completed S2 walk-forward run, when the comparison runs, then the report shows each estimator's realized variance and forecast error, with the spread over time and its stability across sub-periods (2005–2009, 2010–2014, 2015–2019, 2020 to the holdout start).
- **COV-06** Any claim that one estimator beats another is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill. It is given the paired per-rebalance realized-variance differences over the development period. The claim passes only if the null of no difference is rejected at 5%, after correction for all estimator pairs compared.
  - Given the claim "denoised beats sample", when tested, then the report states the test, the p-value, the corrected verdict and the effect size.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A window with T ≤ N → the sample estimate is reported as singular, and is never inverted silently. Portfolios that need an inverse use only the other estimators, and the report says so.

### Walk-forward evaluation (`EVAL`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Behavior:** One shared engine runs every strategy from every stage the same way. At each rebalance it fits on past data only, decides target weights, trades at the next close under the cost model, holds to the next rebalance and records the results. Every evaluation run is logged, including runs that failed.

**Requirements**
- **EVAL-01** At each rebalance date D, a strategy sees only data available as of D (through the loader, LOAD-01).
  - Given a strategy that tries to read data after D, when the run reaches D, then the read is refused and the run stops with an error.
- **EVAL-02** Trades are filled at the close of D+1, and costs are charged at 10 bps per side on traded value.
  - Given a move from 100% cash to 100% in one stock, when the trade is filled, then the cost charged is 0.10% of the traded value.
- **EVAL-03** Every result is reported both at the base cost and at 2× cost.
  - Given a completed run, when results are shown, then both cost levels are shown side by side.
- **EVAL-04** A held stock that leaves the index stays held until the next rebalance. A held stock that stops trading takes its final return, and the proceeds move to cash.
  - Given a holding delisted mid-month, when the month is evaluated, then its final return is included, and its weight is cash until the next rebalance.
- **EVAL-05** Training data for any fitted model is purged. No training sample's target window overlaps the period being scored, and an embargo of at least the longest target horizon separates them.
  - Given 21-day targets and a scoring period starting at day S, when training samples are chosen, then none has a target window reaching day S or later, and none starts within 21 trading days before S.
- **EVAL-06** The locked holdout is used only in final-evaluation mode. Each strategy can be run on it once per version, and every holdout run is recorded.
  - Given a strategy version already evaluated on the holdout, when a second holdout run is asked for, then it is refused and the first result is shown.
- **EVAL-07** Results include: annualized return, volatility, realized variance, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown and turnover, after costs, per run and per sub-period.
  - Given a completed run, when results are shown, then each of these metrics is shown at both cost levels.
- **EVAL-08** Every evaluation run is logged with the strategy, its settings, the data range, the code version and the result. Failed and discarded runs are logged too.
  - Given any run, when the log is opened, then the run appears, and the log entry cannot be edited afterwards.
- **EVAL-09** Runs are reproducible.
  - Given the same data, settings, code version and random seed, when a run is repeated, then all reported metrics are identical.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A rebalance date with fewer than 100 eligible stocks → the rebalance is skipped, the earlier weights are held, and this is recorded.
- A strategy returns weights that don't sum to 1, or are negative where long-only is required → the run stops with an error naming the date and the problem.

### Classical portfolios (`PORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Behavior:** Minimum-variance, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity portfolios are built on each covariance estimate. They are run through the walk-forward engine and compared out of sample on realized variance, with equal weight as the simplest baseline.

**Requirements**
- **PORT-01** All classical portfolios are long-only, fully invested and unlevered.
  - Given any portfolio's weights, when checked, then every weight is ≥ 0 and the weights sum to 1 within 1e-8.
- **PORT-02** The minimum-variance portfolio minimizes the estimated variance under PORT-01.
  - Given a 3-stock covariance with a known solution, when solved, then the weights match it within 1e-6.
- **PORT-03** The risk parity portfolio gives each stock an equal share of the estimated portfolio variance.
  - Given any estimate, when solved, then every stock's share of risk is within 1% (relative) of 1/N.
- **PORT-04** The hierarchical risk parity portfolio clusters stocks by correlation, then splits weight top-down by cluster variance.
  - Given a covariance made of 2 uncorrelated blocks with equal block variance, when solved, then each block receives 50% of the weight within 1e-6.
- **PORT-05** An equal-weight portfolio of the universe is run as the baseline.
  - Given a rebalance date, when equal weight is built, then each eligible stock gets 1/N.
- **PORT-06** Each portfolio method is run on each covariance estimator (COV-01 to COV-03), giving one walk-forward result per pair.
  - Given a completed S2 run, when results are listed, then there are 3 methods × 3 estimators + equal weight = 10 results.
- **PORT-07** Portfolios are compared on out-of-sample realized variance, with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill. It is given paired per-rebalance realized-variance differences between each portfolio and equal weight, and between portfolio methods, over the development period. A "lower variance" claim passes only if the null is rejected at 5% after correction for all comparisons made, at both cost levels.
  - Given the claim "denoised minimum-variance has lower realized variance than equal weight", when tested, then the report states the p-value, the corrected verdict and the variance ratio.
- **PORT-08** The S2 portfolios' holdout results (EVAL-06) are recorded once. They are the benchmarks for S4.
  - Given the S2 final evaluation, when run, then each portfolio's holdout metrics are stored, and S4 comparisons read these stored values.

**Edge cases & errors**
- The optimizer fails to converge → the earlier weights are held for that rebalance, and the failure is recorded with the date.

### S3 — Features tested against chance

### Feature library (`FEAT`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Behavior:** The researcher defines candidate features from prices and volume. Each one is computed per stock per day from data up to that day only, and recorded with its definition.

**Requirements**
- **FEAT-01** Each feature is computed only from prices, volume, returns and corporate actions dated on or before its day.
  - Given a feature value for day D, when all data after D is removed and the feature is recomputed, then the value is unchanged.
- **FEAT-02** Each feature is registered with a name, a plain-language definition, its lookback length and the date it was added.
  - Given the registry, when it is listed, then every feature shows these four fields.
- **FEAT-03** The first candidate set covers at least these families: past-return momentum at several lookbacks, short-term reversal, realized volatility, volume and turnover change, and price range or liquidity measures.
  - Given the registry, when the S3 test run starts, then each family has at least one feature.
- **FEAT-04** Features can be computed on raw returns or on residual returns (FACT-05).
  - Given a momentum feature, when the residual version is requested, then it uses out-of-sample residuals only.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Not enough history for a feature's lookback → the value is missing, never filled with 0.

### Feature testing (`FTEST`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Behavior:** Each candidate feature is tested for out-of-sample information gain in predicting residual (market-removed) forward returns, using a learned model with and without the feature. Only features that pass a corrected test against chance survive into S4. All results are kept, failures included.

**Requirements**
- **FTEST-01** Targets are the forward residual returns over 1, 5 and 21 trading days, built from out-of-sample residuals (FACT-05).
  - Given a stock on day D, when its 5-day target is built, then it covers days D+1 to D+5, with residuals that use exposures fitted only up to D.
- **FTEST-02** A target is kept for a stock that leaves the index or stops trading during its window. It is computed up to the last trading day, never dropped.
  - Given a stock delisted on D+3, when its 5-day target is built, then the target includes returns through its final day.
- **FTEST-03** Information gain is the out-of-sample improvement in prediction error of a learned model given the feature, compared with the same model without it. It is measured on walk-forward, purged and embargoed splits (EVAL-05) within the development period.
  - Given a feature and a horizon, when tested, then the report shows the error with and without the feature on every walk-forward fold.
- **FTEST-04** Each feature at each horizon is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill. It is given the per-fold information gains, and builds a null by breaking the link between the feature and the target in time order. The feature survives only if the null of no gain is rejected at 5%, after correction across every feature-horizon pair ever tested in S3 (from the log, EVAL-08).
  - Given a feature made of random noise, when tested, then it does not survive.
  - Given a simulated feature with a planted link to the target, when tested, then it survives.
- **FTEST-05** The set of surviving features, with their horizons, is recorded as the S3 verdict.
  - Given a completed S3 run, when the verdict is read, then it lists the survivors, or says "none survived".
- **FTEST-06** S4 starts only from a recorded S3 verdict with at least one survivor.
  - Given the verdict "none survived", when an S4 forecasting run is started, then it refuses with "no surviving features from S3".

**Edge cases & errors**
- A feature that is missing for more than 50% of stock-days → reported as "insufficient coverage", and counted as tested in the correction.
- Two features that are almost the same (correlation above 0.95) → both are tested and counted. The report notes the overlap.

### S4 — Forecasting + trading agents

### Neural forecasting (`FCST`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Behavior:** Neural sequence models forecast each stock's residual return and volatility at 1, 5 and 21 days. Their inputs are the surviving S3 features and past returns. The models are built by hand from basic array and automatic-differentiation operations, checked against an established reference implementation, and must beat simple baselines out of sample.

**Requirements**
- **FCST-01** Return forecasts are made at the horizons where S3 features survived. Volatility forecasts are made at 1, 5 and 21 days.
  - Given an S3 verdict with survivors at 5 days only, when return forecasting is set up, then return forecasts are made at 5 days only.
- **FCST-02** Inputs on day D use only data up to D. Inputs include only the surviving features, plus past returns and past volatility.
  - Given a forecast for day D, when all data after D is removed and the forecast is remade, then it is unchanged.
- **FCST-03** Forecasting models are built from basic array and automatic-differentiation operations. No ready-made model components are used.
  - Given each model's layer, when its gradients are compared with numerical differences on a small input, then they agree within 1e-5 (relative).
- **FCST-04** The hand-built model is checked against an established reference implementation of the same model on the same data and settings.
  - Given the same data, settings and seed, when both are trained, then the hand-built model's validation loss is within 5% of the reference's.
- **FCST-05** Return forecasts are compared with baselines on out-of-sample walk-forward folds in the development period. The baselines are: a forecast of zero, the trailing mean, and a linear model on the same inputs.
  - Given a completed forecasting run, when results are shown, then each horizon shows the out-of-sample loss and the rank correlation with outcomes, for the model and for each baseline.
- **FCST-06** Volatility forecasts are compared with baselines out of sample: trailing realized volatility, an exponentially weighted average of squared returns, and a standard autoregressive volatility model.
  - Given a completed run, when results are shown, then each horizon shows the loss for the model and for each baseline.
- **FCST-07** A forecaster is said to "beat" its baselines only if the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, given paired per-fold loss differences against the best baseline, rejects the null of equal loss at 5% after correction across horizons and targets.
  - Given a forecaster no better than the trailing mean, when tested, then the verdict is "does not beat".
- **FCST-08** Training on a rented GPU is optional. Every model can also be trained on the laptop alone, and results are reproducible from a seed either way.
  - Given the same seed and data, when a model is trained twice on the same hardware, then the forecasts match within 1e-6.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Training loss diverges (it becomes undefined or grows tenfold) → training stops, and the run is logged as failed with the step where it happened.

### Forecast portfolios (`FPORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Behavior:** Portfolios whose weights come from the forecasts are run through the same walk-forward engine and cost model. They are compared with every S2 classical portfolio.

**Requirements**
- **FPORT-01** At each rebalance, weights are set from return forecasts, volatility forecasts and the best S2 covariance estimate, all as of the rebalance date. The weights are long-only, fully invested and unlevered.
  - Given a rebalance date, when weights are built, then they satisfy PORT-01 and use only forecasts made with data up to that date.
- **FPORT-02** A turnover limit is applied so costs do not dominate: one-way turnover is at most 50% per rebalance.
  - Given earlier and new target weights, when the trade is made, then one-way turnover is ≤ 50%.
- **FPORT-03** A forecast portfolio "beats the classical portfolios" only if, against each S2 portfolio, both of these hold:
  - its after-cost Sharpe ratio is higher, and the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill rejects the null of equal Sharpe at 5% after correction across all comparisons;
  - its realized variance is lower, tested the same way.

  Both checks run at base and 2× cost, first in walk-forward over the development period, then once on the locked holdout.
  - Given a portfolio with a higher Sharpe ratio but higher variance than minimum-variance, when tested, then the verdict is "does not beat".
- **FPORT-04** The final verdict also needs the paper-trading result (PAPER-05).
  - Given a holdout pass and a paper-trading fail, when the verdict is recorded, then it is "does not beat".

**Edge cases & errors**
- Forecasts are missing for a stock at a rebalance → that stock gets no new weight, and the count is recorded.

### PPO trading agent (`AGENT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Behavior:** A trading agent learns portfolio weights by proximal policy optimization (PPO) inside a backtest environment that uses the same data rules and cost model. It is built by hand from basic array and automatic-differentiation operations, checked against a reference implementation, and judged by the same rules as forecast portfolios.

**Requirements**
- **AGENT-01** The environment's state at day D holds only information as of D: surviving features, forecasts, recent returns and current weights.
  - Given the environment at D, when the state is inspected, then no value is dated after D.
- **AGENT-02** The agent's action is a set of target weights that meet PORT-01. Trades are filled at D+1 under the cost model (EVAL-02).
  - Given any action, when it is applied, then the weights are non-negative and sum to 1, and the cost charged follows EVAL-02.
- **AGENT-03** The reward is the after-cost portfolio return, minus a penalty on realized variance whose weight is a setting.
  - Given a day's return, cost and variance penalty weight, when the reward is computed, then it equals the return minus the cost minus the weighted penalty.
- **AGENT-04** PPO is built from basic array and automatic-differentiation operations. No ready-made reinforcement-learning components are used.
  - Given the clipped policy loss on a hand-worked batch, when computed, then it matches the hand calculation within 1e-6.
- **AGENT-05** The hand-built agent is checked against an established reference PPO implementation, on a standard control benchmark task and on the backtest environment.
  - Given 5 seeds each, when both are trained with the same budget, then the hand-built agent's median final return is ≥ 90% of the reference's on each task.
- **AGENT-06** The agent is trained only on development-period walk-forward training windows, and scored on the following out-of-sample windows.
  - Given a scoring window, when the agent is scored, then none of its training episodes came from that window or later.
- **AGENT-07** Agent results are reported as the median and range across at least 5 seeds.
  - Given a completed agent evaluation, when results are shown, then each metric has a median and a range across the seeds.
- **AGENT-08** The agent "beats the classical portfolios" only under the same tests as FPORT-03 and FPORT-04, using its median seed. It must also beat the forecast portfolios on after-cost Sharpe, tested the same way.
  - Given an agent that beats S2 but not the forecast portfolios, when the verdict is recorded, then it says it beats S2 but not the forecast portfolios.

**Edge cases & errors**
- The policy collapses to a single stock, with any weight above 50%, for 20 or more days in a row → this is flagged in the results.

### Paper trading (`PAPER`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Behavior:** Frozen versions of the candidate strategies and the S2 portfolios are run forward on new trading days as they arrive. Orders are recorded before the prices they will be filled at are known. Simulated fills use the cost model. Nothing connects to a broker.

**Requirements**
- **PAPER-01** Only frozen strategy versions are paper traded: settings, trained weights and code version are fixed at start.
  - Given a running paper session, when any part of a strategy changes, then it counts as a new version and starts a new session.
- **PAPER-02** After each close D, the system makes orders for D+1 from data up to D, and records them with a timestamp before D+1's prices are loaded.
  - Given an order record, when checked, then its timestamp is earlier than the loading of the price used to fill it.
- **PAPER-03** Orders are filled at the close of D+1 under the cost model, and the fills are recorded.
  - Given an order, when it is filled, then the fill price is D+1's close and the cost is 10 bps per side.
- **PAPER-04** Every S2 portfolio is paper traded alongside the candidates, over the same days.
  - Given a paper session for a candidate, when the session is read, then the S2 portfolios have records for the same days.
- **PAPER-05** A paper test counts toward a verdict only after at least 63 trading days (about 3 months). It passes only if the candidate's after-cost Sharpe ratio is higher, and its realized variance lower, than each S2 portfolio's over the same days. The test is also run with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, and its p-value is reported alongside the pass.
  - Given 40 trading days of paper results, when a verdict is requested, then the answer is "not enough days yet".
- **PAPER-06** The paper-trading log can only be added to, never changed.
  - Given a recorded order or fill, when an edit is attempted, then it is refused.

**Edge cases & errors**
- An update is missed for a day → orders for the missed day are not made after the fact. The gap is recorded, and the session continues from the next update.

## Data requirements
- **DATA-01** Daily open, high, low, close and volume, from 2005-01-03 to the latest close. It covers every stock that was an S&P 500 member at any time in that range, plus 3 years before each stock entered the index.

- **DATA-02** Point-in-time S&P 500 membership with effective entry and exit dates, including all former members.

- **DATA-04** Splits and dividends with ex-dates, and ticker changes with their dates.
  - Given 10 known splits and 10 known dividends, when they are checked against the store, then all 20 are present with correct dates and amounts.
- **DATA-05** All data sources together cost at most $50 a month.
  - Given the chosen sources, when costs are summed, then the total is ≤ $50 a month.

  - Given each source, when its terms are checked, then personal research and local storage are allowed.

## Assumptions
- Returns are total returns (splits and reinvested dividends). Price returns are kept alongside.
- The universe on each day is the S&P 500 membership at that day's close. A stock that leaves the index keeps its history up to its exit, final return included.
- Data updates are started by hand. A full download happens once, followed by add-on updates.
- The app runs locally for one user, with no login.
- The PPO agent and the neural forecasters are built by hand from basic operations, and checked against established reference implementations.
- Paper trading orders are fixed before the prices they are filled at are known, and use the backtest cost model.
- History runs from January 2005. Rebalancing is monthly, costs are 10 bps per side with a 2× re-check, the locked holdout is the last 2 years, and a paper test needs at least 3 months to count.
- Data that fails validation is stored with a flag, and the loader leaves it out by default.

## Open questions
- **Variance test against minimum-variance (FPORT-03, AGENT-08):** a return-seeking portfolio will rarely have lower realized variance than the minimum-variance portfolio, whose whole aim is low variance. Under the rule as written, S4 will very likely fail. A possible alternative: compare variance only against risk parity, HRP and equal weight, and require "not significantly higher" against minimum-variance. — The researcher.
- **Paper-test strength (PAPER-05):** 63 days is too short for a meaningful significance test, so the paper pass is directional, with its p-value reported for information. Is that the intended weight? — The researcher.
- **Can the data requirements be met within budget (DATA-03, DATA-05)?** — Settled during architecture, when sources are checked against these requirements.
