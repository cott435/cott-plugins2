### 1. Formulas (preprint pp. 7–9)

**Expected maximum, Eq. (1), p. 7.** Lead-in: "Appendix 1 proves that, under these assumptions, the expected maximum of {SR^_n} after N ≫ 1 independent trials can be approximated as:"

E[max{SR^_n}] ≈ E[{SR^_n}] + sqrt(V[{SR^_n}]) · ( (1−γ) Z^{-1}[1 − 1/N] + γ Z^{-1}[1 − (1/N) e^{-1}] )   (1)

Followed by: "where γ (approx. 0.5772) is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, Z is the cumulative function of the standard Normal distribution, and e is Euler's number."

**DSR / PSR, Eq. (2), p. 8.** Lead-in: "In this paper we propose a Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) statistic that corrects for both sources of SR^ inflation, defined as:"

DSR^ ≡ PSR^(SR^_0) = Z[ (SR^ − SR^_0) · sqrt(T − 1) / sqrt(1 − γ^_3 · SR^ + ((γ^_4 − 1)/4) · SR^²) ]   (2)

Followed by: "where SR^_0 = sqrt(V[{SR^_n}]) ((1 − γ)Z^{-1}[1 − 1/N] + γZ^{-1}[1 − (1/N)e^{-1}]), V[{SR^_n}] is the variance across the trials' estimated SR and N is the number of independent trials. We also use information concerning the selected strategy: SR^ is its estimated SR, T is the sample length, γ^_3 is the skewness of the returns distribution and γ^_4 is the kurtosis of the returns distribution for the selected strategy. Z is the cumulative function of the standard Normal distribution."

SR_0 is Eq. (1) with E[{SR^_n}] = 0, which is the null H0: SR = 0. Kurtosis enters as (γ^_4 − 1)/4, so it is non-excess kurtosis: Normal returns are written as γ^_3 = 0, γ^_4 = 3.

### 2. The numerical example ("A NUMERICAL EXAMPLE", pp. 9–10)

**Inputs, quoted:**
- "He uncovers that many combinations yield an annualized SR^ of 2, with a particular one yielding a SR^ of 2.5 over a daily sample of 5 years."
- "...arguing that an annualized SR^ of 2.5 must be statistically significant."
- "The analyst responds that N = 100, V[{SR^_n}] = 1/2, T=1250, γ^_3 = −3 and γ^_4 = 10."

**Outputs (p. 10, displayed formulas transcribed):**
- "In particular, SR^_0 = sqrt(1/(2·250)) ((1 − γ)Z^{-1}[1 − 1/100] + γZ^{-1}[1 − (1/100)e^{-1}]) ≈ 0.1132, non-annualized (with 250 observations per year), and DSR^ ≈ Z[ (2.5/√250 − 0.1132)·√1249 / sqrt(1 − (−3)·2.5/√250 + ((10−1)/4)·(2.5/√250)²) ] = 0.9004 < 0.95."
- "The investor has recognized that there is only a 90% chance that the true SR associated with this strategy is greater than zero."
- "Should the strategist have made his discovery after running only N=46 independent trials, the investor may have allocated some funds, as DSR^ would have been 0.9505, above the 95% confidence level."
- "If the strategy had exhibited Normal returns (γ^_3 = 0, γ^_4 = 3), DSR^ = 0.9505 after N=88 independent trials."

The confidence level used is 95%.

### 3. Annualised or per-period

The inputs are annualised: SR^ = 2.5 is "annualized", and V[{SR^_n}] = 1/2 is apparently an annualised variance. The formulas are evaluated per period (daily). The paper does not state a conversion rule in words. It shows the conversion only inside the worked formula:
- the Sharpe ratio is divided by √250, giving "2.5/√250";
- the variance is divided by 250, giving "1/(2·250)";
- the result is labelled "non-annualized (with 250 observations per year)";
- T = 1250 daily observations (5 years × 250), entering as √(T−1) = √1249.

So SR_0 ≈ 0.1132 is a daily figure. It is compared with 2.5/√250 ≈ 0.158, not with 2.5.
