# Idea: Predator and prey

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a third environment, the fish tank with a learning predator and foraging prey, so that I can learn co-evolution: two sides learning against each other, each in a world the other keeps changing.

## The problem
Every learner so far faces a world that stays the same while it learns. With two learners, each side's opponent changes under it, so curves can cycle, collapse or forget, and a stalled run might be a bug, a setting or the co-evolution itself. I want to see that happen one step at a time, and learn the standard fix of playing against past selves.

## Who it's for
Me, after the tank: comfortable with PPO and a shared brain for several fish, new to learning against a learning opponent.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goals 1–5; Goal 3 now applies to both sides
- **Touches:** the tank and its physics, views and shared brain (TANK, SENSE, PPO-05); the shared core, which assumes one learner per environment (CORE-01, CORE-03); the bars, which judge one side against bots (EVAL-05–EVAL-07); the scripted fish (FBOT-02)
- **Brief changes:** Not doing: "shared pieces cover Snake and the tank" becomes "shared pieces cover Snake, the tank and predator-prey"
- **Stage:** New S3. Its spec starts after M3, once PPO has run on the tank

## Recommended direction
The tank from S2, with the ball replaced by one predator fish and with food pellets that respawn. Five prey share one brain, as in T4. A prey earns +1 per pellet eaten and is removed when the predator catches it. The predator earns +1 per catch and pays a small cost every step, so waiting doesn't pay. The predator is about 1.5 times faster than a prey, like the ball. Episodes end when every prey is caught or at a time limit.

The environment is climbed as a ladder, one new idea per rung:

| Rung | What learns | Against | New idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | prey | a scripted predator | foraging and fleeing at once |
| P2 | predator | frozen P1 prey | hunting a learned opponent |
| P3 | both, taking turns | each other | non-stationarity under control |
| P4 | both at once | each other | full co-evolution |
| P5 | both | a pool of saved past snapshots | the fix for cycling and forgetting |

In P3 one side is frozen while the other trains, and they swap every few batches. P5 keeps snapshots of each side and trains each against opponents drawn from the other side's pool. Both learners are the hand-written PPO from S2.

Each side is judged on its own bars: prey against a random and a scripted predator (which chases the nearest prey), the predator against random and scripted prey (which flee the predator and pick up nearby pellets). The closing experiment is a cross-play table: every saved predator snapshot against every saved prey snapshot, for P4 and P5, showing whether later snapshots beat earlier ones or cycle.

## Alternatives considered
- Both sides learning at once from the start — a failure would say nothing about which of several new pieces broke
- Four rungs without P3 — the jump from one frozen side to both moving is a cliff
- Four rungs without P2 — a weak predator in P3 would have two possible causes
- Symmetric self-play, one species playing tag — one learner, but loses the predator and prey asymmetry
- Pack hunting with several predators — cooperation is a later environment's concept

## What success looks like
- Each side's final snapshot clearly beats its random opponent and matches or beats its scripted one, on every rung where it learns
- The cross-play table shows P4 cycling or forgetting, and P5 reducing it, or my note explains why it didn't
- I can replay a predator hunting and prey scattering, and narrate who adapted to whom
- The environment reuses the tank and the shared pieces, adding only what two learners in one environment need

## What we're betting on
- Two PPO learners on six bodies learn within the one-hour laptop limit
- Respawning pellets and the predator's time cost are enough shaping, with no hand-tuned bonuses
- A pool of 5–10 snapshots is enough to damp cycling

## Not doing
- Several predators or pack hunting — cooperation is a separate concept
- Separate brains per prey — the shared brain from T4 stays
- Growing tanks, obstacles or extra species — difficulty curricula stay out
- Designing the two-learner core before M3 — PPO should run on the tank first

## Open questions
- The predator's exact speed, catch distance and time cost
- Pellet count, respawn rule and the episode time limit
- How often P3 swaps sides, and the pool size and sampling in P5
- How many snapshots the cross-play table holds, and its time limit
- Whether the predator sees everything or only what's nearby
