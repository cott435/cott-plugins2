# Idea: Forecasts and forecast-driven portfolios

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds neural forecasts of next-month return and volatility, the long-only portfolios they drive, and a simulated paper-trading book that runs them forward beside S2's classical portfolios, so that I learn whether learned forecasting beats simple, classical allocation on data no choice has seen.

## The problem
S2 tells me which covariance estimate lowers variance, and S3 tells me which features carry information. Neither tells me whether forecasts built on those features make better portfolios after costs. Every backtest year has now been looked at: S3's holdout chose the surviving features, so any backtest comparison on those years is biased toward the new model. Neural sequence models also overfit easily and are expensive to tune, so a result needs simple baselines beside it and a test that only the future can pass.

## Who it's for
Me, as the researcher, once S3 has registered and run its feature list, and before any learned trading agent is built.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 4.
- **Touches:** S3's registered feature list and holdout verdicts (`HOLD-03`, `FREP-01`), and its residual-return machinery (`RESID-02`). S2's portfolios and rebalance schedule (`PORT-01`–`PORT-06`, `STRUCT-01`) become the comparison. The daily refresh (`LOAD-02`) drives paper trading. The spec's out-of-scope lines on deep learning, on trading on forecasts and paper trading, and on transaction costs narrow for this stage. The variance-portfolios and residual-features idea files are frozen records and are not edited.
- **Brief changes:** none, unless the cloud-GPU question below changes the constraints.
- **Stage:** New S4 (a roadmap stage on acceptance). Learned allocation agents (PPO) are a separate, later idea.

## Recommended direction
**Baselines first.** Every forecast has a simple rival. For return: the trailing 12-month mean and 12-1 momentum. For volatility: an exponentially weighted estimate of recent daily returns. A neural model counts only if it beats its baseline on development years.

**The neural model.** A sequence model in the temporal-fusion-transformer style (prior art: RF-02, RF-03) reads each name's recent daily history plus S3's surviving features. It predicts next-month return and volatility at each monthly rebalance date. It is small enough to train on an Apple Silicon laptop in a modest time. A rented cloud GPU is an occasional, manual option, never a dependency. If no S3 feature survived, the model uses price and volume history alone, and the report says so. RF-02's split without a gap, its full-sample vocabulary and its zero-filled gaps, and RF-03's dropped delisting tail, are not reused.

**Forecast portfolios.** Two long-only portfolios, rebalanced monthly. The first is the top fifth of names by forecast return, weighted by inverse forecast volatility. The second is S2's equal-risk-contribution portfolio with forecast volatilities in place of the sample's. Turnover costs 5 basis points, applied to every portfolio compared, S2's included.

**Development, then a frozen start.** Backtest years are for development only: model settings, baselines and portfolio rules are chosen there. Then everything is frozen and registered, as S3 does with its feature lists, including the retraining schedule. Paper trading starts on the first rebalance date after registration.

**Paper trading, simulated.** On each daily refresh, the paper book marks every portfolio to market. At each month-end it forms new weights from data known that day and fills them at the next trading day's open, net of costs. The forecast portfolios, S2's classical portfolios and equal weight all run in the same book. Nothing is sent to a broker.

**The verdict.** After 12 months of paper trading, each forecast portfolio's Sharpe ratio after costs is compared with each S2 portfolio's and equal weight's. Each comparison is a claim tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, corrected as one family. The `data-analysis` skill characterises forecast errors and their stability on development years.

## Data
No new sources. Training uses the S1 data version, S2's windows and S3's registered panel, all read as of each date; it covers 2002 to the data version's end, about 490 names. Labels are next-month residual and total returns and realized volatility. Development evaluation is walk-forward with a gap of one month between training and validation targets. The deciding evaluation is 12 months of forward paper trading on daily refreshes. The baselines are the simple forecasts above and S2's portfolios. `null-hypothesis-testing` owns the claims; `data-analysis` owns the exploratory analysis.

## Alternatives considered
- Judging on the backtest years — S3's holdout already chose the features there, so the result would be biased.
- A broker's paper account — a new external service and a possible cost, for fills a daily simulation can model.
- Volatility forecasts only — kept as part of the stage, since the risk-parity portfolio uses them directly.
- PPO agents in the same stage — a separate idea, once forecasts show they add anything.

## What success looks like
- Each neural forecast either beats its simple baseline on development years or is reported as not beating it.
- 12 months of paper trading, marked daily, with every portfolio's returns, turnover and costs.
- A verdict for each forecast portfolio against each S2 portfolio and equal weight: Sharpe after costs, significant or not after correction, with the effect size the 12 months could detect.

## What we're betting on
- S3 leaves at least one surviving feature, or price history alone carries enough for the volatility forecast.
- A small sequence model trains well enough on a laptop.
- 12 months can show a large difference. A small one will read as "not significant", and the paper book can keep running.

## Not doing
- Learned allocation agents (PPO) — the next idea.
- Real-money trading or any broker — the brief's Not Doing list stands.
- Short positions, leverage and intraday fills.
- Retraining or changing rules during the 12 months beyond the registered schedule.

## Open questions
- Cloud GPU: the brief says everything runs locally, and data costs must stay under $50 a month, with the Sharadar price still a $49 placeholder. Is a rented GPU allowed, and within which budget?
- Fills at the next day's open, or the next day's close?
- Should the app show the paper book, or are reports enough at first?
- Which S2 portfolios join the paper book: all nine full-set portfolios, or the best few from S2's own verdict?

  - **Baselines first:** trailing mean and momentum for return, an exponentially weighted estimate for volatility.

  - **Paper trading:** simulated on each daily refresh, with month-end rebalances filled at the next trading day's open.
  - **The verdict:** after 12 months, Sharpe after costs against S2's portfolios and equal weight, tested as one corrected family with `null-hypothesis-testing`.
