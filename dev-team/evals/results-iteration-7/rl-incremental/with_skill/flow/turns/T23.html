The plan is committed (`18a2ac6`), and the Base is re-recorded at that commit (`8328ada`), so the new fixtures are part of what the build starts from. `interfaces_changed` is now clear. **M1 is ready for `/build`**, apart from one question below that could affect the core.

## Environment 3, in terms of the project

**This adds a predator-prey foraging environment,** so that you can learn **co-evolution and self-play**: two learners whose targets keep moving because the other side keeps learning.

It fits the project's rules:
- It's the next concept, one per environment.
- S2 deliberately ruled out "a ball that learns to hunt" because multi-agent competition is its own concept. This idea is that concept, in its own environment.
- It serves the same four goals, so the brief likely doesn't change.

## Where I'd push back

**1. "Beats the baseline" stops meaning much.** When both sides learn, a rising prey score might mean the prey got better or the predator got worse. A learning curve on its own can mislead you. Measuring needs to be part of the design:
- **fixed reference opponents**, such as a scripted chasing predator (the T4 ball's logic) and the scripted flee prey;
- **a cross-play matrix** that plays every saved checkpoint of one side against every saved checkpoint of the other. That heatmap is how you *see* the arms race. It's the "watch each side change what the other must learn" you're after.

**2. Naive self-play often cycles or forgets.** The prey learns to dodge, the predator adapts, the prey forgets its old trick, and round it goes. That's a feature here, because it's exactly the lesson, but it means the design should include a fix to compare against: a pool of past opponents to train against, not just the latest one.

**3. Build it on the tank, not from scratch.** The same round tank, whisker rays, continuous swimming and PPO already exist in S2. Food pellets plus a learned predator in place of the ball is a much smaller step. A fresh environment would spend your time on physics instead of co-evolution.

**4. The core question again, and it matters before M1 runs.** The core assumes **one action space, one observation size and one policy per environment**. A predator and prey with different bodies would need per-team spaces, which is another core change, ideally made before M1-T01 freezes it. My way around it:
- give **both sides the same body**: the same 41 ray values (plus a "pellet" ray kind), the same turn-and-throttle action;
- make the predator faster, with a bigger catch radius;
- give each row a team label in `info`;
- add a small wrapper agent that routes prey rows to one PPO and predator rows to another.

Then the core stays exactly as approved, and M1 can start now.

## Questions that change the direction

1. **Same body, so the core stays unchanged?** That's my recommendation. Otherwise it's per-team spaces, which means a core revision before `/build`.
2. **Population:** 1 predator vs 3 prey, with about 10 pellets that respawn when eaten?
3. **Rewards:** for prey, +1 per pellet and −1 when caught; for the predator, +1 per catch and a small cost per step. Should it be strictly zero-sum, or allowed not to be?
4. **Training schedule:** start with **simultaneous** updates (to watch the instability), then **alternating** freezes, then an **opponent pool** of past checkpoints as the fix?
5. **What "done" looks like:**
   - the learned predator beats the scripted chaser against the flee prey;
   - the learned prey beats the flee fish against the scripted chaser;
   - a cross-play heatmap shows the arms race;
   - training against the pool measurably reduces cycling, compared with naive self-play.

   Right bar?

## Variations

1. **Scripted opponents first.** Prey learn against the scripted chaser, and the predator learns against flee prey, before either side meets a learner. This gives clean single-learner baselines.
2. **Naive simultaneous self-play.** The instability, on purpose.
3. **Alternating freezes.** Train one side while the other is fixed, then swap. The classic stabilizer.
4. **Opponent pool (fictitious self-play).** Train against random past snapshots, the standard fix for cycling.
5. **A cross-play dashboard** with an exploitability-style score against the fixed references, plotted through the stages.
6. **Speed-balance sweep.** Vary the predator's speed to find where the arms race stays interesting.
7. **Populations or communication** between prey: out of scope, since each is its own concept.

My instinct is **1 → 2 → 3 → 4, with 5 throughout**, 6 as a stretch, and 7 out.

Answer whichever questions you like. **Question 1 decides whether M1 can start `/build` right away.** With the same-body design it can.
