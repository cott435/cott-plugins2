### AD-13: The tank simulated in NumPy, a fixed 0.1-second step
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** tank
- **Context:** TANK-02 needs a mirror bounce at a constant speed; TANK-04 a move every 0.1 s capped at 2.5 units per second; TANK-05 a wall stop; TANK-07 removal at the end of each step; TANK-13 identical games from a seed. QR-01 needs a 5-fish PPO run and its test games inside 60 minutes.
- **Decision:** `tank.TankEnv` holds positions as NumPy arrays and runs each step in a fixed order: cap each move's speed, move every acting fish and stop it at the wall (centre at most 9.75 from the centre), move the ball 0.2 units with any bounce taken at the exact point it meets the wall (centre 9.5 from the centre), then remove every fish within 0.75 of the ball. Views are computed for all fish at once.
- **Alternatives:** A physics library (pymunk, Box2D): collision solvers built for rigid bodies, with their own time steps and float paths, for one bouncing ball. A ball bounce found by clamping after the move: the speed changes at a bounce, against TANK-02.
- **Consequences:** One step is a few dozen array operations, cheap enough for 600-step games. The step order is part of the game: changing it changes saved tank games.
- **Probe:** none — no external API.
