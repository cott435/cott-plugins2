# Quant Research Platform

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17

## The problem
I want to research trading signals on US stocks, but research is only as good as the data under it. Free or casually assembled price data usually leaves out companies that were later delisted, and it mixes in information that wasn't known at the time. Both make ideas look better than they are. Before any research, I need daily data I can trust and inspect.

## Who it's for
Me: one researcher working on a local machine, moving from data to signal testing and later to paper trading (simulated trades, no real money).

## Goals
1. Trust that every result reflects only what was knowable on each date, including companies that were later delisted.
2. Know the data is correct, with checks and an independent cross-check whose results I can see, not just assume.
3. Explore the data myself quickly: any ticker's history, its corporate actions (splits, dividends, delistings), and where checks failed.
4. Build each later research stage on the same trusted data without redoing the data work.

## Constraints
- One person, running locally.
- Data budget: under $50 per month. I already pay for Sharadar, which is the main source. Yahoo data, fetched through an unofficial free client, is the cross-check.
- Daily bars only. The universe starts as the historical S&P 500, with membership as it was on each date.
- The exploration app is part of the first stage. It is a TypeScript and React browser interface over a Python backend, with typed contracts between them, a distinctive look, and live progress shown for download and check runs.

## What success looks like
- My list of 10 delisted former S&P 500 members appears with correct histories, membership dates and exits.
- Adjusted returns agree with the Yahoo cross-check within an agreed tolerance on the names Yahoo still covers.
- All validation checks pass, or each failure is explained and visible in the app.

## What we're betting on
- My Sharadar subscription includes delisted companies and enough history of S&P 500 membership by date. If membership history isn't included, the universe needs another source within budget.
- Yahoo covers enough of the same companies to make a meaningful cross-check. It likely misses most delisted names, so those rely on the hand-checked list.
- The unofficial Yahoo client keeps working well enough for occasional checks.

## Not doing
- Intraday data — daily bars are enough for this research.
- Real-money trading — paper trading only, and not in this first stage.
- Markets outside the US, or the whole US market at first — the S&P 500 is small enough to check properly.
- Fundamentals and alternative data — not needed until a signal asks for them.
- Hosting, multiple users or scheduled cloud jobs — it runs locally for one person.
- An automated research pipeline — the first stage ends at trusted data and exploration.

## Open questions
- Does my Sharadar plan include historical S&P 500 membership, or only prices? (To be checked before the architecture relies on it.)
