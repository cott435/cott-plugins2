### Predator-prey tank (`HUNT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Stage:** S3
**Source:** [ideas/predator-prey.md](ideas/predator-prey.md)
**Behavior:** The fish tank with stakes. The bouncing ball is replaced by one predator fish, and food pellets are scattered around the tank. Prey lose energy every step and must eat pellets to keep it up, so a prey that only hides starves. A prey caught by the predator, or out of energy, is removed; the game ends when every prey is gone or the time limit is reached. Prey earn a reward for every step they stay alive; the predator earns one for every prey it catches. Both sides see only nearby things through rays. Each step runs in this order: every fish turns and swims, the predator catches, prey eat, then every prey loses energy.

**Requirements**
- **HUNT-01** The tank is a circle of radius 10 units holding one predator, a set number of prey from 1 to 10 (5 unless set otherwise) and 10 food pellets. The predator starts at the centre with a random heading; each prey starts at a random spot at least 3 units from the predator, with a random heading; each pellet starts at a random spot.
  - Given 5 prey and seed 2, when a game starts, then 1 predator, 5 prey and 10 pellets are inside the tank, and each prey is at least 3 units from the predator.
- **HUNT-02** Each step every living prey chooses a turn of −15° to +15° and a speed of 0 to 0.3 units per step, and the predator chooses a turn of −10° to +10° and a speed of 0 to 0.35 units per step; each fish turns first, then swims forward, and stops at the wall if it swims into it.
  - Given the predator at the centre heading east, when it chooses turn 0° and speed 0.35, then it ends 0.35 units east of the centre, still heading east.
  - Given a prey 0.1 units from the wall heading straight at it, when it swims at speed 0.3, then it ends touching the wall, inside the tank, with its heading unchanged.
- **HUNT-03** No fish's move changes another fish's position: prey pass through each other and through the predator.
  - Given two prey on the same spot, when both move, then each ends exactly where its own move would take it if it were alone.
- **HUNT-04** The predator (radius 0.5) catches every prey whose centre is closer than 0.8 units to its own (predator radius 0.5 plus prey radius 0.3) after the step's moves; a caught prey is removed.
  - Given a prey whose centre is 0.7 units from the predator's after a step, when the step is reported, then that prey is removed and is no longer asked for moves.
- **HUNT-05** Each prey starts with energy 1.0 and loses 0.002 at the end of every step; a prey whose energy reaches 0 starves and is removed.
  - Given a prey that never eats, when step 500 is reported, then its energy is 0 and it is removed.
- **HUNT-06** A prey whose centre comes within 0.5 units of a pellet (pellet radius 0.2 plus prey radius 0.3) eats it: its energy rises by 0.3, up to at most 1.0, the pellet disappears and a new pellet appears at a random spot, so 10 pellets are always in the tank.
  - Given a prey with energy 0.5 that ends its move 0.4 units from a pellet, when the step is reported, then its energy is 0.798 (0.5 + 0.3 − 0.002), that pellet is gone and 10 pellets are in the tank.
  - Given a prey with energy 0.9 that eats a pellet, when the step is reported, then its energy is 0.998 (capped at 1.0, then less 0.002).
- **HUNT-07** A game ends when every prey is gone or after 1,000 steps, with every prey still alive counted as surviving all 1,000.
  - Given 2 prey alive at step 1,000, when the step is reported, then the game has ended and both prey survived 1,000 steps.
- **HUNT-08** Each prey sees through 8 rays spread evenly around its heading, 45° apart, each reaching 4 units. Each ray reports the distance to the wall, to the predator, to the nearest other prey and to the nearest pellet along it, each scaled from 0 (touching) to 1 (nothing within reach); the prey also senses its own current speed, scaled 0 to 1, and its own energy. A prey's observation is these 34 numbers.
  - Given a prey heading east with energy 0.6, a pellet's edge 2 units straight ahead and nothing else within 4 units, when it observes, then the forward ray's pellet reading is 0.5, its energy reading is 0.6, and every other wall, predator, prey and pellet reading is 1.
- **HUNT-09** The predator sees through 8 rays spread the same way, each reaching 4 units, each reporting the distance to the wall and to the nearest prey along it, scaled from 0 to 1; it also senses its own current speed, scaled 0 to 1. The predator's observation is these 17 numbers.
  - Given the predator heading east with a prey's edge 2 units straight ahead and nothing else within 4 units, when it observes, then the forward ray's prey reading is 0.5 and every other wall and prey reading is 1.
- **HUNT-10** Each prey earns +1 for every step it survives and 0 on the step it is removed; the prey's score for a game is the average number of steps its prey survived.
  - Given 2 prey where one is caught at step 100 and the other survives to step 1,000, when the game ends, then the prey's score is 550.
- **HUNT-11** The predator earns +1 on each step for every prey it catches on that step, and 0 otherwise; the predator's score for a game is the number of prey it caught.
  - Given a step on which the predator catches 2 prey, when the step is reported, then its reward is +2.
  - Given 5 prey where the predator catches 3 and 2 starve, when the game ends, then the predator's score is 3.
- **HUNT-12** A game started with the same seed and played with the same moves unfolds identically, including every predator, prey and pellet position.
  - Given seed 7 and a fixed list of moves for both sides, when the game is played twice, then every frame, reward and both final scores are identical.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A move outside a control's limits → the step is refused with an error naming the side, the control and its limits; the game is unchanged.
- A move is sent for a removed prey, or a living prey or the predator has no move → the step is refused with an error naming the fish.
- A step is requested after the game has ended → refused with an error saying the game is over.
- A prey is caught on the step it would eat a pellet or starve → it is caught: the predator earns +1 and the pellet stays.
- Two prey reach the same pellet on the same step → the prey whose centre is closer eats it; on an exact tie, the one listed first; the other gains nothing.
- A new pellet would appear within 0.5 units of a living prey → it is placed elsewhere, so no pellet is eaten on the step it appears.
- A prey is removed on the step the time limit is reached → it is removed first, so it survived 999 steps.

### Self-play ladder (`SELF`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Stage:** S3
**Source:** [ideas/predator-prey.md](ideas/predator-prey.md)
**Behavior:** In the predator-prey tank both sides learn, each with its own copy of the limited-update agent, so each side's world keeps changing as the other improves. A ladder of three ways to train them shows why: taking turns (optional) keeps each learner's world still; simultaneous learners, the broken version kept on purpose, chase a moving target; self-play against past versions, the fix, trains each side against a mix of its opponent's current and saved past generations. Every run saves generations of both sides as it goes, for the cross-play table.

**Requirements**
- **SELF-01** Both sides are trained by the limited-update agent (PG-08), each side with its own policy: one shared by all prey, and one for the predator.
  - Given a predator-prey training run, when it starts, then two policies exist, one per side, and each learns only from its own side's steps.
- **SELF-02** Every predator-prey run saves a generation of each side at every tenth of training, 10 generations per side, the last at the end of training.
  - Given a finished run, when its saved generations are listed, then there are 10 predator generations and 10 prey generations, numbered 1 to 10 in the order they were saved.
- **SELF-03** In the taking-turns rung, training is split into 10 equal blocks that alternate sides, prey first; in each block one side learns while the other plays its latest policy frozen.
  - Given a taking-turns run, when block 1 ends, then the prey's policy has changed and the predator's is identical to its starting policy.
  - Given block 2, when it ends, then the predator's policy has changed and the prey's is identical to its policy at the end of block 1.
- **SELF-04** In the simultaneous rung, every training game pits the current predator against the current prey, and both sides learn after every game.
  - Given a simultaneous run, when any training game ends, then both sides' policies are updated from that game.
- **SELF-05** In the self-play rung, each training game is drawn as one of three kinds: current against current, both learning (50%); current predator against a past prey generation, only the predator learning (25%); a past predator generation against current prey, only the prey learning (25%). The past generation is drawn uniformly from the opponent's generations saved so far; until the first is saved, every game is current against current.
  - Given 10,000 self-play training games after the first generations are saved, when their kinds are counted, then 48–52% are current against current and 23–27% are each of the other two kinds.
  - Given 4 saved prey generations and 10,000 games against past prey, when the drawn generations are counted, then each was drawn in 23–27% of them.
  - Given a game of the current predator against a past prey generation, when it ends, then the predator's policy is updated and neither the current prey policy nor the saved generation changes.
- **SELF-06** The taking-turns rung is optional: S3 is complete with only the simultaneous and self-play rungs.
  - Given finished studies of the simultaneous and self-play rungs and no taking-turns runs, when the stage is checked, then it is complete, and every comparison and table leaves out the missing rung without counting it as failed.
- **SELF-07** A saved generation of either side can be loaded back, and the loaded generation picks the same moves.
  - Given a saved predator generation, when it is loaded and plays an evaluation game with seed 11 against the scripted prey, then it makes exactly the same moves as before saving.
- **SELF-08** A taking-turns or simultaneous rung that does not beat both baselines (EVAL-03), or whose later generations do not beat its earlier ones (CROSS-03), is recorded as a finding in the notes, and S3 doesn't count as failed.
  - Given a simultaneous rung whose last predator generation shows "no detectable difference" from its first, when the stage is checked, then it passes, and the notes page records it as a finding (EXPL-05).

**Edge cases & errors**
- A game where every prey is removed on the first step → both learning sides learn from those steps alone; the run does not stop.
- A saved generation is loaded for the other side → loading is refused with an error naming both sides' observation sizes.
- A run stops before all 10 generations are saved → the saved ones are kept and the run is marked incomplete.

### Cross-play table (`CROSS`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Stage:** S3
**Source:** [ideas/predator-prey.md](ideas/predator-prey.md)
**Behavior:** Each side's reward alone can't show learning, because its opponent improves too. The cross-play table pits every saved generation of the predator against every saved generation of the prey on the same held-out games, so the learner can see whether later generations beat earlier ones, which is real progress, or whether the two sides only chase each other in circles.

**Requirements**
- **CROSS-01** For each finished predator-prey run, every predator generation plays every prey generation on the same 20 held-out games with exploration off, filling a 10 × 10 table of the predator's mean score and the prey's mean score for each pairing.
  - Given a finished run with 10 generations per side, when its table is built, then it has 100 cells, each holding both sides' mean scores over the same 20 held-out games.
- **CROSS-02** The table is shown with predator generations as rows and prey generations as columns, one table per rung averaged over its 5 training seeds, built from saved generations only, never by training again.
  - Given the 5 seeds of the self-play rung, when its table is shown, then each cell holds both sides' mean scores over the 5 seeds' tables.
- **CROSS-03** For each rung and each side, the last generation's scores are compared with the first generation's with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, given each generation's scores against all 10 opponent generations on the same 20 held-out games for each of the 5 training seeds; the later generation beats the earlier only if its scores are higher at a 5% significance level after correcting for every such comparison in the predator-prey tank (6: 3 rungs × 2 sides; 4 without the taking-turns rung).
  - Given finished runs of every rung, when the comparison runs, then the report says, for each rung's predator and each rung's prey, "later beats earlier" or "no detectable difference", with the test result.
- **CROSS-04** In the self-play rung, the last generation beats the first for both the predator and the prey.
  - Given the CROSS-03 report, when it is read, then the self-play rung's predator and prey each show "later beats earlier".

**Edge cases & errors**
- A run has fewer than 10 generations saved → its table covers the saved ones, is marked incomplete, and the run is left out of the CROSS-03 comparison, which names it.
