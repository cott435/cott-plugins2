# Idea: Predator-Prey — Co-evolution and Self-Play

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a third environment, where one learning predator hunts a school of prey foraging for pellets, so that I learn co-evolution and self-play: why two sides learning at once go wrong, and how training against past opponents fixes it.

## The problem
In Snake and the fish tank, the world holds still while the agent learns. When the opponent is learning too, the target moves: progress can cycle, collapse, or look flat while both sides improve. I want to see that failure happen, then fix it myself, and watch the two sides adapt to each other.

## Who it's for
Me, the learner, after the fish tank. I'll have a working PPO and a tank with continuous swimming.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 1 (named concept: co-evolution and self-play), Goal 2 (one side against a frozen opponent, compared with library PPO), Goal 3 (each side beats fixed scripted opponents), Goal 4 (replays across generations), Goal 5 (the shared core, extended to teams)
- **Touches:**
  - The fish tank (FISH): reused physics, with a learning predator instead of the ball, plus pellets.
  - The policy-gradient agents (PG): my S2 PPO, one instance per side.
  - CORE-01 and CORE-02: agents in one environment map to more than one policy (a predator team and a prey team).
  - RUN-02: training saves checkpoints of each side along the way, not only at the end.
  - REPLAY: pick any prey generation and any predator generation.
  - The spec's out-of-scope line "competitive or cooperative multi-agent learning" narrows to cooperative only; "a learned or adaptive ball in the fish tank" stays, since the fish tank itself doesn't change.
- **Brief changes:** none
- **Stage:** new S3, built after S2

## Recommended direction
**The world.** The S2 tank, with one predator in place of the ball and a school of about 6 prey. Pellets appear at random spots and respawn when eaten. Prey earn reward for eating pellets and lose it when caught, so hiding forever isn't the best plan. The predator earns reward for catches. Both swim with continuous vectors under speed caps, and each sees only nearby things.

**The ladder,** one idea per rung:
1. **Fixed opponents.** The prey learn against a scripted predator, and the predator learns against scripted prey. This is the stable baseline, and the rung compared with the library.
2. **Naive co-learning.** Both sides learn at once against each other's latest policy. Expected to cycle or collapse, and that is the lesson.
3. **Self-play with an opponent pool.** Each side trains against a mix of the other's current and past saved policies. Expected to fix rung 2.

**Seeing adaptation:**
- Each side's checkpoints are saved every so often during training, as generations.
- A cross-play table pits every prey generation against every predator generation.
- A rating for each generation shows who's ahead over time.
- A replay gallery plays any prey generation against any predator generation.

**Tools:** both sides use my hand-written PPO from S2. The library comparison trains Stable-Baselines3's PPO on one side against a frozen opponent (rung 1's setup), the only fair single-agent case.

## Data
- All experience comes from the environment.
- **Fixed-opponent evaluation:** each side's final policy is scored against scripted and random opponents on held-out seeds, with 5 training seeds, as in S1 and S2.
- **Cross-play:** generations score against each other on the same held-out seeds.
- Claims such as "beats the scripted predator", "rung 3 beats rung 2 against the panel of past opponents" and "not worse than the library" are tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.

## Alternatives considered
- **Symmetric self-play (one policy plays both roles):** cleaner, but not the predator-prey world I want.
- **Several predators or herding prey:** emergent group tactics are a second concept.
- **No library comparison:** would leave Goal 2 unserved in this environment.

## What success looks like
- Each side clearly beats its scripted opponent after rung 1.
- Rung 2 visibly cycles or collapses in the cross-play table.
- Rung 3's final policies beat rung 2's against a fixed panel of past opponents.
- One side's hand-written PPO reaches 90% of library PPO against the same frozen opponent.
- A replay of an early generation against a late one shows the adaptation.

## What we're betting on
- Rung 2 fails visibly within one hour of training, so the lesson shows.
- A modest pool of past snapshots stabilises learning within the same hour.
- The S2 tank and PPO extend to two teams without a rewrite.

## Not doing
- Cooperative multi-agent learning (prey coordinating on purpose) — a different lesson.
- Several predators — a second concept.
- Symmetric self-play — not this world.
- A library comparison on rungs 2–3 — the library has no two-sided self-play.
- Population-based training or leagues beyond one pool per side — beyond the laptop budget.

## Open questions
- How often is a generation saved, and how large is each side's pool?
- What mix of current and past opponents does rung 3 train against?
- Which rating summarises cross-play (win rate, Elo-style), and what counts as a predator "win"?
- Pellet count, respawn time, predator and prey speeds, catch radius, vision radius: spec-writing proposes numbers.
- Does rung 1's predator learn against a fixed scripted school while the prey learn against a fixed scripted predator, as two separate runs?

  - rung 1, each side against fixed scripted opponents (this rung gets the library-PPO comparison);

- RUN-02 saves checkpoints during training, not only at the end.
