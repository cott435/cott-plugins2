# Idea: Forecasts and Forecast Portfolios

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds return and volatility forecasts from neural sequence models, checked against simple forecasting baselines, and portfolios driven by them, so I learn whether learned forecasts beat the variance-first portfolios net of costs, out of sample and forward in paper trading.

## The problem
The variance-first stage answers which risk estimates lower variance, and the feature stage answers whether any feature carries information. Neither says whether a forecast can be turned into a better portfolio. That is the claim home-built research most often gets wrong. A deep model fits noise in roughly 20 years of monthly decisions; costs erase small edges; and by the time this stage runs, the 2022-onward holdout has already been looked at once, by the feature stage. The only data no decision has touched is the future.

## Who it's for
Me, after the feature stage, with validated data, the variance-first portfolios and the feature report in hand.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** new Goal 8 (forecasts, forecast portfolios and agents judged net of costs against the variance-first portfolios)
- **Touches:** PORT-01 (long-only, 5% cap, kept), PORT-02 and PORT-03 (the portfolios forecasts feed), WALK-01–WALK-06 (the same monthly loop), EVAL-04 (the significance pattern), STRUC-03 (residuals), FREG and GAIN (the surviving features and the feature log), and three out-of-scope lines it narrows: trading signals and Sharpe ratios; portfolios using predictions or machine learning; transaction costs
- **Brief changes:** Goal 8 added
- **Stage:** New S4 (roadmap stage on acceptance); the trading agents are a separate idea and stage, S5

## Recommended direction
**Baselines first.** Every forecast is scored against simple ones on the same months: zero and the trailing mean for returns, an exponentially weighted variance and a HAR model for volatility. A neural forecast that doesn't beat them is reported as such, and its portfolio is not built.

**Volatility first.** The likeliest win is forecasting next-month volatility per stock and putting it into the minimum-variance and risk-parity portfolios in place of the window's sample variances, with correlations still from the variance-first estimators. This keeps the project variance-first.

**Then returns.** A temporal fusion transformer forecasts next-month returns and volatility from daily history, the surviving features and the controls. Its return forecasts drive a mean-variance portfolio with expected returns shrunk hard toward zero, under the same long-only and 5% cap rules. If no feature survived the feature stage, the forecasts use price and volume history and the controls only, and the report says so.

**Judged one way.** Every portfolio is charged 10 bp per side on turnover. The verdict is net-of-cost Sharpe ratio against the best variance-first portfolio, with a paired Sharpe-difference test. Every model configuration tried is logged, and the result is corrected for the number tried (a deflated Sharpe ratio). The null-hypothesis-testing skill owns both tests.

**Planted market first.** Before real data, forecasters and portfolios run on a synthetic market with planted, predictable volatility and a small planted return edge. They must recover both, and must find nothing on a market with neither.

**Forward paper trading decides.** Development is walk-forward on 2007–2021. 2022 onward is reported but labelled as already seen by the feature stage. The models are then frozen and fingerprinted, and run forward month by month, by hand, with no retraining. That forward record is the deciding test.

## Data
- The S1 snapshot, the S2 windows and portfolios, and the S3 surviving features and feature log; no new data or spending.
- About 180 development months × ~500 names, daily history for the sequence inputs.
- Targets: next-month return and realised volatility per member (RF-03 shows the label shapes; its embargo and survivorship caveats apply). Training windows follow RF-02, with an embargo between splits and point-in-time membership.
- Evaluation: walk-forward with annual retraining; volatility by QLIKE against HAR and EWMA, returns by rank correlation against zero, portfolios by net-of-cost Sharpe against the best S2 portfolio; then forward paper trading.

## Alternatives considered
- Mean-variance on raw forecasts — expected-return error swamps it; shrinkage is the minimum.
- Gradient-boosted forecasts only — kept as an option; the user wants a sequence model compared.
- End-to-end differentiable portfolios — hard to check, and they hide which part works.

## What success looks like
- On the planted market, the forecasters recover the planted volatility and edge, and find nothing in noise.
- Each neural forecast reports its margin over the baselines; volatility forecasts beat HAR on QLIKE, or the report says they don't.
- A forecast portfolio's net-of-cost Sharpe beats the best variance-first portfolio after the deflated correction, or the report says it doesn't.
- The forward paper-trading record matches the frozen fingerprint, month after month.

## What we're betting on
- Next-month volatility is predictable enough to improve the variance-first portfolios.
- A sequence model can be trained annually on one laptop in reasonable time.
- Forward paper trading over a few years is long enough to reject a large false claim, though not to prove a small edge.

## Not doing
- Trading agents of any kind — the next stage (S5)
- Shorting, leverage or intraday trading — long-only monthly, as the variance-first stage
- Real money or broker orders — paper trading only, recorded by hand
- Open-ended hyperparameter search — every configuration tried is logged and counted
- Fundamentals or alternative data — the brief keeps them out

## Open questions
- How long must forward paper trading run before a verdict: 24 months?
- Which variance-first portfolio is the benchmark: the one with the best development Sharpe, or each one?
- Forecast horizons: next month only, or also 1 and 5 days as in RF-03?
- Can annual retraining of the sequence model fit on the laptop, or does it retrain every two years?
