# Quant Research Platform

**Status:** Accepted · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18

## The problem
Personal stock research usually fools itself: price histories quietly leave out companies that failed, models peek at future data, and results that beat nothing in particular pass as discoveries. A one-person researcher has no team to catch these mistakes, so the setup itself has to catch them.

## Who it's for
One independent researcher studying the daily behaviour of US stocks, for learning and paper trading only.

## Goals
1. **Data that reflects what was knowable each day:** which stocks traded, including ones later delisted, with splits and dividends handled, and cross-checked against a second source.
2. **No claim without a test:** every estimator, feature, forecast and trading agent is compared out of sample with a simpler baseline, after trading costs, and must pass a test against chance, set before the run. Losses are recorded, not hidden.
3. **Risk before returns:** measure and minimise portfolio risk well first. Every later approach that predicts returns must beat those risk-based portfolios.
4. **One place to look:** the data and every stage's results can be explored visually as the work grows.

## Constraints
- One person, on a laptop, with an occasional rented graphics card for training neural networks.
- Daily US stock data from the existing Sharadar subscription (prices, corporate actions, company listings).
- Paper trading is simulated only. No real money and no broker.

## What success looks like
- About 1,000 of the most-traded US common stocks on each date, delisted names included, with disagreements between the two price sources explained.
- Covariance estimates ranked by the actual risk of the portfolios built from them, measured on later data.
- Every feature claim has a recorded test, corrected for how many features were tried.
- Forecast-driven portfolios and learning trading agents either beat the risk-based portfolios after costs, or the loss is written up.

## What we're betting on
- The subscription's history includes delisted stocks and enough corporate actions to rebuild the stock universe as of each date.
- Once the broad market's influence is removed, daily returns still hold some predictable signal. If not, the later stages end in documented negative results, which still count.
- A laptop can handle about 1,000 stocks over roughly 25 years of daily data.

## Not doing
- Intraday data, options or crypto: daily stocks only.
- An app for other people, or selling signals: this is a personal research tool.
- Real-money trading: paper only.
- Live market data: daily data arrives in batches.
- Company financial statements for now: no source has been chosen.

## Open questions
- Should company financial statements become feature candidates later? That needs a source and a new idea file.

### RF-01: PCA market-mode removal
**File:** `references/market_decomposition.py` · **Hash:** b3993e95 · **Kind:** code · **Milestones:** S2
- **Purpose:** removes the dominant principal components (the market mode) from a daily returns panel, leaving residual returns
- **Key parts:** `pca_factors(returns, k)`, `residualize(returns, factors)`, `remove_market(returns, k)`, `residual_correlation_check(residuals)`, `demean_returns(returns, min_obs)`
- **In → Out:** T×N simple daily returns (dates × tickers) → residuals (T×N), factors (T×k), loadings and betas (N×k), explained variance (k)
- **Use for:** the market-factor removal step and the residual returns later tested against; a "mean absolute correlation drops after removal" check
- **Caveats:** fits means, scales, eigenvectors and betas on the whole panel (look-ahead), so the project must fit per window; silently drops columns with under 60 rows and fills gaps with 0 returns; no random-matrix denoising; no error handling (k > N, empty panel)

### RF-02: Sequence-model window builder
**File:** `references/tft_dataset.py` · **Hash:** fdf31b69 · **Kind:** code · **Milestones:** S4
- **Purpose:** turns a long (ticker, date) panel into lookback/horizon training windows for a temporal-fusion-transformer-style forecaster, with static, known-in-advance and observed inputs kept apart
- **Key parts:** `WindowSpec`, `add_calendar_features(panel)`, `encode_static(panel, cols)`, `normalise_observed(panel, cols, window)`, `build_windows(panel, targets, spec)`, `split_by_origin(arrays, train_end, valid_end)`
- **In → Out:** feature panel + targets keyed on (ticker, date) → arrays static (S×n), known (S×(L+H)×n), observed (S×L×n), target (S×H×n), origin, ticker; split train/valid/test by origin date
- **Use for:** the windowing and dataset step of the neural forecasting stage; a test that observed inputs never pass the origin date
- **Caveats:** `split_by_origin` has no purge or embargo, so overlapping labels leak across splits; `encode_static` builds its vocabulary on the full sample and fails on unseen categories; inner merge and short-ticker skips drop rows silently (survivorship risk); windows can span date gaps; no exchange holiday calendar; stride-1 copies are memory-heavy

### RF-03: Forward return and volatility targets
**File:** `references/tft_targets.py` · **Hash:** fd947a4a · **Kind:** code · **Milestones:** S4
- **Purpose:** builds forward log-return and forward realised-volatility labels per ticker over several horizons
- **Key parts:** `forward_return(prices, horizon)`, `forward_volatility(prices, horizon, annualise)`, `make_targets(prices, horizons)`, `demean_cross_section(targets, cols)`, `drop_incomplete(targets)`

**Writing rules:**

- **One requirement, one behavior.** If a requirement contains "and", it's probably two.
- **Every requirement has at least one acceptance criterion** in Given/When/Then form. If you can't write one, the requirement isn't clear yet.
- **Describe behavior, not implementation.** "The customer sees their usual order" — not "the app queries the orders table."
- **Use exact numbers.** "≤ 2 seconds", "up to 50 regulars", never "fast" or "many".
- **Every feature names one stage.** Its `**Stage:**` line holds only the ID of the roadmap stage that first builds it. `dt lint` fails a feature with no stage, or one naming no stage row.
- **Requirement IDs are permanent.** `CODE-NN`, numbered within the feature, never reused or renumbered.
- **Quality requirements only when measurable.** Leave out generic wishes ("should be secure"); keep checkable ones ("passwords are never shown in full"). Cost, licence and budget limits are `QR-` items ("data costs ≤ $50 a month"), never `DATA-` or feature requirements.
- **No tech stack, libraries, vendors, schemas, endpoints, or file structure, even when the user names them.** Write the behavior the name stands for. The name itself belongs to `architecture-design`, which reads it in `docs/requests.md`.
- **Describe the product as it is now, not how it got here.** Requirement text, scope lists and assumptions carry no change notes, provenance tags ("from the field-photo idea", "UR-03") or process notes ("numbers chosen for review"). History goes in one Changelog row per round, and in git; which requirement honours which request goes in `docs/requests.md` (`dt request trace`).
- **The user's words win over the brief's.** Where a `spec` request is more specific than the brief or idea file ("circular tank", "one predator"), the requirement says what the request says, or the user approves the change.

**Data and ML projects — extra rules:**

- **Specify the data, not the source.** `DATA-NN` requirements say what the data must contain and guarantee ("hourly readings for every meter in the service area, including decommissioned ones, since 2015"; "every record is stamped with the date it became known"). Which API, vendor, or dataset meets them is an architecture decision, made by checking candidates against these requirements. Name a source only when the brief makes it a fixed constraint (our own database, a required benchmark).
- **Write data guarantees as testable criteria.** Leakage, look-ahead, selection bias, and coverage gaps are requirements, not hopes: "Given a forecast issued at time T, when the model requests weather, then no value published after T is returned."
- **Model quality is a requirement with a baseline.** Acceptance criteria name the metric, the held-out data, the threshold, and the baseline to beat: "Given the most recent 6 months of plates (never trained on), when ranked, then reviewing the top 20% catches ≥ 90% of scientist-failed plates, and beats ranking by Z′ alone." Never "the model is accurate".
- **No model types or libraries.** "Gradient boosting" or "PyTorch" are architecture decisions; the spec says what the model must achieve.
- **Learning projects: "build it by hand, then compare with a library" is a valid requirement. Write it without the names.** "The hand-written CNN reaches ≥ 90% of the accuracy of an established reference implementation on the same dataset." "The logistic regression is written from basic array operations; a reference library is used only to check its output." The architecture picks the reference.

## Hygiene check

- **The user's words.** `dt request --open --owner spec` is empty, or each one left is a change you ask the user about.

- **Implementation names.** Search for library, framework, vendor, dataset, file-format, database and model-type names, file and function names (a reference's included), including names the user, the brief or the idea file used. # The user's words: `docs/requests.md`

Every planning skill follows this. The brief and the spec compress, and the next skill often runs in a fresh session that sees only the docs. `docs/requests.md` keeps what the user actually said, word for word, so the skill whose doc must honour it can. `dt request` writes every line; never edit the file by hand.

## What goes in

One request (`UR-NN`) per concrete statement you could check a doc against:

- facts about the domain, the data or the world ("the tank is circular"), numbers, limits, deadlines, budgets
- tools, libraries, vendors, protocols and data sources the user names ("stream prices over a WebSocket", "use yahooquery")
- choices: an option picked from yours ("Go with A"), and the answer to any question a skill asked
- a block acceptance ("assumptions 1–7 are fine"): one request, its `--about` listing what was accepted
- scope: what must be in, or left out

Not: approval of a whole doc ("approved", "looks good"), wishes with nothing to check ("make it nice"), your own proposals the user hasn't picked, or words already recorded (`dt request` lists them).

## Recording

As you hear it, before you write any doc:

```
dt request add <owner> "<their words>" --skill <this skill> [--about "<what it answers>"] [--replaces UR-NN]
```

- **Verbatim.** Quote the shortest span of their message that carries the fact. Never paraphrase, translate, correct or merge. A message stating three facts is three requests.
- **`--about`** whenever the words don't stand alone: "Go with A" → `--about "AD-22 price source: A = Sharadar, B = yfinance"`; "yes, 3" → what was asked. A bare letter means nothing to the next session, and the same words can pick opposite options in two rounds.

- **The user changes their mind:** record the new words with `--replaces` the old UR. A line is never edited or deleted.
- The request that started the run is the user's first message: record it too.

# Quant Research Platform — Spec

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **Brief:** [brief.md](brief.md)

## Summary
A personal research platform for daily US stocks, built in four stages: a point-in-time daily dataset with its loader, checks and exploratory analysis; market-factor removal, covariance estimation and classical risk-based portfolios; candidate features tested for information gain over market-removed returns; and neural forecasts, forecast-driven portfolios and learning trading agents held to the bar of beating the classical portfolios after costs, ending in simulated paper trading. Every comparison runs through one shared out-of-sample testing harness, and the data and every recorded result can be browsed in a local app. See the [brief](brief.md) for why.

## Users
| User | What they need to do |
|---|---|
| The researcher (sole user) | Build and refresh the dataset, load it as of any date, run each stage's experiments, see every result (wins and losses) with its pre-registered test, and browse data and results visually |

## Scope
**In scope:** Dataset build (`BUILD`), Point-in-time universe (`UNIV`), Second-source cross-check (`XCHK`), Research loader (`LOAD`), Exploratory analysis (`EDA`), Explorer app (`APP`), Market-factor removal (`FACT`), Correlation denoising (`DENO`), Covariance comparison (`COV`), Classical portfolios (`PORT`), Testing harness (`HARN`), Results explorer (`RESX`), Candidate features (`FEAT`), Feature tests (`FTEST`), Neural forecasts (`FCST`), Forecast-driven portfolios (`FPORT`), Trading agents (`AGENT`), Simulated paper trading (`PAPER`)

**Out of scope:**
- Intraday data, options and crypto: daily US stocks only.
- An app or web UI for other people, and selling signals: one user, on their own machine.
- Real-money trading, broker accounts (including broker paper accounts): paper trading is simulated.
- Live market data: daily data arrives in batches.
- Company financial statements as data or features, until a source is chosen in a new idea file.

## Features

### Dataset build (`BUILD`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** The researcher runs one command to download the daily prices, corporate actions and company listings from the subscription, clean them, validate them and store them locally. A later run fetches only what is new. Each run ends with a validation report; data that fails validation is never silently stored as good.

**Requirements**
- **BUILD-01** A full build downloads daily prices, corporate actions and company listings for every US common stock in the subscription, including delisted ones, and stores them locally.
  - Given an empty local store, when the researcher runs a full build, then afterwards the store holds prices, corporate actions and listings for every common stock the subscription lists, active and delisted.
- **BUILD-02** An incremental build adds only trading days and corrections published since the last build.
  - Given a store built through date D, when the researcher runs an update after new data is published, then only data after D (and vendor corrections to earlier rows) is downloaded, and the earlier stored rows are unchanged except where corrected.
  - Given a vendor correction to an earlier row, when it is stored, then the earlier value is kept with the date it was replaced, so an as-of-D load still returns what was known on D.
- **BUILD-03** Cleaning resolves duplicate rows, non-positive prices and missing trading days before storage.
  - Given a raw batch with a duplicated (stock, date) row, when it is cleaned, then exactly one row is stored and the duplicate is listed in the run report.
  - Given a price ≤ 0 or a missing trading day inside a stock's listed life, when it is cleaned, then the row is flagged (not filled with a zero return) and listed in the run report.
- **BUILD-04** Every build ends with a validation report that passes or fails.
  - Given a completed build, when the report is produced, then it states counts of stocks, stock-days, delisted stocks and flagged rows, and the result of every check in `XCHK` and `UNIV`.
  - Given any check that fails, when the build finishes, then the run is marked failed and the loader refuses to treat it as the current dataset until the researcher accepts it.
- **BUILD-05** An interrupted build can be resumed without starting over.
  - Given a build stopped part-way (network loss, laptop sleep), when the researcher reruns it, then it continues from the last completed batch and the final store equals one built without interruption.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Subscription rejects the credentials or the quota is hit → the run stops with that reason, stores nothing partial as current, and can be resumed later.
- A ticker is reused by a different company after a delisting → the two are stored as distinct stocks, never merged.
- A stock changes ticker → its history stays one stock under its permanent identity.

### Point-in-time universe (`UNIV`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** For every trading date the platform knows which stocks were in the research universe that day, using only what was knowable the day before: about 1,000 of the most-traded US common stocks, delisted names included.

**Requirements**
- **UNIV-01** For each trading date, the universe is the 1,000 US common stocks with the highest trailing dollar volume, measured over the previous 63 trading days.
  - Given trading date D, when the universe for D is requested, then it holds the 1,000 common stocks with the highest median daily dollar volume (price × shares traded) over the 63 trading days ending the day before D.
  - Given a stock with fewer than 63 trading days of history before D, when the universe for D is built, then it is ranked only once it has at least 63 days.
- **UNIV-02** Universe membership on date D uses no data from D or later.
  - Given the universe for D, when any price, volume or listing published on or after D is changed, then the universe for D is unchanged.
- **UNIV-03** Stocks that later delist stay in the universe on every date they qualified.
  - Given a stock that qualified on D and delisted after D, when the universe for D is requested, then the stock is in it, and its final return through delisting is part of the data.
- **UNIV-04** If the data can't support the dollar-volume universe, the universe falls back to S&P 500 membership as it was on each date.
  - Given the decision (made when the data source is checked) that the dollar-volume universe can't be built, when the universe for D is requested, then it holds the stocks that were S&P 500 members on D, including those later removed or delisted.
- **UNIV-05** Only common stocks are eligible.
  - Given a listing that is an ETF, ADR, preferred share, warrant, unit or fund, when the universe is built, then it is never included.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Fewer than 1,000 eligible stocks on an early date → the universe holds all eligible stocks, and the report states the count for that date.
- Ties at rank 1,000 → broken by the permanent stock identity so the result is reproducible.

### Second-source cross-check (`XCHK`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** Every build compares the stored prices against a second, independent price source and explains every disagreement that matters.

**Requirements**
- **XCHK-01** Daily total returns are compared with a second independent source for every universe stock it covers.
  - Given a completed build, when the cross-check runs, then for each universe stock-day both sources cover, the two daily total returns (dividends and splits included) are compared, and the report states the share of stock-days within 0.5% of each other.
  - Given the share within 0.5% is below 99%, when the build finishes, then the cross-check fails.
- **XCHK-02** Every stock-day disagreeing by more than 2% is listed with a reason.
  - Given a stock-day whose returns differ by more than 2%, when the report is produced, then it appears with one reason: split, dividend, missing in one source, stale price, or unexplained.
- **XCHK-03** Corporate actions are checked against the second source.
  - Given a split or dividend in the stored data, when the cross-check runs, then the report states whether the second source shows the same action within 1 trading day, and lists each mismatch.
- **XCHK-04** Coverage gaps in the second source are reported, not treated as errors.
  - Given a stock the second source doesn't cover (often a delisted one), when the cross-check runs, then it is counted as "not covered", and the report states the share of universe stock-days covered.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Second source unreachable → the cross-check is marked "not run" (not passed), and the build can still be accepted manually with that noted.

### Research loader (`LOAD`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** Every later stage gets its data through one loader: for a date range and an as-of date, it returns prices, total returns, corporate actions and the point-in-time universe exactly as they were knowable.

**Requirements**
- **LOAD-01** The loader returns daily adjusted prices and total returns for a date range.
  - Given a date range, when the researcher loads prices, then each stock-day carries the close, the split-adjusted close and the total return including dividends.
- **LOAD-02** The loader returns corporate actions for a date range.
  - Given a date range, when the researcher loads corporate actions, then every split, dividend, ticker change and delisting in that range is returned with its effective date.
- **LOAD-03** A load can be restricted to the point-in-time universe.
  - Given a date range, when the researcher loads universe returns, then each date holds only the stocks in that date's universe, and stocks that later delisted are present on their qualifying dates.
- **LOAD-04** A load as of date A returns only what was known on A.
  - Given as-of date A, when anything is loaded, then no row, correction or adjustment published after A is returned.
- **LOAD-05** Missing data stays missing.
  - Given a stock-day with no trade, when it is loaded, then its return is marked missing, never filled with zero.
- **LOAD-06** The loader only serves accepted builds.
  - Given the latest build failed validation and wasn't accepted, when the researcher loads, then the last accepted build is served and a warning names the failed one.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A date range before the data starts or after the latest build → the loader returns what exists and states the actual range covered.

**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** A repeatable exploratory analysis characterises the dataset: coverage over time, return distributions and tails, missing data, correlation structure, and how many stocks delist each year.

**Requirements**
- **EDA-01** The exploratory analysis is run with the `data-analysis` skill on the accepted dataset.

- **EDA-02** The analysis results are saved with the build they came from and can be rerun.
  - Given the same accepted build, when the analysis is rerun, then it produces the same figures and numbers.
- **EDA-03** The analysis results can be viewed in the explorer app.
  - Given a saved analysis, when the researcher opens it in the app, then every figure and table is shown with the build it came from.

### Market-factor removal (`FACT`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Within each estimation window, the dominant factors of the returns (the market first) are found by principal component analysis and removed, leaving residual returns that later stages study.

**Requirements**
- **FACT-01** Factor removal fits principal components of the window's universe returns and subtracts each stock's exposure to the top k components.
  - Given an estimation window and k, when factors are removed, then the residual returns for that window are the returns minus each stock's fitted exposure to the top k principal components of that window's returns.
- **FACT-02** Factor removal uses only data inside the estimation window.
  - Given a window ending on date E, when any return after E is changed, then the factors, exposures and residuals up to E are unchanged.
  - Given residuals for a later out-of-sample day, when they are produced, then they use the components and exposures fitted on the window before that day.
- **FACT-03** The number of components removed is chosen by a rule fixed before the run.
  - Given a run, when it starts, then k is either a fixed number or chosen per window by a stated rule (such as the count of eigenvalues above the random-matrix bound in `DENO`), and the choice is recorded with the run.
- **FACT-04** Removal reduces cross-stock correlation, and this is checked.
  - Given a window's raw and residual returns, when the check runs, then the mean absolute pairwise correlation of the residuals is lower than that of the raw returns, and both values are recorded.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A stock with fewer than 80% of the window's days observed → excluded from that window's fit (not filled with zeros), and the exclusion is listed.
- k ≥ the number of stocks in the window → the run stops with that reason.

### Correlation denoising (`DENO`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** The window's correlation matrix is cleaned with random-matrix theory: eigenvalues inside the Marchenko–Pastur noise band for the window's shape are treated as noise and flattened, while those above it are kept.

**Requirements**
- **DENO-01** Eigenvalues at or below the Marchenko–Pastur upper edge for the window's ratio of stocks to days are replaced by their average, and the ones above are kept.
  - Given a correlation matrix from T days and N stocks, when it is denoised, then every eigenvalue ≤ (1 + √(N/T))² (scaled by the fitted noise variance) is replaced by the mean of those eigenvalues, the eigenvalues above it are unchanged, and the result is rescaled to a unit diagonal.
- **DENO-02** The denoised matrix is a valid correlation matrix.
  - Given any denoised matrix, when it is checked, then it is symmetric, has a unit diagonal and has no negative eigenvalues.
- **DENO-03** Denoising recovers known structure on synthetic data.
  - Given a synthetic return panel with a known number of factors plus noise, when its correlation is denoised, then the count of kept eigenvalues equals the true factor count, and the denoised matrix is closer to the true correlation than the sample one.
- **DENO-04** The noise fit and the count of kept eigenvalues are recorded per window.
  - Given a denoising run, when it finishes, then each window's fitted noise variance, upper edge and number of kept eigenvalues are saved.

### Covariance comparison (`COV`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Three covariance estimators (sample, shrinkage and denoised) are estimated on each rolling window and ranked by the realized variance of the portfolios built from them on the following, unseen period.

**Requirements**
- **COV-01** Each estimation window produces a sample, a shrinkage and a denoised covariance estimate of the universe returns.
  - Given an estimation window, when the estimates are made, then the sample covariance, a covariance shrunk toward a structured target with its intensity chosen from that window's data only, and a covariance rebuilt from the `DENO` correlation with the window's volatilities are all produced.
- **COV-02** Estimators are ranked by the out-of-sample realized variance of the portfolios built from them.
  - Given the walk-forward schedule of `HARN`, when each estimator's covariance drives each portfolio method of `PORT`, then the realized variance of each portfolio over the following unseen period is recorded, and estimators are ranked per portfolio method by average realized variance.
- **COV-03** The comparison of estimators is analysed with the `data-analysis` skill and tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
  - Given the out-of-sample realized variances, when the comparison is made, then the `data-analysis` skill characterises them (by period and market regime), and the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill tests each estimator against the sample covariance on the paired per-period variances; an estimator counts as better only if the null of no difference is rejected at 5% after correction for the number of comparisons.
- **COV-04** Forecast accuracy of the risk estimate is reported alongside.
  - Given each portfolio and estimator, when results are recorded, then the predicted variance at rebalancing and the realized variance afterwards are both stored, with their ratio.

### Classical portfolios (`PORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Minimum-variance, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity portfolios are built from each covariance estimate, rebalanced on a fixed schedule, and compared out of sample on realized variance after trading costs, with turnover reported. These are the benchmarks every later stage must beat.

**Requirements**
- **PORT-01** A minimum-variance portfolio is built from each covariance estimate.
  - Given a covariance estimate, when the portfolio is built, then its weights are fully invested, long-only, at most 5% per stock, and minimise predicted variance under those limits.
- **PORT-02** A risk parity portfolio is built from each covariance estimate.
  - Given a covariance estimate, when the portfolio is built, then each stock's contribution to predicted portfolio variance is equal to within 1%, and weights are fully invested and long-only.
- **PORT-03** A hierarchical risk parity portfolio is built from each covariance estimate.
  - Given a covariance estimate, when the portfolio is built, then stocks are clustered by correlation distance into a tree, ordered by it, and weights are split down the tree in inverse proportion to each branch's variance, fully invested and long-only.
- **PORT-04** Portfolios are compared out of sample on realized variance after costs, with turnover reported.
  - Given the walk-forward schedule of `HARN`, when each portfolio is held over its unseen period, then its realized variance, its return after the per-trade cost, and its turnover per rebalance are recorded.
- **PORT-05** An equal-weight portfolio of the universe is the simple baseline.
  - Given any comparison, when results are reported, then the equal-weight universe portfolio, under the same schedule and cost, is shown alongside.
- **PORT-06** The best classical portfolio is named as the benchmark for later stages.
  - Given the completed comparison, when it is recorded, then the portfolio (method and estimator) with the lowest out-of-sample realized variance after costs is saved as the stage benchmark, together with all the others.

**Edge cases & errors**
- The optimiser can't meet the limits on a date → that date keeps the previous weights, and the event is recorded with the run.
- A held stock delists mid-period → its final return through delisting is realised and its weight sits in cash until the next rebalance.

### Testing harness (`HARN`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** One shared harness runs every comparison in the project. Before a run, the researcher registers the claim, its baseline, the metric, the test and its threshold; the harness then runs it walk-forward on unseen data, applies costs, and records the result whether it wins or loses.

**Requirements**
- **HARN-01** Every run is pre-registered before it touches out-of-sample data.
  - Given a new comparison, when the researcher starts it, then the claim, the baseline, the metric, the test, the significance level and the final test period must be stated, and are saved and locked before any result is computed.
  - Given a registered run, when its settings are changed after results exist, then the harness records it as a new run, never as an edit of the old one.
- **HARN-02** Comparisons run walk-forward: estimate on a past window, then hold on the following unseen period.
  - Given a strategy, when it is run, then by default it is estimated on the previous 252 trading days and rebalanced every 21 trading days, and each period's result uses only data from before that period.
- **HARN-03** Learned models are trained, tuned and tested on separated periods with a gap.
  - Given a model whose labels look h trading days ahead, when data is split, then training, validation and test periods are in time order, and at least h trading days are removed between each pair so no label overlaps the next period.
- **HARN-04** A final test period is held back for each claim and used once.
  - Given a registered claim, when it is run on its final test period, then that run is the claim's verdict, and any later run on the same period is marked as a repeat.
- **HARN-05** Every comparison applies one per-trade cost and reports turnover.
  - Given any portfolio or agent, when its result is computed, then a cost of 10 basis points of traded value is charged on every trade, the cost is stated with the result, and turnover per rebalance and per year is reported.
- **HARN-06** Every claim is tested against chance with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
  - Given a completed run, when its verdict is decided, then the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill is given the per-period results of the candidate and the baseline and the registered test; the claim passes only if the null of no improvement is rejected at the registered level, after correction for every candidate tried under that claim.
- **HARN-07** Every run's result is recorded, wins and losses alike.
  - Given any finished or abandoned run, when it ends, then its registration, results, test verdict and a written conclusion are saved, and a loss is recorded with the same detail as a win.
- **HARN-08** Every run can be reproduced.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A run is stopped before finishing → recorded as abandoned, with how far it got.
- A run would read data inside its final test period before its verdict run → the harness refuses and says why.

### Results explorer (`RESX`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** The explorer app grows into a lab notebook: every recorded run from every stage can be found, compared and charted next to its baseline and test verdict.

**Requirements**
- **RESX-01** The researcher can list every recorded run.
  - Given recorded runs, when the researcher opens the results list, then each run shows its stage, claim, date, verdict (passed, failed, abandoned) and baseline, and the list can be filtered by stage and verdict.
- **RESX-02** The researcher can open a run and see it against its baseline.
  - Given a run, when the researcher opens it, then its registration, the candidate's and baseline's out-of-sample results over time (variance, return after costs, turnover) and the test verdict are shown.
- **RESX-03** The researcher can compare runs side by side.
  - Given 2 to 6 runs, when the researcher selects them, then their out-of-sample results are charted on shared axes.
- **RESX-04** Each later stage's results appear without a new screen being built for them.
  - Given a run from stage S3 or S4 recorded through `HARN`, when the researcher opens the results list, then it appears and opens like any S2 run.

### Candidate features (`FEAT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** Candidate features are built from the daily data for every universe stock-day, using only what was known by that day's close, so they can be tested against later residual returns.

**Requirements**
- **FEAT-01** Each candidate feature is computed per universe stock-day from data up to that day's close.
  - Given a feature value for stock S on date D, when any data after D is changed, then the value is unchanged.
- **FEAT-02** Each feature is registered with a definition before it is tested.
  - Given a new feature, when it is added, then its definition, inputs and lookback are recorded, and it counts toward the number of features tried from that moment.
- **FEAT-03** Features are standardised across the stocks of each date's universe.
  - Given date D, when features are standardised, then each feature has mean 0 and standard deviation 1 across D's universe, using only D's universe members.
- **FEAT-04** The initial candidate set covers price, volume and corporate-action information.
  - Given the first feature run, when the candidates are listed, then they include at least: past residual returns over several lookbacks, residual volatility, dollar volume and its change, and recent corporate actions.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A feature can't be computed for a stock-day (not enough history) → it is marked missing, never filled with zero, and the missing share is reported per feature.

### Feature tests (`FTEST`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** Each candidate feature is tested for information gain over future market-removed returns using learned models on real data. Only features that pass a pre-registered test against chance, corrected for how many features were tried, survive to stage S4.

**Requirements**
- **FTEST-01** The target is the next-period residual return from `FACT`.
  - Given date D, when the target is built, then it is each universe stock's residual return over the registered horizon after D, with factors fitted only on data up to D.
- **FTEST-02** Information gain is measured as the out-of-sample improvement of a learned model with the feature over the same model without it.
  - Given a feature, when it is tested, then a model trained with it and one trained without it are compared on the `HARN` test period using a registered metric (such as the rank correlation of predicted and realised residual returns), and the difference is the feature's gain.
- **FTEST-03** Every feature claim is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
  - Given a feature's per-date gains on the held-out period, when it is tested, then the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill is given those gains and the count of every feature tried; the feature survives only if the null of zero gain is rejected at 5% after correction for that count.
- **FTEST-04** The data behind each test is characterised with the `data-analysis` skill on the real data.
  - Given a feature test, when it is run, then the `data-analysis` skill reports the feature's distribution, missing share, stability across years and its correlation with the other candidates, on the real universe data.
- **FTEST-05** Every feature's verdict is recorded, failed ones included.
  - Given a tested feature, when its verdict is decided, then the feature, its gain, the test result and the correction applied are recorded through `HARN`, and the list of survivors is saved as the stage S3 result.

**Edge cases & errors**
- No feature survives → stage S3 records a documented negative result, and stage S4 forecasts are built on the baseline inputs only (past returns and volatility).

### Neural forecasts (`FCST`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Neural networks that read each stock's recent history as a sequence forecast its future return and volatility from the features that survived stage S3, and are compared against simple forecasting baselines.

**Requirements**
- **FCST-01** Sequence models forecast each stock's forward return over registered horizons.
  - Given date D and a stock in D's universe, when a forecast is made, then the model reads the stock's surviving-feature history over a fixed lookback ending at D and outputs its forward return for each registered horizon (by default 1, 5 and 21 trading days).
- **FCST-02** Sequence models forecast each stock's forward realised volatility over the same horizons.
  - Given date D, when a volatility forecast is made, then it is the realised volatility of daily returns over the h trading days after D, for each registered horizon.
- **FCST-03** Forecast inputs never reach past the forecast date.
  - Given a forecast made on D, when any input dated after D is changed, then the forecast is unchanged.
- **FCST-04** Return forecasts are compared with simple baselines.
  - Given the test period, when return forecasts are scored, then their rank correlation with realised returns is compared with that of a zero forecast and of the stock's own trailing mean; the claim passes only if the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill rejects the null of no improvement over the better baseline at 5% after correction for the number of models and horizons tried.
- **FCST-05** Volatility forecasts are compared with simple baselines.
  - Given the test period, when volatility forecasts are scored, then their error against realised volatility is compared with that of the trailing 21-day volatility and an exponentially weighted volatility; the claim passes only if the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill rejects the null of no improvement at 5% after correction.
- **FCST-06** Delisted stocks' final periods stay in the test.
  - Given a stock that delists within a forecast horizon, when its target is computed, then its realised return through delisting is used, not dropped.
- **FCST-07** Training can run on a rented graphics card and the results are brought back to the laptop.
  - Given a training run, when it runs on rented hardware, then its trained model, settings and seeds are saved so that forecasts and the `HARN` record are produced on the laptop.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A stock or category not seen in training appears at test time → it is handled as unknown, never causing a failure.
- Gaps in a stock's history inside the lookback → the gap is visible to the model as missing, never stitched across.

### Forecast-driven portfolios (`FPORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Portfolios built from the forecasts are compared out of sample, after costs, with the stage S2 benchmark portfolio. Either they beat it, or the loss is documented.

**Requirements**
- **FPORT-01** Forecast-driven portfolios use the return forecasts with a covariance estimate from stage S2.
  - Given a rebalance date, when the portfolio is built, then its weights use the `FCST` return (and optionally volatility) forecasts with the best `COV` estimator, under the same long-only and 5%-per-stock limits as `PORT`.
- **FPORT-02** Forecast-driven portfolios must beat the stage S2 benchmark out of sample after costs.
  - Given the `HARN` final test period and the 10 basis point cost, when each forecast-driven portfolio's risk-adjusted return after costs is compared with the `PORT` benchmark's, then it passes only if the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill rejects the null of no improvement at 5% after correction for every portfolio variant tried.
- **FPORT-03** A loss is a recorded result.
  - Given a forecast-driven portfolio that fails to beat the benchmark, when the run ends, then the loss is recorded through `HARN` with its results and a written explanation.

### Trading agents (`AGENT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Learning trading and allocation agents are trained by proximal policy optimisation (repeated, clipped policy updates from rewards earned in a backtest environment) and held to the same bar as forecast-driven portfolios: beat the stage S2 benchmark out of sample after costs, or document the loss.

**Requirements**
- **AGENT-01** The backtest environment replays the point-in-time universe day by day.
  - Given a training or test episode, when the agent acts on date D, then it sees only data known by D's close, its action sets portfolio weights for D+1, and the reward is the next day's portfolio return after the 10 basis point cost, less a registered risk penalty.
- **AGENT-02** The environment applies the same rules as every other portfolio.
  - Given an agent's weights, when they are applied, then they are long-only, fully invested (cash allowed), at most 5% per stock, and delisted stocks' final returns are realised.
- **AGENT-03** Agents are trained by proximal policy optimisation.
  - Given a training run, when the policy is updated, then each update is limited by a clipped ratio between the new and old policy, using rewards collected in the backtest environment on the training period only.
- **AGENT-04** Agents must beat the stage S2 benchmark out of sample after costs.
  - Given the `HARN` final test period, when each agent's risk-adjusted return after costs is compared with the `PORT` benchmark's, then it passes only if the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill rejects the null of no improvement at 5% after correction for every agent configuration tried.
- **AGENT-05** Agent results are robust to training randomness.
  - Given an agent configuration, when it is trained with at least 5 different random seeds, then the verdict uses the results across all seeds, never the best one alone.
- **AGENT-06** A loss is a recorded result.
  - Given an agent that fails to beat the benchmark, when the run ends, then the loss is recorded through `HARN` with its results and a written explanation.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Training diverges (rewards collapse or weights break the limits) → the run is recorded as failed with the training curve, not silently restarted.

### Simulated paper trading (`PAPER`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Strategies are run forward on each new daily batch as simulated paper trades, with no broker and no real money, so their behaviour on data that didn't exist when they were built is tracked.

**Requirements**
- **PAPER-01** Paper trading runs the stage S2 benchmark and every stage S4 strategy that passed its bar, forward from a start date.
  - Given a start date, when a new daily batch is accepted, then each paper-traded strategy's orders for the next day are generated from data known at that day's close, and filled at the next day's close with the 10 basis point cost.
- **PAPER-02** Paper positions, trades and results are recorded daily and shown in the app.
  - Given a paper-traded strategy, when the researcher opens it in the app, then its positions, trades, daily return after costs and turnover since start are shown against the benchmark's.
- **PAPER-03** Paper trading never contacts a broker.
  - Given any paper-trading run, when it runs, then no order is sent outside the laptop.
- **PAPER-04** A paper strategy's settings are frozen at its start.
  - Given a paper-traded strategy, when it runs, then it uses the model and settings it started with; any change starts a new paper record.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A daily batch is missing or fails validation → that day is not traded, and the gap is shown in the record.
- No stage S4 strategy passes its bar → only the stage S2 benchmark is paper-traded, and that is noted.

## Data requirements

## Quality requirements
- **QR-01** Everything runs on one laptop, except optional neural network training on rented hardware.
  - A full build, the stage S2 comparison and the stage S3 feature tests each complete on the laptop; only `FCST` and `AGENT` training may use rented hardware.
- **QR-02** The full dataset fits on the laptop.
  - The stored dataset for 1,000 universe stocks over 25 years, with all listings, takes ≤ 20 GB of disk, and loading 25 years of universe returns takes ≤ 30 seconds and ≤ 8 GB of memory.
- **QR-03** An incremental daily update completes in ≤ 10 minutes, cross-check included.
  - Timed on the laptop on a normal trading-day update.
- **QR-04** Data costs nothing beyond the existing subscription and a free second source.
  - The architecture lists every paid service; the only one is the existing subscription (and rented graphics hardware per `QR-05`).
- **QR-05** Rented graphics hardware costs at most a budget set per training run.
  - Each `FCST` or `AGENT` training run states its expected hours and cost before it starts.
- **QR-06** The app is reachable only from the laptop itself.
  - Opening the app from another device on the same network fails.
- **QR-07** Subscription credentials are never shown in full or stored in the project's history.
  - A search of the project's committed files and logs finds no credential.

## Assumptions
*Proposed, not yet confirmed by the user:*
- The universe ranks by median daily dollar volume over the previous 63 trading days (about 3 months), recomputed every trading day.
- History starts 2000-01-03 (about 25 years).
- Walk-forward default: estimate on 252 trading days, rebalance every 21 trading days.
- Per-trade cost: 10 basis points of traded value, one number for all stocks.
- Classical portfolios are long-only, fully invested, with at most 5% per stock.
- Cross-check pass bar: ≥ 99% of compared stock-days agree within 0.5%; every disagreement over 2% is explained.
- Significance level 5% after correction for the number of candidates tried, for every claim.
- Forecast horizons 1, 5 and 21 trading days.
- Paper trading runs the S2 benchmark and every S4 strategy that passed; fills at the next day's close.
- Stage S3 features use only price, volume and corporate-action data (no financial statements).
