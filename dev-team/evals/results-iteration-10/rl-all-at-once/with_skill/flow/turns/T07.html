Read before proposing the shape, and when weighing Grow.

- **Single package** (proof of concept, library, one service, research code): `docs/architecture.md` plus `docs/packages/<pkg>/contract.md`. Components are modules, one subfolder each; decisions live in the one doc. 1-3 pages.

**Requirements**

- **SNAKE-03** Eating food grows the snake by one cell.
  - Given the snake's head moves onto the food, when the step ends, then the snake is 1 cell longer and new food has appeared on a free cell.
- **SNAKE-04** Each step the agent picks one of three moves: turn left, go straight, or turn right.
  - Given any game state, when the agent asks for its allowed moves, then it gets exactly these 3.
- **SNAKE-05** The game ends when the head hits a wall or the snake's own body.
  - Given the head moves into a wall or a body cell, when the step ends, then the game is over and no further moves are accepted.
- **SNAKE-06** The game ends after 200 moves in a row without eating.
  - Given the snake has made 200 moves since it last ate, when the 200th move ends without food, then the game is over.
- **SNAKE-07** A game's score is the number of food pieces eaten.
  - Given a game that ended after eating 7 food, when its score is read, then it is 7.
- **SNAKE-08** The agent observes the food's position and the nearby walls and body relative to its head, never a picture of the grid.
  - Given any game state, when the agent's observation is read, then it holds only positions, directions and distances.
- **SNAKE-09** A game is fully set by its seed.

  - Given the only move toward the food would hit the body, when the player moves, then it picks a safe move instead.

- The snake fills every cell → the game ends with the top score of 141, recorded as a full board.

- **POLICY-02** A reference implementation of the same method trains on the same tank, with the same time budget and matching settings.

- **POLICY-03** The policy learner comes close to the reference on mean survival time (EVAL-04).
  - Given trained policy-learner and reference runs, when both are evaluated, then the EVAL-04 verdict is "pass".

**Edge cases & errors**
- The policy collapses to nearly fixed choices early in training → the run's randomness signal (RUNS-03) shows it, and the run continues unless it diverges.
- The policy learner diverges → as VALUE's divergence edge case.

### Predator & prey world (`PREY`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** The fish tank, with no ball. 6 prey eat food pellets while 1 predator hunts them. Both sides see only nearby things.

**Requirements**
- **PREY-01** The world is the fish tank (TANK-01, TANK-04, TANK-05, TANK-10) with no ball, and with the predator and pellets added.
  - Given a new predator & prey episode, when it starts, then the tank is the same circle with no ball.
- **PREY-02** Each episode has 1 predator and 6 prey at random starting positions, with the predator at least a quarter-radius from every prey.
  - Given a new episode, when it starts, then there is 1 predator and there are 6 prey.
- **PREY-03** 10 food pellets are in the tank at all times. A pellet a prey touches is eaten and reappears at a random spot.
  - Given a prey touches a pellet, when the step ends, then the prey's eaten count rises by 1 and 10 pellets are still in the tank.
- **PREY-04** A prey the predator touches is caught and leaves the tank.

- **PREY-05** The predator and each prey see only the predator, prey, pellets and wall within a quarter of the tank's radius.

- **PREY-06** The predator's score is prey caught. The prey's score is pellets eaten, minus a set penalty for each prey caught. The penalty is a run setting.
  - Given 12 pellets eaten, 2 prey caught and a penalty of 5, when scored, then the prey score is 2 and the predator score is 2.
- **PREY-07** An episode ends when all prey are caught or after 60 seconds of tank time.
  - Given 6 prey caught at 40 seconds, when that step ends, then the episode is over.
- **PREY-08** All prey share one brain, and the predator has its own.
  - Given two prey with identical views, when they act, then they get the same choice (before any randomness).
- **PREY-09** A hand-written predator is available. It heads for the nearest prey it sees. Otherwise it swims straight and turns at the wall.

- A prey touches a pellet and is caught in the same step → it is caught, and the pellet counts as eaten.

### Both sides learning at once (`COEVO`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** The predator and prey learn in the same run, each against the other's current version. Saved versions of both sides then play each other, so I can see the two sides chasing each other in circles.

**Requirements**
- **COEVO-01** In "both at once" mode, the default, the predator and prey both update during the same run, each playing the other's current version.
  - Given a new predator & prey run with no mode set, when it trains, then both sides' learning curves move during the run.
- **COEVO-02** A run saves both sides at least 20 times, at evenly spaced training steps.
  - Given a finished run, when its saved agents are listed, then there are ≥ 20 predators and ≥ 20 prey, paired by step.
- **COEVO-03** A cross-play chart shows every saved predator against every saved prey. Each cell is the catch rate over 20 unseen episodes. It is drawn with the `dataviz` skill.
  - Given 20 saved predators and 20 saved prey, when the chart is made, then it has 20 × 20 cells, each from 20 episodes.
- **COEVO-04** Circling is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill on the cross-play results. It is found only if the newest predator catches recent prey (the latest quarter of saved prey) at a higher rate than older prey, significant at 5%. That means it beats recent prey but loses to older ones.
  - Given the newest predator catching 70% of recent prey and 30% of older prey, significant at 5%, when checked, then the report says "circling found".
- **COEVO-05** At each save point, the latest predator plays the latest prey for 20 unseen episodes. The run reports the swing: the highest catch rate minus the lowest across the last half of training.
  - Given catch rates between 0.2 and 0.8 over the last half, when the swing is read, then it is 0.6.

**Edge cases & errors**
- One side stops scoring entirely (catch rate stuck at 0 or 1) → the run continues and the report flags "one side dominates" with the step it began.

### Training against past opponents (`POOL`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 2
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** Once I've seen the circling, I switch a run to "past opponents". Each side then trains against frozen copies of the other side's earlier versions. That should settle the chase.

**Requirements**
- **POOL-01** In "past opponents" mode, each side trains against frozen past versions of the other side, never only its current version.
  - Given a past-opponents run, when an episode is played, then the opponent is a frozen saved version.
- **POOL-02** A frozen copy of each side joins its pool at a set interval, and each pool holds up to 20 copies. When a pool is full, the oldest copy is dropped.
  - Given a full pool of 20, when a new copy joins, then the pool still holds 20 and the oldest is gone.
- **POOL-03** Each episode's opponent is drawn at random, with equal chance, from the other side's pool.
  - Given a pool of 10 copies over 10,000 episodes, when opponent picks are counted, then each copy was picked about equally often (no copy outside 800–1,200).
- **POOL-04** A past-opponents run can start fresh, or continue from a saved both-at-once run's final versions and saved copies.
  - Given a finished both-at-once run, when a past-opponents run is started from it, then both pools begin with that run's saved versions.
- **POOL-05** Settling is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill. It compares this run's swing (COEVO-05) with a both-at-once run of the same world settings and length. It passes only if the swing is ≤ half the both-at-once run's swing, significant at 5%.
  - Given a both-at-once swing of 0.6 and a past-opponents swing of 0.2, significant at 5%, when checked, then the report says "settled".

**Edge cases & errors**
- The pool is empty at the start of a fresh run → the first copy of each side is saved before the first episode, so there is always an opponent.

- **DATA-01** All experience comes from the worlds themselves, and no outside data is used.
  - Given any run, when its inputs are listed, then they are only the world settings, the seed and the learner settings.
- **DATA-02** Evaluation seeds never overlap training seeds.

## Data
No outside data (DATA-01). Every episode is generated by a world from an integer seed.

- **Seeds (AD-09):** a run's seed drives one `np.random.Generator` and `torch.manual_seed`. Training episodes draw their seeds from `[0, 2³¹)` and every seed used is saved with the run. Evaluation seeds come from `[2³¹, 2³²)`, so they can't overlap. `evaluate` still checks the saved training seeds and refuses on any overlap (DATA-02, EVAL edge case).
- **Run folder (AD-08):** `runs/<world>/<run_id>/` holds `run.json` (spec, code version, start, duration, status), `curve.csv` (one row per finished episode: step, side, score), `signals.csv` (step, side, name, value), `train_seeds.npy`, `agents/<side>/step_<n>.pt` (or `.zip` for a library agent), `eval/<id>.npz`, `verdicts/<name>.json`, and for predator & prey `swing.json` and `crossplay.npz`.
- **Training setup:** a run stops at its step cap or its wall-clock limit, whichever comes first (RUNS-09). A library run copies its hand-written twin's world settings, seed, step cap and time limit (AD-10).

## Cross-cutting concerns
- **Time limit (QR-01, RUNS-09):** `Recorder.should_stop` checks the wall clock after each episode and each update. At the limit the learner saves its current agent and returns, and the run is marked "time limit reached". The default limit is 60 minutes.
- **Reproducibility (RUNS-04, REPLAY-05, SNAKE-09):** CPU only by default, `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)`, a fixed thread count saved in the spec, and no global random state: every component takes a `Generator` or a seed (AD-05, AD-09).
- **Failure states:** a non-finite loss or value raises `core.Diverged`, and the run is marked "diverged" at that step with its curve kept. A run whose `run.json` still says "running" with no live process is shown as "incomplete" (RUNS edge case).
- **Significance claims:** every one (EVAL-03, PREY-12, COEVO-04, POOL-05) goes through `evaluate`, which follows the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill: null and alternative, statistic, 5% level, null distribution. `evaluate` saves the inputs and the skill's result as a `runs.Verdict`. No other module makes a significance claim.
- **Charts:** every chart is drawn by `charts` following the `dataviz` skill, and `replay` reuses `charts.plot_curve` for its curve panel.
- **Speed:** worlds step all their agents at once with NumPy array math, with no per-fish Python loop. Cross-play and evaluation games run in a process pool, because each game is independent given its seed.
- **Testing:** pytest. Worlds and players are tested against the spec's Given/When/Then cases, learners on tiny worlds for a few hundred steps, and `reference` only when the extra is installed.

## Decisions
### AD-01: Python 3.12 with NumPy and PyTorch, one package
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** UR-11: the user writes the learners "myself in NumPy/PyTorch, then compare against a library". VALUE-01 and POLICY-01 ask for learners built from basic array and gradient operations. It is a one-person learning project with one install.
- **Decision:** Python 3.12 and one package, `rllab`. Worlds and players use only NumPy. Learners use PyTorch tensors, autograd, `nn.Linear` and an optimizer. The replay buffer, targets, advantages, clipping and update loop are written by hand. Nothing imports a reinforcement-learning library except `reference`.
- **Alternatives:** NumPy only, with hand-written backprop: it teaches backprop rather than reinforcement learning, and the library comparison would be against a different numeric stack. JAX: capable, but has less laptop tooling and isn't what the user named. Separate packages per world: there is no separate install or deployment to justify them.
- **Consequences:** One dependency stack. The library seam (`reference`) and the worlds can be split out later without changing their contracts.
- **Probe:** none — no external service; the library versions are settled in AD-04.

### AD-02: DQN as the value learner
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `dqn`
- **Context:** UR-05: Snake is a "good first env for DQN-style value learning". VALUE-01–VALUE-04 need a value learner that beats the head-toward-food player by 1.5× and reaches 90% of a library version within the time limit.
- **Decision:** DQN: an MLP from observation to the value of each of the 3 moves, a replay buffer, a target network synced every N steps, ε-greedy exploration with a linear decay, Huber loss, and Adam. Its settings and defaults mirror Stable-Baselines3's DQN, so the library run copies them one-for-one (AD-10). It logs mean value estimate, loss and ε (RUNS-03).
- **Alternatives:** Tabular Q-learning: it would work on Snake's small observation, but it isn't the method the user asked for. Double or dueling DQN: these are steps on the Snake method ladder, which is out of scope. They can become a setting later.
- **Consequences:** DQN has a library twin with the same knobs. Divergence shows up as a non-finite loss or value and is caught at the update (VALUE edge case).
- **Probe:** none — the method is hand-written, with no external dependency.

### AD-03: PPO as the policy learner, one Gaussian policy over turn and speed
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `ppo`, `coevo`
- **Context:** UR-08: the fish tank is "good for policy-gradient / PPO and continuous control". POLICY-01 needs continuous turn and speed. TANK-09 and PREY-08 need one shared policy per side, trained on every member's experience.
- **Decision:** PPO with the clipped objective, generalised advantage estimates, a separate actor and critic MLP, and a diagonal Gaussian over (turn, speed) with a learned log-std. The world clips actions to its limits (TANK-04). Each fish is its own trajectory: a caught fish's trajectory ends there, and the others continue. `ppo.PPOLearner` exposes act, record and update separately, so `coevo` can drive two learners in one loop. It logs policy loss, value loss, entropy, log-std and clip fraction (RUNS-03). Defaults mirror Stable-Baselines3's PPO.
- **Alternatives:** REINFORCE: simpler, but too noisy for a 60-minute budget. SAC: strong on continuous control, but off-policy and not the method the user named.
- **Consequences:** The same learner serves S2 and both sides of S3. Policy collapse is visible in the entropy and log-std signals (POLICY edge case).
- **Probe:** none — the method is hand-written, with no external dependency.

### AD-04: Stable-Baselines3 as the reference, an optional extra behind one module
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `reference`
- **Context:** UR-11: compare against a library. VALUE-01 and POLICY-01: the hand-written learners must run with the library not installed. VALUE-02 and POLICY-02: the library version trains on the same world. Stable-Baselines3 supports DQN on Discrete actions and PPO on Box actions ([RL algorithms](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/algos.html), read 2026-10-18). It requires Python 3.10+ and PyTorch ≥ 2.8 ([installation](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/install.html), read 2026-10-18). With a `seed` given at model creation it is deterministic on one platform, but not across platforms or between CPU and GPU (RL algorithms page, "Reproducibility"). Its vectorised environments reset each sub-environment automatically when that sub-environment is done, returning the last observation in `infos[i]["terminal_observation"]` ([vectorized environments](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/vec_envs.html), read 2026-10-18). The docs say nothing about several agents sharing one episode.
- **Decision:** `rllab[reference]` installs `stable-baselines3` 2.x and `gymnasium`, both pinned in the lockfile. Snake is wrapped as a Gymnasium `Env`. The tank is wrapped as a custom `VecEnv` with one slot per fish, sharing one tank. A caught fish's slot reports done once, with its terminal observation. After that it returns a zero observation flagged as dead with zero reward until the tank episode ends, and then every slot resets together. The adapter logs each finished tank episode through the run's `Recorder`, and a callback copies the library's loss and exploration values into the run's signals. Library agents are saved as `.zip` files and loaded back as a `core.Agent`. import-linter enforces that only `reference` imports the library.
- **Alternatives:** CleanRL: single-file scripts that are good to read, but not an installable answer key with a stable API. RLlib: it handles multi-agent tanks natively, but is far heavier than a laptop project needs. Using PettingZoo and SuperSuit for the shared episode: the same dead-slot idea with two more dependencies, and our own world API would have to change to fit theirs.
- **Consequences:** The library's PPO trains on some dead-slot steps that our PPO doesn't see, so the 90% comparison (POLICY-03) is slightly in our learner's favour on data use. The probe below checks that this doesn't break the library's learning.
- **Probe:** PB-01 — does Stable-Baselines3 PPO learn the 8-fish tank through the shared-episode `VecEnv` (dead slots inert until all reset) within 60 minutes on the laptop CPU, and does a seeded run reproduce its learning curve?

### AD-05: Laptop CPU by default, rented GPU only as a measured fallback
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** UR-15: "Laptop CPU (Apple Silicon)". UR-16: "I can rent a cloud GPU for a few dollars if needed." QR-01: 60 minutes per world on the laptop CPU. QR-02: at most $5 rented per world. RUNS-04 asks for exact reproduction on the laptop. PyTorch doesn't guarantee reproducible results across platforms or between CPU and GPU, even with the same seed ([Reproducibility](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/notes/randomness.html), read 2026-10-18).
- **Decision:** Every run uses `device="cpu"` with deterministic algorithms and a fixed thread count saved in the run spec. Apple's GPU backend (MPS) isn't used. A run may set `device="cuda"` on a rented GPU only after a laptop run of that world has hit its 60-minute limit short of its verdicts. That run is marked as rented, and its cost is noted in its run folder.
- **Alternatives:** MPS on the laptop: its ops are less deterministic, and small networks gain little. Rented GPU by default: it costs money and breaks laptop reproducibility for no gain on networks this small.
- **Consequences:** RUNS-04 holds for every laptop run. A rented run reproduces only on the same kind of machine. For networks this small, more CPU cores often help more than a GPU, so measure before renting.
- **Probe:** none — the hardware is the user's own, and the PyTorch notes settle determinism.

### AD-06: Our own small multi-agent World API
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `core`
- **Context:** Snake has one agent. The tank has 8 fish that leave as they're caught (TANK-06). Predator & prey has two roles (PREY-08). One evaluation, replay and training path should serve all three. AD-01 keeps reinforcement-learning libraries out of the core.
- **Decision:** `core.World` maps agent ids to observations, takes a dict of actions, and returns a `StepResult` saying which agents finished. A `roles` map groups agents under a shared policy, and `frame()` returns shapes for drawing. Players and learners are `core.Agent`s that act for every agent of one role.
- **Alternatives:** Gymnasium in the core: it is single-agent, so the tank and predator & prey would need workarounds in every module. PettingZoo `ParallelEnv`: a close fit, but it puts a library in every world, and AD-04 already handles the library's need with one adapter.
- **Consequences:** The library adapters live in `reference` and nowhere else. Worlds stay plain NumPy and are easy to test against the spec's cases.
- **Probe:** none — no external dependency.

### AD-07: Fixed-length relative observations with presence flags
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `snake`, `tank`, `prey`
- **Context:** SNAKE-08, TANK-05 and PREY-05 ask for positions, directions and distances relative to the agent, never pixels. A fish that sees nothing must say so (TANK edge case). MLPs need a fixed input length.
- **Decision:** Snake: danger distance in each of the 3 move directions, food offset in the head's frame, and heading. Tank and prey: in the agent's frame, its own heading and speed, its distance and bearing to the wall, and then fixed slots for the ball or predator, the K nearest others and (for prey) the K nearest pellets. Each slot holds a presence flag, a relative offset and a distance. An empty slot has its flag at 0 and its values at 0. K defaults to 3 and is a world setting.
- **Alternatives:** Variable-length sets with attention: more general, but a second method to learn. A grid of nearby cells: close to pixels, and larger.
- **Consequences:** Each agent sees only its nearest K others. With 8 fish and a quarter-radius view, that rarely drops anything that matters.
- **Probe:** none — no external dependency.

### AD-08: Runs stored as plain files, one folder per run
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `runs`
- **Context:** RUNS-01–RUNS-07 and EVAL-05: save everything about a run, list runs, compare them, and keep verdicts with the run. One user on one laptop.
- **Decision:** The folder layout in Data, written only by `runs`. Listing scans `runs/`. The code version is the git commit, plus a flag if the tree had uncommitted changes. `run.json` is written at the start with status "running" and the process id, and rewritten at the end.
- **Alternatives:** MLflow: comparison views for free, but a server and a heavy dependency for one user. TensorBoard: good curves, but no place for settings diffs or verdicts.
- **Consequences:** Runs are easy to inspect, copy and delete by hand. Comparison and charts are ours to write, and `charts` already owns that.
- **Probe:** none — no external dependency.

### AD-09: One run seed, separate seed ranges for training and evaluation
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** RUNS-04 (same seed, same curve), SNAKE-09 and REPLAY-05 (a game is set by its seed), EVAL-01 and DATA-02 (evaluation seeds never seen in training).
- **Decision:** The run seed creates one `np.random.Generator` and calls `torch.manual_seed`. Both are passed down, and nothing uses global random state. Training episode seeds come from `[0, 2³¹)` and are saved in `train_seeds.npy`. Evaluation seeds are drawn from `[2³¹, 2³²)` by an evaluation seed that is saved with the report. Every player in one evaluation plays the same list.
- **Alternatives:** Only check for overlap: correct, but it fails late, after training has run.
- **Consequences:** Overlap is impossible by construction, and the explicit check guards against seeds passed in by hand.
- **Probe:** none — no external dependency.

### AD-10: A library run copies its twin's settings, step cap and time limit
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `lab`, `reference`
- **Context:** VALUE-02 and POLICY-02 ask for the same world settings, the same time budget and matching settings. EVAL-04 compares the two runs under the same time budget.
- **Decision:** A library run is started from a finished hand-written run with `--reference-of <run_id>`. It copies that run's world settings, seed, step cap and wall-clock limit, and maps each learner setting to the library's argument of the same meaning. A setting with no library equivalent is listed as unmatched in the run, and the side-by-side settings view shows it.
- **Alternatives:** Match wall-clock time only: the faster implementation gets more experience. Match steps only: this breaks the spec's time-budget wording if one of them runs past 60 minutes.
- **Consequences:** Both runs see at most the same number of steps within the same limit, and any step shortfall is visible in each run's duration and last step.
- **Probe:** none — the mapping follows the library docs cited in AD-04.

### AD-11: Two PPO learners for predator & prey, with pools of saved agents
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `coevo`
- **Context:** COEVO-01: both sides learn at once by default. POOL-01–POOL-04: in past-opponents mode, opponents are drawn from pools of up to 20 frozen copies, dropping the oldest, and a run can start from a finished both-at-once run. COEVO-02 and COEVO-05: at least 20 evenly spaced saves of each side, and a latest-vs-latest match at each save.
- **Decision:** One `ppo.PPOLearner` per side. In both-at-once mode, each rollout collects both sides' experience from the same episodes, then both update. In past-opponents mode, each episode plays one learning side against a frozen copy drawn uniformly from the other side's pool. The sides take turns rollout by rollout. A pool is the list of the other side's saved agents in the run folder, read through `lab.load_agent`'s loaders, so the pool is just the newest 20 saves. Saves happen every `max_steps / 20` steps, or more often if set. At each save, the latest predator plays the latest prey for 20 unseen episodes, and the results are saved to `swing.json`.
- **Alternatives:** Both sides learning against the pool at the same time: harder to reason about, and the spec doesn't ask for it. A separate pool store: it would duplicate the saved agents.
- **Consequences:** POOL-04 is a copy of the parent run's saved agents into the new run's folder. The swing test (POOL-05) needs the episode-level outcomes, which `swing.json` keeps.
- **Probe:** none — no external dependency.

### AD-12: Matplotlib for charts and the replay viewer, re-simulated from seeds
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `charts`, `replay`
- **Context:** REPLAY-01–REPLAY-05: play, pause, step, 0.25×–4× speed, score and step shown, and the learning curve beside the game with a marker. RUNS-08 and COEVO-03: charts drawn with the `dataviz` skill. The replays are for the user only.
- **Decision:** One Matplotlib figure: the world on the left, drawn from `World.frame()`, and the curve on the right from `charts.plot_curve`. Keys: space to pause, → to step one frame, + and − to change speed. A replay is re-simulated live from the saved agent and the seed with `explore=False`, never recorded.
- **Alternatives:** pygame: smoother animation, but the curve panel would be drawn twice in two libraries. A browser viewer: out of scope (no web app).
- **Consequences:** Charts and replays share one library and one style. Very long tank episodes at 4× may drop frames, but the step counter stays exact.
- **Probe:** none — no external dependency.

## Risks and open questions
- **PB-01 (AD-04):** the library's PPO may not learn well through dead fish slots. If it fails, POLICY-02 and POLICY-03 have no fair answer key. Fallback: wrap the tank as 8 independent single-fish tanks for the library run only, and bring that change back for a decision. The hand-off goes to `api-probe`.
- **Tank training speed (QR-01):** PPO on 8 fish × 600 steps per episode must reach its verdicts in 60 minutes in pure NumPy and PyTorch. Mitigation: vectorised world steps, and measure steps per second in the first S2 milestone before tuning anything else. AD-05's rented GPU is the last resort.
- **Cross-play cost (COEVO-03):** 20 × 20 cells × 20 episodes is 8,000 tank episodes. That doesn't count toward QR-01's training limit, but it may take tens of minutes. Mitigation: a process pool, run once per run.
- **Settling test (POOL-05):** each run gives one swing. The significance test must resample the episode-level outcomes saved at each save point. The `null-hypothesis-testing` skill chooses the procedure, and `swing.json` keeps what it needs.
- **Snake's limited view (AD-07):** with only nearby danger visible, long snakes may trap themselves. This is enough for 1.5× the head-toward-food player, but may cap the score. A richer observation would be a later decision.

## Assumptions (choices I made)
- **Interface between worlds and learners:** each world takes moves and returns observations for every agent at once, grouped by role: snake, fish, predator, prey.
- **What the fish and predators see:** a fixed-length list of numbers, relative to the agent. It has slots for the ball or predator, the 3 nearest others, and for prey the 3 nearest pellets. Each slot has an "is anything there" flag, so seeing nothing is explicit.
- **Snake's view:** danger in each of its 3 directions, where the food is relative to its head, and which way it's heading.
- **Default settings:** DQN and PPO start from Stable-Baselines3's own defaults, so matching the library is a straight copy.
- **Run storage:** each run is a plain folder under `runs/`, with no MLflow or TensorBoard.
- **Replays:** a Matplotlib window with the world on the left and the learning curve on the right. Keys: space pauses, → steps one frame, + and − change speed. A replay is re-played from the saved agent and seed, not recorded.
- **Seeds:** training uses seeds in [0, 2³¹) and evaluation uses [2³¹, 2³²), so they can never overlap. There's also an explicit check.
- **Predator & prey training:** one PPO learner per side. The pool of past opponents is just each side's newest 20 saved versions.
- **Placeholder tank numbers:** ball speed 0.5, max turn 0.5 rad per step, a 0.1 s time step, and the predator as fast as the prey. The spec doesn't fix these, so they're world settings you can change.

## Open questions and risks
- **Probe PB-01:** if the library can't learn with the empty fish slots, the fallback is 8 separate one-fish tanks for the library run only. That change would come back to you for a decision.
- **Fish training speed:** it's unclear whether PPO on 8 fish fits in 60 minutes. I'd measure steps per second early in the fish-tank stage.
- **Cross-play cost:** comparing 20 × 20 saved versions is 8,000 episodes, which could take tens of minutes. It runs in parallel and doesn't count toward the 60-minute training limit.
- **"Settled" check:** each run gives a single swing number. The significance test has to resample the per-episode results saved at each save point, and the skill picks the method.
