### Simulated backtest (`BT`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S4
**Source:** [ideas/forecast-portfolios.md](ideas/forecast-portfolios.md)
**Behavior:** Every portfolio the forecast and agent studies compare is formed at each month-end rebalance date (PORT-02), held through the following month, and charged 10 bps per side on the trading its rebalance causes. The backtest is simulated: it places no orders anywhere. For every portfolio it reports the after-cost Sharpe ratio, volatility, maximum drawdown and turnover. The classical portfolios it is compared with are the best portfolio-study portfolio and 1/N.

**Requirements**
- **BT-01** Every portfolio in the forecast and agent studies, the 12 portfolio-study portfolios (4 estimates × 3 rules) and the equal-weight portfolio (PORT-13, "1/N") are held through one simulated backtest: formed at each rebalance date and held through the following month as in PORT-14.
  - Given a portfolio-study portfolio, when it is run through the backtest, then its daily returns before costs equal the portfolio study's for every holding month.
- **BT-02** The backtest places no orders and contacts no broker or trading service.
  - Given a backtest run with no network connection, when it completes, then its results are identical to a run with one.
- **BT-03** At every rebalance date, a portfolio is charged 10 bps (0.10%) times the sum of the absolute changes from its drifted stock weights to its new stock weights, deducted from its return on that date. A portfolio's first formation counts as a change from all cash. An allocation across sleeves is charged on the change in the stock weights it holds through its sleeves.
  - Given drifted weights (0.6, 0.4) and new weights (0.5, 0.5), when the cost is charged, then it is 0.10% × 0.2 = 0.02% of the portfolio's value.
  - Given a portfolio's first formation, when the cost is charged, then it is 0.10%.
  - Given an allocation moving 0.1 from a sleeve holding one stock to cash, when the cost is charged, then it is 0.10% × 0.1 = 0.01%.
- **BT-04** A portfolio's monthly after-cost return compounds its daily returns over the holding month, after the cost charged at the rebalance date that starts it.
  - Given a cost of 0.02% and daily returns of +1% and +1% in a two-day month, when the monthly return is computed, then it is (1 − 0.0002) × 1.01 × 1.01 − 1 = +2.0080% to 4 decimals.
- **BT-05** For every portfolio, the backtest reports, separately for the development months and the holdout (INFO-02): the annualized Sharpe ratio (the mean monthly after-cost return divided by its sample standard deviation, × √12, with a 0% risk-free rate), the annualized volatility (that standard deviation × √12), the maximum drawdown of the after-cost value, and the mean turnover (PORT-17).
  - Given 12 monthly after-cost returns alternating +2% and 0%, when the Sharpe ratio is computed, then it is 3.32 to 2 decimals.
  - Given after-cost monthly returns of +10%, −20% and +12.5%, when the maximum drawdown is computed, then it is 20%.
- **BT-06** The best portfolio-study portfolio is the one of the 12 with the highest after-cost Sharpe ratio on the development months. It is chosen once, and then used unchanged as a comparator and a sleeve.
  - Given a return changed on a day after 2021-09-30, when the choice is repeated, then the same portfolio is chosen.
- **BT-07** Every weight a portfolio or an agent sets at rebalance date R uses no data from after R.
  - Given a price, volume or return changed on a day after R, when the study is run again, then every weight set on R or earlier is identical.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A portfolio with no stocks on a rebalance date (fewer than 2 eligible stocks, PORT-03) → it holds cash at 0% that month, and the report says why.
- A rule that finds no weights on a rebalance date → the failure is reported and the portfolio keeps its drifted weights with no trade that month, so every portfolio has a return every month and the comparisons stay paired.

### Forecasts (`FCST`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 3
**Stage:** S4
**Source:** [ideas/forecast-portfolios.md](ideas/forecast-portfolios.md)
**Behavior:** The researcher runs one command. At each rebalance date, a forecasting model reads each eligible member's daily history and the feature values that passed the feature study on the development months, and forecasts the member's total return and volatility over the following month. The forecasts are scored against simple baselines: a zero forecast and a penalized linear model for returns, and an exponentially weighted variance and a heterogeneous-horizon regression for volatility. The study then builds the forecast-driven portfolios (FPORT) and runs them through the backtest (BT). Its report opens in the app.

**Requirements**
- **FCST-01** One command runs the whole forecast study and writes one report with five parts: forecast scores, forecast profile, backtest results, verdicts and holdout record.
  - Given stored data from 1998-01-02 and a completed feature-study development run, when the command is run, then one report is written that has all five parts.
- **FCST-02** Each eligible member's return target for R is its total return over the month after R, compounded from its daily total returns through its last trading day if it stops trading.
  - Given daily total returns of +1.0%, −0.5% and +0.2% in the month after R, when the target is computed, then it is 1.01 × 0.995 × 1.002 − 1 = +0.696% to 3 decimals.
  - Given a stock whose last trading day is the 10th trading day of the month after R, when its target is computed, then it compounds the returns through day 10, that day included.
- **FCST-03** Each eligible member's volatility target for R is the square root of its realized variance over the month after R, computed as in PORT-15, through its last trading day if it stops trading.
  - Given 20 daily returns alternating +1% and −1% in the month after R, when the target is computed, then it is √0.0252 = 15.87% to 2 decimals.
- **FCST-04** The forecasts for R read only each eligible member's daily total returns and volume over the 252 trading days ending R, in order, and the values on R of the candidate features that passed INFO-07 on the development months.
  - Given a feature-study development run in which 1-month reversal and volatility 21 pass INFO-07, when the forecast inputs are listed, then they are those 2 features plus daily returns and volume.
  - Given a feature that passes INFO-07 on the development months and fails on the holdout, when the forecast inputs are listed, then it is still one of them.
  - Given a company that changed ticker within the 252 days ending R, when its inputs are read, then they form one continuous series under its permanent identifier (UNIV-03).
- **FCST-05** The forecasting model and the fitted baselines are trained walk-forward on the schedule of INFO-05: the first test month is the 61st rebalance date, refits come every 12 rebalance dates, and each refit on R trains only on earlier rebalance dates whose target month ended on or before R. Settings are chosen on development months only, and holdout months are forecast with the frozen settings, refit on the same schedule.
  - Given a refit on rebalance date R, when its training months are listed, then every one is earlier than R and its target month ended on or before R.
- **FCST-06** Return forecasts are scored by the mean monthly rank correlation across stocks with the return targets (as INFO-06), with the out-of-sample R² against a zero forecast, 1 − Σ(target − forecast)² / Σ target², reported next to it.
  - Given forecasts that are all 0, when the R² is computed, then it is 0.
  - Given a month where the forecasts rank the stocks exactly as their targets, when scored, then that month's rank correlation is 1.
- **FCST-07** The return baselines are a zero forecast and a linear model on the same candidate features plus each stock's total return over the last 21 and 252 days, with a penalty on the squared size of its weights whose strength is chosen on the training months at each refit.
  - Given a completed study, when the forecast-scores part is read, then the model and both return baselines show their scores on the development test months and, once scored, the holdout.
- **FCST-08** Volatility forecasts are scored by the mean over stock-months of QLIKE on variances, v / f − ln(v / f) − 1 for realized variance v and forecast variance f (lower is better), with the mean squared error of the variance forecasts reported next to it.
  - Given a forecast equal to the realized variance, when scored, then its QLIKE is 0.
  - Given a forecast variance of 0.02 and a realized variance of 0.04, when scored, then its QLIKE is 2 − ln 2 − 1 = 0.307 to 3 decimals.
- **FCST-09** The first volatility baseline is the exponentially weighted average of the squared daily returns over the 252 days ending R, with decay 0.94 per day and weights normalized to sum to 1, annualized × 252.
  - Given a stock whose daily returns over the 252 days alternate +1% and −1%, when the baseline forecasts, then its volatility is 0.01 × √252 = 15.87% to 2 decimals.
- **FCST-10** The second volatility baseline regresses next-month realized variance on the mean squared daily return over the last 1, 5 and 22 trading days ending R, fitted across all stocks on the training months at each refit.
  - Given synthetic stocks whose next-month realized variance is exactly 0.2 × the 1-day, 0.3 × the 5-day and 0.5 × the 22-day value, when the baseline is fitted, then its coefficients are 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5 within 1e-6.
- **FCST-11** The return forecasts are tested against each return baseline with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, on the paired monthly rank correlations: the null is that the model's score is no higher than the baseline's. It passes only if the null is rejected one-sided at 5% after Holm correction across the 2 return baselines, on the development test months and, once scored, the holdout.
  - Given a completed study, when the forecast-scores part is read, then each return baseline shows the model's corrected p-value against it and whether the null is rejected.
- **FCST-12** The volatility forecasts are tested against each volatility baseline with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, on the paired monthly mean QLIKE: the null is that the model's loss is no lower than the baseline's. It passes only if the null is rejected one-sided at 5% after Holm correction across the 2 volatility baselines, on the development test months and, once scored, the holdout.
  - Given a completed study, when the forecast-scores part is read, then each volatility baseline shows the model's corrected p-value against it and whether the null is rejected.
- **FCST-13** The forecasts are profiled with the `data-analysis` skill on every rebalance date: coverage (the share of eligible members with both forecasts), distribution summaries, and calibration (the mean forecast against the mean realized value within each forecast decile). It passes when the report has all three for both forecasts across all test months.
  - Given a completed study, when the forecast-profile part is read, then the return and volatility forecasts each show coverage by rebalance date, a distribution summary and a 10-decile calibration table.
- **FCST-14** The forecast study's report can be opened in the app.
  - Given a completed study, when the researcher opens it in the app, then its five parts' tables and charts are shown.
- **FCST-15** The full forecast study, tests and holdout scoring included, runs in ≤ 8 hours on the researcher's machine, or on a rented GPU within QR-06.
  - Timed on the full history since 1998-01-02.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Study started with no completed feature-study development run → refused with a message to run the feature study first.
- No candidate feature passed INFO-07 on the development months → the forecasts read daily returns and volume only, and the report says so.
- A stock with no trading day after R → it has no targets for R, is left out of that month's scoring, and the number left out is reported.
- A stock with fewer than 5 trading days in the month after R → it has no volatility target for R and is left out of that month's volatility scoring.
- The model or a baseline fails to fit at a refit → the failure is reported, and the months it would have forecast are left out of every comparison that uses it, so they stay paired.

### Forecast-driven portfolios (`FPORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S4
**Source:** [ideas/forecast-portfolios.md](ideas/forecast-portfolios.md)
**Behavior:** At each rebalance date, three long-only rules turn the forecasts into weights: a mean-variance portfolio, a risk-parity portfolio on forecast volatilities, and an equal-weight portfolio of the top fifth by return forecast. They run through the backtest next to the best portfolio-study portfolio and 1/N. A rule beats the classical portfolios only if its after-cost Sharpe ratio on the holdout is higher than both, beyond chance. "Nothing beats them" is a valid result.

**Requirements**
- **FPORT-01** The forecast covariance for R has each stock's forecast volatility (FCST-03 target) on its diagonal and the correlations of the shrinkage estimate (PORT-06) on R's window off it: each entry is σᵢ × σⱼ × ρᵢⱼ.
  - Given 2 stocks with forecast volatilities 10% and 20% and a shrinkage correlation of 0.5, when the covariance is formed, then its entries are 0.01, 0.04 and 0.01.
- **FPORT-02** The mean-variance rule picks the long-only, fully invested weights w that maximize wᵀμ − (γ / 2) wᵀΣw, with μ the return forecasts, Σ the forecast covariance, and the risk aversion γ chosen on the development months by after-cost Sharpe ratio and then frozen.
  - Given 2 uncorrelated stocks with forecast returns 10% and 6%, forecast variances 0.04 each and γ = 2, when the rule runs, then the weights are 0.75 and 0.25.
- **FPORT-03** The forecast risk-parity rule applies PORT-11 to the forecast covariance.
  - Given 2 uncorrelated stocks with forecast volatilities 10% and 20%, when the rule runs, then the weights are 2/3 and 1/3.
- **FPORT-04** The top-fifth rule gives equal weight to the fifth of eligible stocks with the highest return forecasts, rounded down to a whole number of stocks and at least 1.
  - Given 400 eligible stocks with forecasts, when the rule runs, then the 80 highest each get 1/80 and the rest get 0.
  - Given 7 eligible stocks, when the rule runs, then the 1 highest gets all the weight.
- **FPORT-05** The 3 rules, the best portfolio-study portfolio (BT-06) and 1/N are all run through the backtest, and the backtest-results part reports BT-05's measures for each of the 5.
  - Given a completed study, when the backtest-results part is read, then all 5 portfolios show Sharpe ratio, volatility, maximum drawdown and turnover for the development months and, once scored, the holdout.
- **FPORT-06** Each rule is tested against the best portfolio-study portfolio and against 1/N with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, on the paired monthly after-cost returns: the null is that the rule's Sharpe ratio is no higher than the comparator's. A comparison passes only if the null is rejected one-sided at 5% after Holm correction across the 6 comparisons (3 rules × 2 comparators).
  - Given a completed study, when the verdicts part is read, then each of the 6 comparisons shows the Sharpe ratio difference, the corrected p-value and whether the null is rejected, for the development months and, once scored, the holdout.
- **FPORT-07** Each rule gets one verdict: it beats the classical portfolios only if it passes FPORT-06 against both comparators on the holdout. Until the holdout is scored, the verdict reads "development only".
  - Given a rule that passes against 1/N on the holdout but not against the best portfolio-study portfolio, when the verdict is read, then it does not beat them.
  - Given a study where no rule beats them, when the report is read, then it says so plainly, with every rule's figures still shown.
- **FPORT-08** Every backtested portfolio's monthly after-cost returns, drawdowns and turnover are profiled over time with the `data-analysis` skill. It passes when the backtest-results part has all three, by month, for each of the 5 portfolios.
  - Given a completed study, when the backtest-results part is read, then each portfolio shows its monthly after-cost returns, its drawdown and its turnover over time.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A stock without a return or volatility forecast on R → it is left out of every forecast-driven portfolio formed on R, and the number left out is reported.
- Ties in the return forecasts at the top-fifth cut-off → broken by the permanent identifier, so the same inputs always give the same portfolio.
