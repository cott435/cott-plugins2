# Idea: Predator and prey

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a third environment where prey fish forage for food pellets while a learning predator hunts them, so that the learner can see and measure how each side's learning changes what the other has to learn.

## The problem
Snake and the fish tank each have one learner facing a world that never changes its rules. Once a second learner shares the world, the opponent changes while you learn. Old lessons stop working, the two sides can chase each other in circles, and "it learned" becomes hard to measure. None of this shows up with one learner, so it needs a world built to show it.

## Who it's for
The brief's one learner, once the fish tank is done, studying two learners that adapt to each other (co-evolution) and the self-play fix for the problems that causes.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 1 (each step of the ladder written by hand), Goal 2 (random and scripted baselines for both sides), Goal 3 (the tournament chart explains the arms race), Goal 4 (replays), Goal 5 (the third environment reuses the tank and PPO)
- **Touches:**
  - The S2 tank: circle, ball physics, swim-velocity moves and view (TANK-01–TANK-13), PPO and the shared brain (PG-04, PG-06).
  - The spec's out-of-scope line "a ball that learns to hunt", and the frozen [fish-tank](fish-tank.md) idea's "learning hunter", which this idea takes on as its own lesson.
  - The baselines and comparisons (BASE-*, EVAL-01–EVAL-07).
  - RUN-08 and RUN-09: saved Snake and tank runs still rerun, compare and replay unchanged.
- **Brief changes:** None
- **Stage:** New S3 (roadmap stage on acceptance)

## Recommended direction
**The world.** Same circular tank, swim-velocity moves and PPO as the fish tank. A learning predator fish replaces the ball. Five prey fish eat food pellets, and an eaten pellet appears again somewhere else. The predator touching a prey removes it. Everyone has the same short view radius as the S2 fish.

**Two learners.** The 5 prey share one brain, as in S2. The predator has its own. Prey are rewarded for pellets eaten, and being caught ends that prey's game. The predator is rewarded for catches. The pellets matter: they pull prey toward danger, so prey can't just learn to flee as the S2 fish did.

**The ladder.** Each rung adds one fix for a moving opponent, with a before-and-after comparison:
1. **Both learn at once:** plain co-evolution, expected to chase or cycle.
2. **Take turns:** one side is frozen while the other trains, swapping every N updates.
3. **Train against past versions:** each side trains against a pool of the other side's saved past versions (self-play).

**The control.** One side learns against a scripted opponent, which measures what a moving opponent costs.

**Baselines.** For the predator: a random swimmer and a scripted chaser that swims at the nearest prey it sees. For the prey: a random swimmer and a scripted forager that flees the predator when it sees it, and otherwise swims to the nearest pellet.

**Done.** Two conditions:
- Tested against fixed opponents on held-out tanks, each side beats the random player and beats or ties its scripted bot. An explained tie counts.
- A tournament chart (predator version i against prey version j) shows a measured trend, or an explained cycle.

## Data
- No outside data. Both sides create their own experience in the tank, from seeds.
- Evaluated on fixed held-out test tanks against fixed opponents, never against the co-learning partner alone.
- Each comparison is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill across 5 training seeds. The learning curves and the tournament grid are drawn with `dataviz`.

## Alternatives considered
- Both learn at once only: shows the problem but not the fix.
- Past-versions self-play only: shows the fix without the problem it fixes.
- A new world instead of the tank: the lesson would mix physics changes with the two-learner effects.
- Predator sees further than prey: adds an imbalance to explain before the dynamics.

## What success looks like
- Each side beats random and beats or ties its scripted bot when tested against fixed opponents, with every result explained.
- Each rung and the control has a measured before-and-after comparison.
- The tournament chart shows whether later predators beat earlier prey (and the other way round), or why they cycle.
- Any game can be watched as a replay, predator and prey together.

## What we're betting on
- Two learners together still train in under an hour on the laptop.
- The tank and PPO take a second learner with changes, not a rewrite.
- A balanced predator speed exists, one where neither side wins trivially.
- Lower rungs may fail or cycle. That is part of the lesson, as long as it's explained.

## Not doing
- Two or more predators (cooperative hunting): a different lesson.
- Separate brains per prey: the shared brain keeps the S2 choice.
- Evolutionary or genetic algorithms: "co-evolution" here means two learners adapting to each other.
- Library multi-agent algorithms: written in the project, as before.
- Shaped rewards beyond pellets and catches.

## Open questions
- The control: run it in both directions (learning predator against the scripted forager, and learning prey against the scripted chaser), or the predator only? Running both makes the ladder 3 rungs plus 2 controls = 5 setups × 5 seeds = 25 runs.
- Predator top speed compared with prey (2.5 per second in S2), and whether the predator can only catch prey by cornering them against the wall.
- Pellet count, how fast eaten pellets reappear, the reward sizes, and the time cap.
- Turn length (N) for rung 2, and the pool size and sampling for rung 3.
- How often versions are saved for the tournament chart.
- The replay's view of the predator's move and of the pellets.
