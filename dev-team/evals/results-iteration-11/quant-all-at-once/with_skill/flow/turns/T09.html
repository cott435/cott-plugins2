# Quant Research Platform

**Status:** Accepted · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18

## The problem
Doing quantitative research on US stocks alone is easy to get wrong. Free data quietly drops companies that went bust or left the index, and models quietly use information from the future. Either one makes a strategy look better than it is. Without a trusted base, every later result is suspect.

## Who it's for
One person: the owner, researching US stocks for learning and paper trading, not for clients.

## Goals
1. **Data I can trust.** Daily US stock prices, returns and corporate actions, with index membership as it was on each date, including companies that later left.
2. **No look-ahead, ever.** Every model is fit only on data available at the time, and one shared check catches any step that peeks at the future.
3. **One honest scoreboard.** Every portfolio or strategy is scored out of sample on the same period, with the same trading costs, by after-cost Sharpe ratio (return per unit of risk) and realised variance.
4. **Claims tested against chance.** Every claim that a signal or model helps must pass a significance test before anything is built on it.
5. **See it, don't just compute it.** A browser app to explore the data and results, gaining views as each part lands.

## Constraints
- Research and paper trading only: no real money, no live brokerage.
- Risk before returns: get variance (risk) right before any return prediction.
- Prices come from an existing paid daily-data subscription, cross-checked against a free public source.
- Universe: S&P 500 members as of each date, from 2000 on, delisted companies included.
- Built in stages, each usable on its own.

## What success looks like
- Ten known delisted former S&P 500 members all appear correctly in the data.
- A cleaned-up risk estimate (shrunk or noise-filtered) measurably lowers out-of-sample variance compared with the raw sample estimate.
- Any later forecasting or trading approach beats the classical risk-based portfolios on the shared scoreboard, or is reported as not doing so.

## What we're betting on
- Removing the market-wide moves from returns leaves structure worth studying.
- Daily price and volume data, with no company fundamentals, is enough to find signals worth testing.
- Out-of-sample scoring with honest costs is strict enough to stop fooling ourselves.

## Not doing
- Company fundamentals (earnings, balance sheets) — not a concern now; price and volume features only.
- Real trading or broker connections — paper trading only.
- Intraday data — daily bars are enough for this research.
- Markets outside US stocks.
- Other users, accounts or hosting — a personal tool.

On that data it studies risk structure and classical portfolios, then tests candidate signals, neural forecasts and reinforcement-learning agents against those portfolios on one shared out-of-sample scoreboard. A browser app shows the data and results. Everything is research and paper trading; see the [brief](brief.md) for why.
7	
8	## Users
9	| User | What they need to do |
10	|---|---|
11	| Owner (sole researcher) | Build and refresh the dataset, run each study, compare strategies on the scoreboard, follow a forward paper book, and explore everything in the browser app |
12	
13	## Scope
14	**In scope:** Data ingest & validation, Look-ahead check, Exploratory analysis, Explorer app, Market-factor removal, Covariance estimates, Classical portfolios, Shared scoreboard, Feature discovery, Neural forecasts, Forecast-driven portfolios, Paper book, Reinforcement-learning agents.
15	
16	**Out of scope:**
17	- Company fundamentals (earnings, balance sheets); features use price and volume only.
18	- Real money, real orders and any broker connection.
19	- Intraday data; daily bars only.
20	- Markets outside US stocks, and stocks never in the S&P 500.
21	- Other users, accounts or hosting.
22	- Learned models of any kind in the S2 studies (factor removal, covariance estimates, classical portfolios).
23	
24	## Shared terms
25	- **Walk-forward:** on the first trading day of each month, every model and estimate is refit using only the trailing 252 trading days that ended the previous trading day, then held fixed for that month.
26	- **Portfolio rules:** long-only, fully invested, no stock above 5% weight, rebalanced monthly on the walk-forward dates, holding only stocks that are index members on the rebalance date.
27	- **Trading cost:** 10 basis points (0.10%) of traded value per side, charged on every rebalance's turnover. The same cost applies to every strategy.
28	- **Scoring period:** from the first trading day of 2002 to the latest close.
29	- **Holdout:** the most recent 3 years of the scoring period (1,095 calendar days back from the latest close at the time the holdout is fixed).
30	- **Purge gap:** for any target that looks h trading days ahead, training data stops at least h trading days before the first test date.

34	### Data ingest & validation (`LOAD`)
35	**Serves:** Goal 1
36	**Stage:** S1
37	**Behavior:** The owner runs one command to build the dataset from scratch, and another to add the latest trading day. Each run downloads from the primary source, cleans the data, cross-checks it against a second, independent source, stores it, and writes a validation report. Research code loads the data as it was known on any chosen date.
38	
39	**Requirements**
40	- **LOAD-01** A full build downloads every stock that was an S&P 500 member at any time since 2000-01-03, with its prices, corporate actions and membership dates.
41	  - Given an empty store, when the owner runs the full build, then the store holds data for every stock in the primary source's membership history from 2000-01-03 to the latest close.
42	- **LOAD-02** A daily update adds the latest trading day without rebuilding earlier days.
43	  - Given a store complete to day D-1, when the owner runs the update after day D's close, then day D is added and no stored value for an earlier day changes, unless a corporate action was announced; that case is listed in the run's report.
44	- **LOAD-03** Cleaning removes or flags impossible values instead of filling them silently.
45	  - Given a raw row with a price ≤ 0, a high below the low, or negative volume, when cleaned, then the row is excluded from returns and listed in the validation report with its stock, date and reason.
46	  - Given a missing trading day for a stock that was a member that day, when cleaned, then the day stays missing (never filled with a zero return) and is counted in the report.
47	- **LOAD-04** Returns are total returns: they include dividends and are corrected for splits.
48	  - Given a stock with a 2-for-1 split on date S and no price move, when daily returns are computed, then the return on S is 0%, not -50%.
49	  - Given a stock paying a dividend of 1% of its price on its ex-date, with a close-to-close price fall of 1%, when daily returns are computed, then the return on the ex-date is 0% (± 0.01%).
50	- **LOAD-05** Every stock's closes are cross-checked against a second, independent source.
51	  - Given both sources cover a stock, when validated, then the report gives, per stock, the share of days whose split-adjusted closes agree within 0.5%, and the stock passes only if that share is ≥ 99%.
52	  - Given any day whose closes differ by more than 0.5%, when validated, then that stock, date and both values are listed in the report.
53	- **LOAD-06** The owner's list of 10 delisted former S&P 500 members is checked on every full build.
54	  - Given the list of 10 stocks with their membership dates, when the full build finishes, then each one is present, its membership start and end dates match the list, and it has prices up to its last trading day; any mismatch fails the build's validation.
55	- **LOAD-07** Data is stored once and reloaded without contacting any source.
56	  - Given a completed build, when research code loads data with no network connection, then it gets the same values the build stored.
57	- **LOAD-08** The loader returns the data as it was known on a chosen date.
58	  - Given an as-of date T, when the owner loads the S&P 500 members, prices and returns, then only members on T are returned as current members, and no value dated after T is returned.
59	  - Given a stock that left the index on date E, when loading as of a date before E, then the stock is included; when loading as of a date after E, it is not a current member but its history up to E stays available.
60	
61	**Edge cases & errors**
62	- A source is unreachable or rejects the credentials → the run stops with a message naming the source; the store is left as it was before the run.
63	- The second source lacks a stock (common for delisted names) → that stock is marked "not cross-checked" in the report; it does not fail the build.
64	- A stock changes its ticker → its history is kept as one stock across the change.
65	- A stock leaves and later rejoins the index → both membership periods are kept.
66	- The update is run on a non-trading day → it reports "no new trading day" and changes nothing.

68	### Look-ahead check (`LEAK`)
69	**Serves:** Goal 2
70	**Stage:** S1
71	**Behavior:** Any step in the platform that produces values by date can be run through one shared check. The check runs the step on the full data and again on data cut off at a chosen date, and reports any output on or before that date that changed. Every later step that fits or estimates anything is required to pass it.
72	
73	**Requirements**
74	- **LEAK-01** The check reruns a step on data cut off at a date and confirms that outputs up to that date don't change.
75	  - Given a step and a cut-off date T, when checked, then every output dated on or before T is identical (to 1e-10 relative) whether the step saw data to T or to the latest close.
76	- **LEAK-02** The check reports which outputs moved.
77	  - Given a step that uses one future value, when checked at any T before that value's date, then the check fails and names the first output date and column that changed.
78	- **LEAK-03** Every step that fits, estimates or scores anything by date passes the check at 3 or more cut-off dates spread across the scoring period before it is used in a study.
79	  - Given a study run, when any step it uses has not passed the check at 3 or more cut-off dates, then the study refuses to run and names the step.
80	
81	**Edge cases & errors**
82	- A step whose output has no date (a single summary number) → the check is run on the dated inputs it consumes, and the step must declare the date its output is as of.
83	- A cut-off date that is not a trading day → the previous trading day is used, and the report says so.

123	### Market-factor removal (`FACT`)
124	**Serves:** Goal 2
125	**Stage:** S2
126	**Behavior:** Each month, the platform finds the dominant common movements of member returns over the trailing window and removes them, leaving residual (market-removed) returns. The factors are the principal components of the standardised returns. The owner sees how many components were removed and how much the correlation between stocks fell.
127	
128	**Requirements**
129	- **FACT-01** Factors are the top k principal components of the members' standardised daily returns, fit on the walk-forward window only.
130	  - Given a refit date, when factors are fit, then they use only returns from the trailing 252 trading days, and the step passes the look-ahead check.
131	- **FACT-02** Residual returns are each stock's returns minus their fitted exposure to the k factors, with exposures from the same window.
132	  - Given a month after a refit, when residuals are computed for each day of that month, then they use the exposures fixed at the refit date.
133	- **FACT-03** The number of factors k is chosen from the window's eigenvalues, using only that window.
134	  - Given a refit date, when k is chosen, then k is the number of eigenvalues of the window's correlation matrix above the random-matrix noise edge (defined in COV-03), with at least 1.
135	- **FACT-04** Removing the factors lowers the correlation between stocks.
136	  - Given the scoring period, when residuals are computed, then the mean absolute pairwise correlation of residual returns is reported per year beside that of raw returns, and is lower in every year.
137	- **FACT-05** Stocks with short or broken histories are handled without biasing the panel.
138	  - Given a member with fewer than 200 of the 252 window days, when factors are fit, then it is left out of that refit and listed, and its gaps are never filled with zero returns.
139	
140	**Edge cases & errors**
141	- A stock joins the index mid-month → it gets residuals from the next refit on which it has 200 or more window days.
142	- A refit window shows near-equal top eigenvalues → k is still chosen by the noise edge; the refit is listed as unstable in the report.

144	### Covariance estimates (`COV`)
145	**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 4
146	**Stage:** S2
147	**Behavior:** Each month, the platform builds three covariance estimates for the members from the trailing window: the plain sample estimate, a shrinkage estimate, and a noise-filtered estimate. It compares how well each predicts next month's realised risk. The comparison is studied with the `data-analysis` skill and tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
148	
149	**Requirements**
150	- **COV-01** The sample estimate is the plain sample covariance of the window's daily returns.
151	  - Given a refit date, when estimated, then it equals the sample covariance of the trailing 252 days' returns for the members kept by FACT-05.
152	- **COV-02** The shrinkage estimate pulls the sample estimate toward a structured target, with a shrinkage intensity chosen from the window's data.
153	  - Given a refit date, when estimated, then the intensity is between 0 and 1, is reported, and is chosen using only the window.
154	- **COV-03** The noise-filtered estimate replaces the correlation eigenvalues that fall inside the random-matrix noise band with their average.
155	  - Given a window of T days and N stocks, when estimated, then eigenvalues of the correlation matrix below the upper edge (1 + √(N/T))² of the noise distribution expected for pure noise of that shape are replaced by their average, the trace is kept, and the result is turned back into a covariance with the sample variances.
156	- **COV-04** Every estimate is usable by the optimisers.
157	  - Given any estimate, when checked, then it is symmetric and positive definite (smallest eigenvalue > 0).
158	- **COV-05** The three estimates are compared on how well they predict the next month's realised covariance, using the `data-analysis` skill.
159	  - Given the scoring period, when compared, then the report gives each estimate's monthly error against the next month's realised covariance, its eigenvalue spectrum, and its stability from month to month.
160	- **COV-06** A shrinkage or noise-filtered estimate counts as better only if it lowers out-of-sample variance significantly against the sample estimate.
161	  - Given the minimum-variance portfolios built on each estimate (PORT-01) over the scoring period, when compared with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, then the result states, for shrinkage and for noise-filtered, whether realised variance is lower than with the sample estimate at 5% significance (after correcting for the two comparisons).
162	
163	**Edge cases & errors**
164	- More stocks than window days (N > T) → the sample estimate is singular; it is still reported, and the portfolios that need an invertible estimate use the smallest stable fix (a tiny ridge, reported) for the sample estimate only.

166	### Classical portfolios (`PORT`)
167	**Serves:** Goal 3
168	**Stage:** S2
169	**Behavior:** Each month, the platform builds three classical portfolios on each covariance estimate: minimum variance, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity. That gives nine portfolios, all following the portfolio rules, all scored on the shared scoreboard. None uses return forecasts.
170	
171	**Requirements**
172	- **PORT-01** The minimum-variance portfolio has the lowest predicted variance allowed by the portfolio rules.
173	  - Given an estimate, when optimised, then no other weight vector meeting the portfolio rules has a lower predicted variance (to solver tolerance 1e-8).
174	- **PORT-02** The risk-parity portfolio gives every stock an equal share of predicted portfolio risk, within the portfolio rules.
175	  - Given an estimate, when built without hitting the 5% cap, then each stock's share of predicted risk is within 1% (relative) of 1/N.
176	- **PORT-03** The hierarchical risk-parity portfolio groups stocks by correlation into a tree and splits weight down the tree in inverse proportion to each branch's predicted risk.
177	  - Given an estimate, when built, then the stock order follows the tree's leaves, and at each split the two branches' weights are in inverse proportion to their predicted variances.
178	- **PORT-04** Every portfolio follows the portfolio rules.
179	  - Given any rebalance, when weights are produced, then they are ≥ 0, sum to 1 (± 1e-9), are ≤ 5% each, and cover only members on that date.
180	- **PORT-05** No portfolio uses return forecasts or any learned model.
181	  - Given the inputs to any classical portfolio, when inspected, then they are the covariance estimate and the member list only.
182	
183	**Edge cases & errors**
184	- The optimiser fails to converge → that month falls back to the previous month's weights, renormalised over current members, and is flagged on the scoreboard.
185	- A held stock leaves the index mid-month → it is held to the next rebalance (or sold at its last price if delisted), and the trade is charged the trading cost.

187	### Shared scoreboard (`SCORE`)
188	**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
189	**Stage:** S2
190	**Behavior:** One scoreboard scores every strategy in the project the same way: walk-forward, out of sample, same period, same trading costs. It reports after-cost Sharpe ratio and realised variance, and tests claimed differences against chance. The app shows the scoreboard and each strategy's history.
191	
192	**Requirements**
193	- **SCORE-01** Every strategy is scored on the same dates with the same costs.
194	  - Given two strategies on the scoreboard, when compared, then both cover the same trading days and both are charged the same trading cost.
195	- **SCORE-02** The scoreboard reports after-cost Sharpe ratio for each strategy.
196	  - Given a strategy's daily after-cost returns, when scored, then the annualised Sharpe ratio is the mean over standard deviation times √252, using after-cost returns in excess of zero.
197	- **SCORE-03** The scoreboard reports realised variance for each strategy.
198	  - Given a strategy's daily after-cost returns, when scored, then annualised realised variance (daily variance × 252) is reported for the whole period and per year.
199	- **SCORE-04** The scoreboard reports turnover and total cost paid.
200	  - Given a strategy, when scored, then average monthly turnover and total trading cost paid are reported.
201	- **SCORE-05** Any claim that one strategy beats another is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
202	  - Given two strategies and a metric, when compared, then the result gives the test used, the p-value after correction for the number of comparisons made in that study, and whether it is significant at 5%.
203	- **SCORE-06** "Beats the classical portfolios" has one fixed meaning.
204	  - Given a candidate strategy and the best S2 portfolio by after-cost Sharpe ratio over the same period, when compared, then the candidate beats it only if its after-cost Sharpe ratio is higher at 5% significance (SCORE-05) and its realised variance is ≤ 1.25 × that portfolio's.
205	- **SCORE-07** The holdout is used once.
206	  - Given the holdout, when any S3, S4 or S5 study selects features, tunes or chooses a model, then no holdout day is used; when a final scoreboard run on the holdout is made, it is recorded with its date, and a second run on the same holdout for the same strategy is refused unless the owner marks it as a deliberate rerun, which the scoreboard then shows.
207	- **SCORE-08** The app shows the scoreboard.
208	  - Given scored strategies, when the owner opens the scoreboard view, then it shows each strategy's after-cost Sharpe ratio, realised variance, turnover and cumulative after-cost return chart, filterable by stage.
209	
210	**Edge cases & errors**
211	- A strategy missing days in the period → it is not ranked; the scoreboard lists the missing days.

213	### Feature discovery (`FEAT`)
214	**Serves:** Goal 4
215	**Stage:** S3
216	**Behavior:** The owner builds candidate features from past prices and volume, such as momentum, reversal, volatility and volume changes. Each feature is tested for whether it adds predictive information about future residual returns, using a learned model. Only features that pass a significance test with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill survive to S4.
217	
218	**Requirements**
219	- **FEAT-01** Every candidate feature uses only past prices and volume.
220	  - Given a feature value for stock s on date t, when its inputs are traced, then they are prices and volume of stocks up to and including t only, and the feature passes the look-ahead check.
221	- **FEAT-02** Targets are future residual returns over 1, 5 and 21 trading days.
222	  - Given date t and horizon h, when the target is built, then it is the sum of the stock's residual returns over days t+1 to t+h, and never includes day t's return.
223	- **FEAT-03** A feature's information gain is the improvement in out-of-sample prediction of the target when a learned model is given the feature, compared with the same model without it.
224	  - Given a feature, a horizon and the walk-forward with a purge gap of at least h days, when tested, then the gain is reported per refit and on average.
225	- **FEAT-04** A feature survives only if its information gain passes a test against chance with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
226	  - Given all candidate features and horizons tested, when assessed, then a feature survives only if its gain is significant at 5% after correction for the number of features × horizons tested, against a null where the feature carries no information.
227	- **FEAT-05** Feature studies never use the holdout.
228	  - Given the holdout, when features are built, tested or chosen, then no holdout day is in any training or test set.
229	- **FEAT-06** The app shows each feature's result.
230	  - Given a completed feature study, when the owner opens the feature view, then each feature shows its gain over time, its corrected p-value and whether it survived.
231	
232	**Edge cases & errors**
233	- A feature undefined for a stock on a date (too little history) → that stock-date is left out of that feature's test, never filled with zero; the count left out is reported.
234	- Two features nearly identical (correlation > 0.95) → both are tested, and the pair is listed so a survivor can be read with care.

236	### Neural forecasts (`FCST`)
237	**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
238	**Stage:** S4
239	**Behavior:** Neural network models that read a sequence of past days for each stock forecast its future return and its volatility over 1, 5 and 21 trading days. They use only the features that survived S3. The forecasts are compared with simple baselines on data never trained on.
240	
241	**Requirements**
242	- **FCST-01** Forecast models are neural networks that read a sequence of past days of each stock's inputs.
243	  - Given a stock and an origin date t, when forecast, then the model's input is a window of that stock's surviving features (and past returns) for days up to and including t.
244	- **FCST-02** Forecasts cover future return and future volatility over 1, 5 and 21 trading days.
245	  - Given an origin date t, when forecast, then the model outputs the return and the realised volatility over days t+1 to t+h for each h in 1, 5, 21.
246	- **FCST-03** Models use only features that survived S3.
247	  - Given a forecast model, when its inputs are listed, then every feature on the list is marked as survived in the S3 study.
248	- **FCST-04** Training and testing follow the walk-forward with a purge gap of at least 21 trading days, and never use the holdout until the final run.
249	  - Given any training run, when its data is checked, then the last training target ends before the first test date, and no holdout day is used.
250	- **FCST-05** Return forecasts are compared with simple baselines.
251	  - Given the test periods, when scored, then the forecast's error and rank correlation with realised returns are reported beside a zero-return baseline and a trailing-mean baseline, and the forecast counts as better only if the difference is significant at 5% with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
252	- **FCST-06** Volatility forecasts are compared with simple baselines.
253	  - Given the test periods, when scored, then the forecast's error against realised volatility is reported beside a trailing-volatility baseline and an exponentially weighted volatility baseline, and the forecast counts as better only if the difference is significant at 5% with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
254	- **FCST-07** Training is reproducible.
255	  - Given the same data and the same random seed, when a model is trained twice, then its forecasts match to 1e-6.
256	- **FCST-08** The app shows forecasts against outcomes.
257	  - Given completed forecasts, when the owner opens the forecast view for a stock, then it shows forecast and realised return and volatility per horizon over time.
258	
259	**Edge cases & errors**
260	- A stock delisted before t+h → its target uses returns up to its last trading day (including the delisting return), and it is never dropped from the test because it left.
261	- Training fails to improve on the validation part of a window → that refit uses the baseline forecast, flagged in the report.

263	### Forecast-driven portfolios (`FPORT`)
264	**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
265	**Stage:** S4
266	**Behavior:** Portfolios whose weights come from the S4 forecasts, under the same portfolio rules as the classical portfolios. They are scored on the shared scoreboard against the best S2 portfolio.
267	
268	**Requirements**
269	- **FPORT-01** Portfolio weights use the forecasts made on the rebalance date.
270	  - Given a rebalance date t, when weights are set, then they use only forecasts with origin t and risk estimates from the walk-forward window.
271	- **FPORT-02** Forecast-driven portfolios follow the portfolio rules and trading cost.
272	  - Given any rebalance, when weights are produced, then PORT-04 holds and the trading cost is charged.
273	- **FPORT-03** Each forecast-driven portfolio is judged against the best S2 portfolio on the holdout.
274	  - Given the final holdout run, when compared by SCORE-06, then the scoreboard states "beats" or "does not beat" for each forecast-driven portfolio.
275	
276	**Edge cases & errors**
277	- Forecasts missing for some members on a rebalance date → those members get zero weight that month, and the count is reported.

279	### Paper book (`PAPER`)
280	**Serves:** Goal 3
281	**Stage:** S4
282	**Behavior:** After each daily update, every strategy the owner has added to the paper book records the trades it would make and tracks its simulated after-cost profit and loss from that day forward. No real order is ever sent.
283	
284	**Requirements**
285	- **PAPER-01** The owner adds or removes a strategy from the paper book.
286	  - Given a scored strategy, when the owner adds it, then its paper record starts on the next trading day with a stated starting value of 1,000,000 simulated dollars.
287	- **PAPER-02** After each daily update, each paper strategy records its simulated trades and value.
288	  - Given a daily update for day D, when it completes, then each paper strategy records the trades it would make (on rebalance dates) at day D's close, pays the trading cost, and records its value at D.
289	- **PAPER-03** Paper results are recorded forward only.
290	  - Given a past day's paper record, when any later run happens, then that record never changes.
291	- **PAPER-04** The app shows the paper book.
292	  - Given paper strategies, when the owner opens the paper view, then each shows its value over time, its trades, and its after-cost Sharpe ratio and realised variance since it started.
293	
294	**Edge cases & errors**
295	- A daily update is missed for some days → the next update records the missing days in order; it never skips them.
296	- A paper strategy holds a stock that is delisted → it is sold at its last price on its last trading day, and the cost is charged.

298	### Reinforcement-learning agents (`AGENT`)
299	**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
300	**Stage:** S5
301	**Behavior:** Trading and allocation agents learn by trial and error in a backtest environment built from the historical data. Training uses proximal policy optimisation, which limits how far each update can move the policy away from the previous one. The agents are scored on the shared scoreboard against the best S2 portfolio.
302	
303	**Requirements**
304	- **AGENT-01** The backtest environment replays history one trading day at a time and gives the agent only what was known that day.
305	  - Given day t, when the agent observes, then it sees members, prices, returns and features up to t only, and the environment passes the look-ahead check.
306	- **AGENT-02** The agent's actions are portfolio weights that follow the portfolio rules.
307	  - Given any action, when applied, then the resulting weights satisfy PORT-04 and the trading cost is charged on their turnover.
308	- **AGENT-03** The agent's reward is its after-cost return, with risk counted.
309	  - Given a step, when the reward is computed, then it is the after-cost portfolio return minus a variance penalty, whose weight is fixed before training and reported.
310	- **AGENT-04** Agents are trained with proximal policy optimisation: each policy update is limited by clipping the ratio of new to old action probabilities.
311	  - Given a training update, when it is applied, then the clipping range (default 0.2) is recorded with the run.
312	- **AGENT-05** Agents are trained and tuned on the walk-forward, never on the holdout.
313	  - Given any training or tuning run, when its data is checked, then no holdout day is used.
314	- **AGENT-06** Each agent is judged against the best S2 portfolio on the holdout.

# Quant Research Platform — Architecture

**Status:** Draft · **Shape:** Multi-package · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **Spec:** [spec.md](spec.md)

## Overview
`quant` is the Python research library. Its `ingest` component downloads Sharadar data, cross-checks it against Yahoo Finance, and writes the only copy of the dataset: an append-only Parquet store read through a point-in-time `AsOfView`. On that view, `quant` runs the look-ahead check, the walk-forward, the S2 and S3 studies, the shared scoreboard and the paper book, and writes every result to a results store. `deep` adds the torch-based S4 forecasts and S5 agents, and plugs them into the scoreboard and paper book as `quant.Strategy` objects. `server` is a read-only FastAPI process that serves the stores to `web`, the React app, over HTTP and a WebSocket.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  SH[(Sharadar)] --> quant
  YQ[(Yahoo Finance)] --> quant
  deep -- imports --> quant
  quant -- writes --> ST[(data store + results store)]
  deep -- writes via quant.results --> ST
  server -- reads via quant --> ST
  web -- HTTP / WebSocket --> server
```

## Dependency rules
- `quant` imports nothing internal and never imports torch, `deep`, `server` or any web framework.
- `deep` may import `quant`; `server` may import `quant`; neither imports the other, so `server` installs without torch.
- `web` reaches the backend only through the `server` contract over HTTP and WebSocket, never by reading the stores.
- Only `quant`'s `ingest` component writes the data store. Everything else writes results only through `quant.start_run` (AD-08) and reads data only through `quant.load_asof`.
- Enforced by `import-linter` contracts in CI for the Python packages, and by the generated TypeScript client being `web`'s only network module (AD-02).

## Cross-cutting concerns
- **No look-ahead (Goal 2):** every dated step is a `quant.DatedStep` and passes `quant.check_lookahead`; studies call `quant.require_checked` first (AD-07).
- **Reproducibility (QR-05, FCST-07, AGENT-07):** every run's manifest records settings, seeds, store version and code version; neural and RL training run on CPU with deterministic algorithms (AD-08, AD-12, AD-13).
- **Paper only (QR-01):** no package depends on a broker library or sends orders; reviewed at each milestone, and import-linter forbids known broker SDKs.
- **Costs (QR-02):** the only outside sources are the Sharadar subscription and Yahoo Finance; `ingest` is the only component with network code.
- **Configuration:** one `quant.toml` (store path, credentials env var name, cost, window, holdout); credentials only from the environment (`NASDAQ_DATA_LINK_API_KEY`), never in files.
- **Errors:** a source error stops the run with the source named, and the store stays on its previous version (AD-05).
- **Significance claims (Goal 4):** every claim goes through `quant.compare_paired`, whose procedure the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill sets (AD-09).

## Decisions
### AD-01: Four packages in one uv and pnpm workspace
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** UR-06: "a TypeScript + React browser frontend over a Python backend (HTTP and WebSocket)" puts two runtimes in one project. S4 and S5 (FCST, AGENT) bring torch and Stable-Baselines3, a stack the data, S2 and S3 code and the backend shouldn't carry. The backend runs as its own long-lived process while builds and studies run as commands.
- **Decision:** four packages: `quant` (research library and CLI), `deep` (torch: forecasts and agents), `server` (FastAPI backend), `web` (React app). Python packages share one uv workspace and lockfile; `web` uses pnpm. All are versioned together.
- **Alternatives:** One Python package plus `web` — torch becomes a dependency of the backend and of every S1–S3 test run. Splitting `quant` further (a separate `ingest` or `data` package) — no separate install, runtime or owner; import-linter already keeps research code away from `ingest`.
- **Consequences:** `server` installs without torch. `deep`'s strategies reach the scoreboard and paper book only through `quant.Strategy` (AD-16). Four package docs to keep in step.

### AD-02: Dependency rules enforced in CI
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** Goal 2 and QR-07-style guarantees rest on research code never reaching the network or the store directly, and on `quant` never importing torch (AD-01).
- **Decision:** import-linter contracts for every rule under Dependency rules, including component rules inside `quant` (only `ingest` imports the source clients; no component imports `ingest` except the CLI). In `web`, the generated API client is the only module that calls `fetch` or opens a WebSocket, checked by an ESLint `no-restricted-globals` rule outside it.
- **Alternatives:** Code review only — drift shows up late. Separate repositories — heavier than one person needs.
- **Consequences:** a new package or component adds its row to the import-linter config in the same change.

### AD-03: Sharadar via Nasdaq Data Link as the primary source
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `quant`
- **Context:** UR-03: "from my Sharadar subscription"; UR-24: "I already subscribe to Sharadar for prices." Constrained by DATA-01–DATA-04, LOAD-01, LOAD-06, QR-02 and QR-03. Sharadar's Prices product includes the Stock Prices (SEP), Corporate Actions, Tickers and S&P 500 Constituents tables, covering active and delisted stocks, from December 1997 for prices and January 1998 for constituents (sharadar.com/prices, read 2026-10-18). SEP gives split-adjusted open, high, low, close and volume, plus `closeadj` (splits, dividends, spinoffs) and `closeunadj` (sharadar.com/docs/stocks, read 2026-10-18). TICKERS gives `permaticker`, "a unique and unchanging identifier for a security (share class)", and `isdelisted` (sharadar.com/docs/tickers, read 2026-10-18). ACTIONS covers ticker changes, splits, cash dividends, spinoffs, listings and delistings (sharadar.com/docs/actions, read 2026-10-18). SP500 rows are (date, action, ticker, contraticker) (sharadar.com/docs/sp500, read 2026-10-18); the docs list no action values.
- **Decision:** `quant`'s `ingest` reads SEP, TICKERS, ACTIONS and SP500 through the official `nasdaq-data-link` client, using bulk table export for the full build and date filters for the daily update. Stocks are identified by `permaticker`, never by ticker.
- **Alternatives:** Yahoo Finance as primary — no point-in-time S&P 500 membership and weak delisted coverage (fails DATA-02, DATA-04). Another paid vendor (CRSP, Norgate) — extra cost (fails QR-02).
- **Consequences:** the source sits behind `ingest`'s source module, so a switch touches one module. SP500's action values and its ticker-to-`permaticker` mapping must be learned from data, not docs.
- **Probe:** PB-01 — does the owner's subscription return SP500, ACTIONS and TICKERS as well as SEP; what action values does SP500 use and does it map to `permaticker` through ticker changes and ticker reuse; do the owner's 10 delisted names match (spec open question); how long does a full bulk export take (QR-03)?

### AD-04: Yahoo Finance through yahooquery as the cross-check source
**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `quant`
- **Context:** UR-04: "cross-checked against Yahoo Finance through yahooquery". Constrained by LOAD-05, DATA-05 and QR-02, QR-03. `Ticker(symbols).history(start=, end=, interval="1d")` returns open, high, low, close, volume, `adjclose` and `dividends` indexed by (symbol, date) for many symbols at once (yahooquery historical-data guide, github.com/dpguthrie/yahooquery docs/guide/ticker/historical.md, read 2026-10-18). The docs don't say what an unknown or delisted symbol returns, or what rate limits apply.
- **Decision:** `ingest` cross-checks Sharadar's split-adjusted `close` against yahooquery's `close`, both rebased to the same split basis, in batches of symbols, with Sharadar tickers mapped to Yahoo's form (`BRK.B` → `BRK-B`). A symbol Yahoo doesn't return is "not cross-checked", never a failure.
- **Alternatives:** yfinance — the user named yahooquery; similar coverage. Stooq — free, but thinner US history and not what the user asked for.
- **Consequences:** an unofficial API can change or throttle without notice; the cross-check is a report, so an outage degrades the report, not the build.
- **Probe:** PB-02 — what share of current members and of the 10 delisted names does yahooquery return, what does it return for a delisted symbol, and how long do about 1,100 symbols since 2000 take?

### AD-05: Append-only Parquet store of unadjusted data, versioned per run
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `quant`
- **Context:** LOAD-02 says earlier days never change except for a listed corporate action, but Sharadar's split-adjusted columns are rewritten at every split (AD-03). LOAD-04 needs total returns, LOAD-07 offline reloads, LOAD-08 as-of loads, and the edge case "the store is left as it was before the run". QR-03 and QR-04 bound run times.
- **Decision:** the store keeps the raw source rows as received, then a clean layer of unadjusted OHLCV (`closeunadj`, with open, high and low unadjusted by the same ratio), membership periods, splits and dividends, keyed by (`permaticker`, date). Split-adjusted prices (rebased to each stock's first stored day, so later splits never change them) and total returns are computed in `quant`, not taken from the vendor. Each run writes a new store version into a staging folder and switches the `current` pointer only when validation finishes; a failed run leaves the pointer alone. Parquet holds the data; DuckDB queries it.
- **Alternatives:** Keep Sharadar's adjusted columns — every split rewrites history, breaking LOAD-02. SQLite or Postgres — slower column scans for a 25-year panel, and a server process for one user. Arctic or another time-series database — more moving parts than Parquet plus DuckDB.
- **Consequences:** `quant` owns the adjustment code, tested against LOAD-04's cases. Versions are kept until pruned by hand; a run's manifest names the version it read (AD-08).

### AD-06: Point-in-time access only through `quant.AsOfView`
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** LOAD-08, VIEW-01 and every walk-forward step need data as of a date, and LEAK-01 needs the same step run on data cut at T.
- **Decision:** research code and `server` read data only through `quant.load_asof(T)`, which returns an `AsOfView` holding nothing dated after T and members as of T. `AsOfView.truncate(T2)` gives the cut view the look-ahead check uses. Missing member-days stay missing (NaN), never zero (LOAD-03, FACT-05).
- **Alternatives:** Pass dataframes around — no single place enforces the cut. A bitemporal store with knowledge dates — the spec asks for values dated up to T, not revisions known at T.
- **Consequences:** every dated step takes an `AsOfView`, which makes it checkable by AD-07.

### AD-07: One shared look-ahead check over dated steps
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** LEAK-01–LEAK-03; FACT-01, FEAT-01, AGENT-01 and every later fitting step must pass it.
- **Decision:** a `quant.DatedStep` is a named callable from `AsOfView` to a date-indexed frame. `quant.check_lookahead` runs it on the full view and on `view.truncate(T)` for each cut-off, compares outputs dated ≤ T to 1e-10 relative, and records a pass keyed by step name, step source hash and store version. `quant.require_checked` raises before a study starts if any step it uses lacks passes at 3 or more cut-offs. Every component that fits, estimates or scores by date exposes its steps as `DatedStep`s.
- **Alternatives:** Static review of code for future indexing — misses leaks through library calls. Checking only final outputs — can't name the step that leaked (LEAK-02).
- **Consequences:** a change to a step's code invalidates its passes, so checks rerun; the check costs about four reruns per step.

### AD-08: One results store with run manifests and a holdout ledger
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** QR-05 (rerun matches to 1e-6), SCORE-07 (the holdout is used once and recorded), VIEW-05 (the app shows stored results only), and every study in every stage writes results the app shows.
- **Decision:** every study writes through `quant.start_run`, which creates `store/results/<study>/<run_id>/` with a manifest (settings, seeds, store version, code version, start and end time), Parquet tables and `quant.Claim` rows. Holdout runs are recorded in one ledger; a second holdout run of a strategy is refused unless marked a deliberate rerun. The app reads results only through `quant.read_runs` and `quant.read_table`.
- **Alternatives:** MLflow — a tracking server and UI the app already replaces. Ad hoc files per study — the app and the reproducibility check would need per-study readers.
- **Consequences:** each study's tables and their names are listed in that study's component contract; the store grows with every run.

### AD-09: Significance tests through the null-hypothesis-testing skill
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** system
- **Context:** Goal 4; SCORE-05 and every requirement that asks for significance at 5% after correction (COV-06, FEAT-04, FCST-05, FCST-06, SCORE-06).
- **Decision:** every claim that one thing beats another is made by `quant.compare_paired` (or `quant.correct_family` for a study's set of claims), and the test, statistic, null distribution and correction are chosen and built with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill. Every module that makes a significance claim writes a `quant.Claim` to its run. Real-data characterisation (distributions, spectra, stability) is built with the `data-analysis` skill in the component that reports it.
- **Alternatives:** A fixed t-test everywhere — daily returns are autocorrelated and fat-tailed, so its p-values would be wrong.
- **Consequences:** the correction family is the study, named on each claim.

### AD-10: Return-structure and portfolio numerics
**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Scope:** `quant`
- **Context:** FACT-01–FACT-05, COV-01–COV-04, PORT-01–PORT-05, with RF-01 as the reference for market removal; PORT-01 needs a solver tolerance of 1e-8 under box constraints.
- **Decision:** PCA, residuals and noise filtering are written in numpy and scipy, following RF-01's steps but fit on each walk-forward window only, standardising and regressing on the same window's returns, and leaving out stocks with fewer than 200 window days instead of filling gaps. Shrinkage is scikit-learn's Ledoit–Wolf toward a scaled identity. Minimum variance is a cvxpy quadratic program solved by Clarabel. Risk parity solves the convex log-barrier form with scipy and applies the 5% cap by clipping and re-solving on the rest. Hierarchical risk parity uses scipy's hierarchical clustering on correlation distance, then capped the same way.

# quant — Architecture

**Package:** quant · **Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **System:** [../../architecture.md](../../architecture.md)

## Overview
`ingest` builds or updates the dataset from Sharadar, checks it against Yahoo Finance, and writes a new store version (AD-03–AD-05). Everything else reads it through `store`'s `AsOfView` (AD-06). `leak` checks every dated step (AD-07), and `wf` holds the shared terms: the walk-forward, the holdout, the purge, the portfolio rules and the trading cost. The S2 and S3 studies (`factors`, `cov`, `port`, `features`) run on that, `score` scores every strategy the same way, `paper` runs the forward paper book, and every result goes through `results` (AD-08).

- **Store layout:** `store/data/v<NNNN>/` holds `raw/<table>/` (rows as received), `clean/bars/` (unadjusted OHLCV by `permaticker`, date), `clean/membership.parquet`, `clean/actions.parquet` (splits, dividends, ticker changes, delistings), `report/validation.json`; `store/data/current` names the live version (AD-05). `store/progress/<run_id>.json` holds a running build's progress.

- **Studies:** walk-forward refits monthly on the trailing 252 days from 2002; the holdout is fixed once when S3 starts and stored in `store/results/holdout.json`.

# deep — Architecture

**Package:** deep · **Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **System:** [../../architecture.md](../../architecture.md)

## Overview
`deep` holds everything that needs torch. On a `quant.AsOfView`, `targets` builds forward return and volatility labels and `windows` turns S3's surviving features into per-stock sequences. `forecast` trains the sequence models on the walk-forward and tests them against simple baselines. `fport` turns forecasts into portfolios, and `env` and `agents` train PPO agents (S5, optional extra `deep[rl]`). Both kinds of strategy are registered with `quant` (AD-16) and scored on its scoreboard. Every fitting step is a `quant.DatedStep` and passes `quant.check_lookahead` (AD-07).

## Components
| Component | Responsibility | Covers | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| `targets` | Forward return and realised-volatility targets over 1, 5 and 21 trading days, after RF-03 with its caveats fixed | FCST-02 | `quant` |
| `windows` | Per-stock input windows of surviving features and past returns, after RF-02 with a purge and no zero-filling | FCST-01, FCST-03, FCST-04 | `quant`, `targets` |
| `forecast` | LSTM forecaster, CPU-deterministic training, baselines and their significance tests | FCST-01–FCST-07 | `quant`, `windows` |
| `fport` | Forecast-driven strategies under the portfolio rules | FPORT-01–FPORT-03 | `quant`, `forecast` |
| `env` | Gymnasium backtest environment replaying one trading day at a time | AGENT-01–AGENT-03 | `quant` |
| `agents` | PPO training (Stable-Baselines3), agent strategies, run records | AGENT-04–AGENT-07 | `quant`, `env` |
| `cli` | `deep forecast`, `deep portfolios`, `deep agents`; registers strategies | — | all |

`env` and `agents` import Stable-Baselines3 only through the `deep[rl]` extra; nothing else in `deep` imports them.

## Interfaces
See [contract.md](contract.md).

## Data
- **Inputs:** `quant.load_asof`, `quant.surviving_features`, `quant.build_features`, `quant.residual_returns`, `quant.estimate_cov`, `quant.walk_forward` with the holdout excluded and a 21-day purge (FCST-04, AGENT-05).
- **Outputs:** runs through `quant.start_run`: tables `forecasts` (`deep.ForecastFrame`), `baseline_errors`, `training_log`, and `quant.Claim` rows for FCST-05 and FCST-06; trained weights under the run folder as `model.pt`.
- **Training:** one model per refit, trained on the refit's trailing window minus the purge, with the last 20% of training origins as validation; a refit whose validation loss fails to beat the baseline uses the baseline, flagged.

## Project layout
```
packages/deep/src/deep/<component>/   → one folder per component; __init__ exports its contract
packages/deep/tests/                   → folders only; files are planned in pipelines.md
```

# server — Architecture

**Package:** server · **Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **System:** [../../architecture.md](../../architecture.md)

## Overview
A FastAPI process on 127.0.0.1 (AD-14). `data` routes answer from `quant.load_asof` and `quant.read_validation_report`; `results` routes answer from `quant.read_runs`, `quant.read_table` and `quant.read_paper`; `progress` polls `quant.read_progress` each second and pushes changes over one WebSocket. It never writes a store, never contacts a data source, and never imports `deep` or torch: `deep`'s results arrive as run tables (VIEW-05). Its pydantic models are published as OpenAPI, from which `web`'s client is generated (AD-15).

## Components
| Component | Responsibility | Covers | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| `models` | Pydantic request and response models; the OpenAPI schema | VIEW-01–VIEW-05 | — |
| `data` | Status, members, stock search and history, validation report routes | VIEW-01–VIEW-03, VIEW-05 | `models`, `quant` |
| `progress` | WebSocket that pushes build and update progress | VIEW-04 | `models`, `quant` |
| `results` | Scoreboard, strategy history, feature study, forecasts and paper book routes | SCORE-08, FEAT-06, FCST-08, PAPER-04 | `models`, `quant` |
| `app` | FastAPI app, error handling (no build → `server.ApiError` naming `quant build`), static files for the built `web` bundle | VIEW-05 | all |

## Interfaces
See [contract.md](contract.md).

## Cross-cutting concerns
- **Caching:** one `AsOfView` per store version, reopened when `current` changes.
- **Errors:** every 4xx and 5xx body is a `server.ApiError`.
- **Run:** `quant-server` (uvicorn), serving the API under `/api`, the WebSocket at `/ws/progress`, and the built app at `/`.

## Project layout
```
packages/server/src/server/<component>/   → one folder per component; __init__ exports its contract
packages/server/tests/                     → folders only; files are planned in pipelines.md
```

**Shape: four packages**, because you asked for TypeScript + React over a Python backend, and the neural (S4) and reinforcement-learning (S5) stages bring torch:
- **`quant`** (Python): the dataset, the look-ahead check, the walk-forward, the S2/S3 studies, the scoreboard and the paper book. It imports nothing else in the project.
- **`deep`** (Python, torch): S4 forecasts and forecast-driven portfolios, and S5 PPO agents. It depends on `quant`.
- **`server`** (FastAPI): read-only HTTP and WebSocket access to the stored data, depending on `quant`. It never imports torch.
- **`web`** (React + Vite): talks to the backend only through the server's API.

The cross-package flows are PL-01 to PL-05 in the root doc.

**Decisions needing attention:**
- **AD-03 (Sharadar) and AD-04 (yahooquery) depend on two probes and stay Proposed until they're answered:**
  - PB-01 checks four things: whether your subscription returns the S&P 500, corporate-actions and tickers tables as well as prices; how membership maps to a stable stock ID through ticker changes and reused tickers; whether your 10 delisted names match; and how long a full export takes.
  - PB-02 checks how much of the current and delisted names Yahoo covers, and how long a full download takes.
  - Sharadar's docs don't list the membership table's action values, which is why PB-01 is needed. After approval the next step is `api-probe`.
- **AD-05:** the store keeps Sharadar's *unadjusted* prices and computes split adjustments and total returns itself. Sharadar rewrites its adjusted prices at every split, which would break the rule that a daily update never changes earlier days (LOAD-02).
- **AD-07:** one shared look-ahead check. Passes are recorded per step's code hash, so changing a step's code means rerunning its check.
- **AD-16:** `deep`'s strategies plug into the scoreboard and paper book by registering with `quant`, so `quant` never imports `deep`.

**Size warning:** `quant`'s contract is 282 lines, over the 200-line guideline. That's a warning, not an error. I already moved internal-only commands out of it. The rest are symbols `deep` or `server` really call, and size alone isn't a reason to split the package.

- "a TypeScript + React browser frontend over a Python backend (HTTP and WebSocket)" (UR-06) → AD-01 (four packages), AD-14 "FastAPI… pushed over a WebSocket" and AD-15 "React app on Vite".
- Changes proposed: none. Open: none.

**ASSUMPTIONS**
- Package ids are `quant`, `deep`, `server` and `web`; S5 ships as the optional extra `deep[rl]`.
- Stocks are identified by Sharadar's `permaticker` (a stable per-security ID), never by ticker.
- For COV-02's "structured target", shrinkage uses Ledoit–Wolf toward a scaled identity.
- **Feature test model (FEAT-03):** LightGBM with fixed settings.
- **First forecaster:** an LSTM, not a temporal fusion transformer.
- **Training:** neural and RL training run on CPU, so reruns match to 1e-6.
- **Build progress:** the build writes a progress file, and the server polls it every second. The app never starts a build.
- **Holdout:** the holdout is excluded through `quant.walk_forward`, so `deep` never handles holdout dates itself.
- **Charts:** the app uses ECharts, and its TypeScript types are generated from the server's API schema.
