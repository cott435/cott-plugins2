# Quant Research Platform

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17

## The problem
I want to learn quant research properly and find out whether any trading signal survives honest testing. Most do-it-yourself research fails before any signal is tested, because the data is quietly wrong. Free price data usually lists only companies that exist today, so failed and acquired companies vanish and every backtest looks better than it should. Ticker symbols get reused by unrelated companies, and price adjustments for splits and dividends are hard to check. Without data I can trust and inspect, no later result means anything.

## Who it's for
Me alone: one researcher on one laptop, doing research and paper trading only.

## Goals
1. Research on an honest universe: S&P 500 membership as it was on each past date, including companies that later failed, were acquired or were renamed. Each company keeps one permanent identity whatever its ticker, and how it left the index is recorded.
2. Trust prices and returns: split- and dividend-adjusted series I can check against raw prices at any corporate action.
3. See data problems instead of missing them: gaps, stale prices, suspicious jumps and source disagreements are flagged and easy to inspect, never silently fixed.
4. Reproduce any result: each one names the exact data snapshot it used, and rebuilding from that snapshot gives the same answer.
5. Add or swap a data source without rewriting research code.

## Constraints
- One MacBook Pro (Apple Silicon), local storage, no servers to run.
- Daily bars for US equities, at least 10 years of history, covering the 2008 crisis.
- Data budget: an existing Sharadar subscription, plus free sources or under $50 a month in total.
- Python for everything except the browser app, which is TypeScript and React with typed contracts to the backend.
- No fixed deadline.

## What success looks like
- All ten test companies (BUD, DNA, WB, LEH, BSC, CFC, MER, SGP, WYE, EK) appear with the correct identity, last trading day, index removal date and type of exit.
- Reused tickers (BUD, DNA) are never spliced onto a different company.
- A sample of current members matches a second source, or each difference is flagged.
- Rebuilding from a stored snapshot reproduces it exactly.
- In the browser app I can open any ticker, see its quality flags, and compare raw and adjusted prices around a split, while long download and validation runs show live progress.

## What we're betting on
- My Sharadar plan includes delisted companies, membership history and corporate actions. If it doesn't, the universe source changes first.
- Sharadar's history and quality are good enough for research, once validated rather than trusted.
- Recording how each company exited is enough, even without exact final-day returns.
- A hand-check of ten names plus a sampled cross-check catches the errors that matter.

## Not doing
- Real money or brokerage connections: research and paper trading only.
- Intraday data, fundamentals or live market streaming: daily prices first.
- Companies outside the S&P 500 for now: a smaller universe I can verify beats a bigger one I can't.
- Full two-source reconciliation of every price: the free sources lack the delisted names where the bias lives.
- Deciding what comes after the data stage.
- A database server or scheduler: one laptop, run by hand.

## Open questions
- Does my Sharadar plan include membership history, corporate actions and delisted companies, and how is it accessed? Settle with a probe before the architecture.
- Which free source will cross-check current members? Decided in the architecture.
