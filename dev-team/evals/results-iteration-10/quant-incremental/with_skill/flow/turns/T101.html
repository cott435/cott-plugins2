# Grow check, cutting a stage, scoping a milestone

Read before planning a milestone, before any task is written. Example: `examples-cut.md` (a large stage cut into slices).

Read only `dt status`, the roadmap, the packages' `pipelines.md`, `contract.md` headings and `tests.md`, the spec sections they cover, the skill list and the `milestone_references` entries.

Before planning, check the Grow triggers. `grow_triggers` lists the measurable ones (> 3 pipelines, > 12 modules, package docs > 2,000 lines). Check the others yourself:
- The milestone brings a dependency stack the rest shouldn't carry (torch, CUDA).
- A part runs or deploys on its own (a paper-trading service).

If one fires and no `AD-NN` already records the decision to stay single, **stop** and route to `architecture-design` Grow. Plan after Grow, pipeline-design Grow and build-setup Extend have caught up.

## 2. Cut the stage into milestones

When `stage_to_cut` names a stage, its pipelines are `pipelines_inferred`: the ones covering the stage's spec IDs. When the brief lists every stage, each stage's Source is `brief` and the spec's only Changelog row says IDs `all`, so `dt` can't tell which pipelines belong to which stage and `pipelines_inferred` comes back empty: assign each approved pipeline to the stage whose outcome it delivers, by its Covers.

- **Order by dependency.** Which pipeline needs another's outputs? A consumer needs its provider's *interface*, not its finished build: a boundary the interface task freezes lets both sides build in the same milestone.
- **The first milestone is a walking skeleton:** the thinnest end-to-end path through every package the stage touches, one pipeline per package at spine version, joined across each boundary, so the user can run it end to end.
- **Each later milestone is a vertical slice:** it adds a capability the user can run end to end (categories, transfers, the review screen), thickening the skeleton. Never a horizontal layer ("all of the relay", "every model"), which can't run on its own, and never a leftover of what didn't fit: viewers, reference checks and write-ups go in the milestone that builds what they show, and that milestone's exit holds the stage's quality bar (the snake beats both baselines).

Depends lists only the milestones whose outputs this one needs, never a build order or a note naming another milestone: `dt` reads every `M<n>` in the cell as an edge. Independent tracks stay in one milestone as parallel waves: one milestone is active at a time, so splitting them only serializes them.

# Idea: Return structure and classical portfolios

**Status:** Specced · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)
**Spec:** STRUC-01–STRUC-05, PORT-01–PORT-20, VIEW-08 (changed)

> Record of why, frozen when specced. The spec is current: [spec.md](../spec.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a study of how S&P 500 returns move together, and of which covariance estimate gives the least risky classical portfolios out of sample, so that I settle variance optimization before any return prediction.

## The problem
Every portfolio built later rests on a covariance estimate. With about 500 stocks and 252 days of history, the sample covariance is singular and noisy, and most of what it shows is the market moving everything at once. Shrinkage, random-matrix denoising and factor models all claim to fix this, but I don't know which one actually lowers realized risk on my own point-in-time data, or whether the gain beats chance.

## Who it's for
Me, with Stage 1's trusted as-of members and total returns, before I build anything that predicts returns.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 1 (it consumes the as-of members and returns, and uses nothing published after each rebalance date); Goal 3 (its report opens in the app)
- **Touches:** UNIV-01, UNIV-02, RET-01, RET-04, RET-06 (inputs); EDA-01, EDA-03 (the same report pattern); VIEW-08 and Out of scope (research views, backtests)
- **Brief changes:** Constraints (two stages decided); Not doing narrowed (return signals, return-prediction models and strategy backtests; the app also opens reports)
- **Stage:** New S2 (roadmap stage on acceptance)

## Recommended direction
One command runs the whole study and writes a report that the existing app opens, the way the EDA report does. The report has three parts.

**Structure.** On rolling 252-day windows since 1998: the eigenvalue spectrum of the members' correlation matrix against the random-matrix (Marchenko–Pastur) edge, the market factor's share of variance over time, and the mean absolute correlation left once the top components are removed.

**League table.** At each monthly rebalance, four covariance estimates are fitted on the previous 252 days of the stocks that are members that day: sample, shrinkage, Marchenko–Pastur-denoised correlation, and a factor model built from the top principal components plus residual variances. Each feeds three portfolio rules: minimum variance, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity, all long-only and fully invested, with no costs. A 1/N portfolio is the floor. Each portfolio's realized variance over the following month is recorded, and the report ranks the estimators for each rule. Turnover is reported alongside, as a number, without being part of the ranking.

**Significance.** The winner for each rule is tested against the sample estimate for a lower out-of-sample realized variance, beyond chance.

Removing the market factor serves both the structure part and the factor-model estimator. RF-01 is prior art for it, refitted per window with no zero-filling.

## Data
Stage 1's store: as-of S&P 500 members and daily total returns from 1998, about 330 monthly rebalances. Everything is built and tested on synthetic data first, and the real comparison runs once PB-01 is answered and the Stage 1 sources are real. The structure work is done with the `data-analysis` skill. The comparison of the winner against the sample estimate is done with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill. Baselines: the sample covariance and 1/N.

## Alternatives considered
- Structure only, no portfolios — it shows whether there's noise to remove, but not whether removing it lowers risk.
- Minimum variance with sample against denoised only — faster, but leaves out two of the three rules I want compared.
- Tuning each estimator's settings in a nested walk-forward — extra leakage risk and cost before the plain comparison exists.
- Separate results for 2000–02, 2008–09 and 2020 — useful later, once the overall ranking is known.

## What success looks like
- One report ranks the four estimators by out-of-sample realized variance for each of the three rules, with 1/N alongside and turnover reported.
- The test says whether each rule's winner beats the sample estimate.
- The structure part shows the top eigenvalues standing clear of the random-matrix edge, and residual correlation well below raw.
- No estimate uses a return from after its rebalance date.

## What we're betting on
- 252 days of roughly 500 members is enough for the estimators to differ measurably.
- About 330 months of out-of-sample results is enough for a difference to show up as significant.
- Stage 1's as-of members and returns, including delisted names, are trustworthy enough to rest the study on.

## Not doing
- Expected-return inputs, return forecasts or machine learning — that's the point of the order: variance first.
- Shorting, leverage or transaction costs — the rules are long-only, fully invested and cost-free.
- Tuning estimator settings in a nested walk-forward — the plain comparison comes first.
- Interactive research views — the app opens the report; it doesn't become a research tool.
