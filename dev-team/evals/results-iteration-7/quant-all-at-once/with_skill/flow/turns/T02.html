# Quant Research Platform

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17

## The problem
Stock market research done in one-off scripts fools its author easily. Common causes are using information that wasn't known at the time, quietly dropping companies that failed, and calling a lucky result a discovery. Each of these makes a strategy look better than it is. I want to research US stocks step by step, from risk structure to forecasting and trading agents, and trust each result before building the next step on it.

## Who it's for
Me: one researcher working on a laptop, exploring and testing ideas for my own learning and paper trading.

## Goals
1. Trust every result. No decision uses information from after its date, companies that left the index stay in the history, and no claim counts until it beats chance in a proper test.
2. Judge every method against a simple baseline, on the same unseen data and with the same trading costs. A more complex method has to beat every simpler one above it on the ladder.
3. Get risk right before predicting returns. Know which parts of the correlation between stocks are real and which are noise.
4. See the data and results. Explore the dataset in a browser and watch long download and checking runs as they happen.

## Constraints
- Data costs nothing, or less than $50 a month.
- Daily prices for large US companies: roughly the S&P 500 as it was on each date, including former members.
- A MacBook Pro with no NVIDIA graphics card, plus a cloud GPU rented now and then for a few dollars.
- One person, part time.
- Paper only: forward testing and simulated orders, with no real money and no brokerage connection.
- The browser app is TypeScript and React, talking to a Python backend.

## What success looks like
- A dataset whose index membership on any past date can be checked against public records, with failed companies included.
- A cleaned-up risk estimate that gives lower out-of-sample variance than the plain historical one.
- Every feature kept for forecasting has passed a test against chance, with correction for testing many features.
- Forecast-driven portfolios and trading agents are judged on unseen data, after trading costs, on both Sharpe ratio and variance against the classical portfolios. They count only if they win on both.

## What we're betting on
- Free or cheap data can give historical index membership and prices for companies that were later removed. This is the dealbreaker: without it, Goal 1 fails.
- Daily data and a large-company universe are rich enough to find structure worth studying.
- A laptop plus cheap rented GPU time is enough compute.

## Not doing
- Real money or a live brokerage connection: research and paper trading only.
- Intraday data, options, futures, crypto or non-US markets: daily US stocks are enough.
- Small companies or the full US market: data for them costs more and is messier.
- Paid company financials or alternative data: over budget. Features come from prices and volume.
- Building the next stage on a failed one: if no feature survives its test, forecasting waits.
- A hosted or multi-user app: it runs locally, for me.

## Open questions
- Does a source within budget really cover former index members with clean price history? This gets checked first, when sources are chosen.
