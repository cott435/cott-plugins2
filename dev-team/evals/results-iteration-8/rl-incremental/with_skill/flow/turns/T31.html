The bar extension is approved and committed as `a8e4fb9`, and `dt base` set M1's base to that commit (the Base line is committed as `54896e2`). Build-setup reports everything current. **S1 and S2 are fully planned.**

Now Environment 3. A brief exists, so this is another idea file. This turn is understanding and widening the idea only; nothing gets written yet.

## In terms of the project

This adds **a third environment, predator-prey foraging**, so that **you** can learn **co-evolution: two sides learning against each other at once**, where each side's world keeps changing because the other side is learning.

**What it touches, honestly:**
- **The brief's Not Doing line** now says "shared pieces cover Snake and the tank, nothing more". A third environment means changing it again, the line you narrowed earlier today.
- **The shared core.** AD-14 assumes one agent (one brain) per environment, acting on a batch. Predator and prey are two different learners in one environment, which is the biggest core change since batching.
- **Bars (Goal 3).** "Beats the random and scripted bot" needs a version for *both* sides. It also needs an answer to "beats it compared to *when*?", because opponents change.
- **The package split.** AD-13 says to revisit at a third environment.
- **The tank.** It could be reused heavily: the tank's physics, PPO, the shared brain for prey, and the nearby view.

**One honest flag:** none of S1 or S2 is built yet. Designing a third environment now means designing the core change for two learners before you've seen one PPO curve. I'd write and accept the idea file now and hold its spec until M3, where PPO first runs, is done. It's your call, as it was for S2.

## How might we…

**…make non-stationary learning visible and explainable one step at a time, so that when predator and prey stop improving or start cycling, you can tell whether it's a bug, a bad setting, or the co-evolution itself?**

## Questions

1. **Co-evolution or self-play?** These are different ideas.
   - Co-evolution: two different species learn against each other.
   - Self-play: one policy learns against copies of itself or its past selves.

   My guess: co-evolution of predator and prey, with "play against past selves" as the fix you add once naive training cycles.
2. **How is each side judged?** My guess:
   - Each side must beat a random opponent and a scripted one (a predator that chases the nearest prey; prey that flee the predator and grab nearby pellets).
   - Plus a **cross-play table**: each saved predator snapshot against each saved prey snapshot. That's where cycling becomes visible.
3. **Scale:** 1 predator against 5 prey sharing one brain (reusing T4), and pellets that respawn? Or several predators?
4. **Rewards:**
   - prey: +1 per pellet, and removed when caught;
   - predator: +1 per catch;
   - an episode limit.

   Is a small cost for the predator's time OK, so it doesn't learn to wait?
5. **Timing:** write the idea now and spec it after M3 (my recommendation), or plan it all the way to milestones now, like S2?

## Variations to react to

1. **As stated:** both sides learn PPO at once from the start. This is the most direct, and the hardest to read when it fails.
2. **The co-evolution ladder (my recommendation):**

   | Rung | What learns | Against | New idea |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | P1 | prey | a scripted predator | foraging plus fleeing |
   | P2 | predator | frozen P1 prey | hunting |
   | P3 | both, taking turns (freeze one, train the other, swap) | each other | controlled non-stationarity |
   | P4 | both at once | each other | full co-evolution |
   | P5 | both | a pool of past snapshots | the fix for cycling and forgetting |
3. **Symmetric self-play instead:** one species plays tag against itself. There's only one learner, which is simpler, but it loses the predator/prey asymmetry.
4. **Cross-play as the experiment:** the snapshot-vs-snapshot table is this environment's "compact vs raw" result. It shows cycling, forgetting, and whether P5 fixes them.
5. **Reuse the tank:** the predator replaces the ball and pellets are added. This needs almost no new physics, and S2's view and shared-brain work carries straight over.
6. **Foraging-only warm-up:** pellets and no predator. It's probably unnecessary, since P1's scripted predator already isolates foraging.
7. **Pack hunting**, where several predators cooperate. This is a later environment's idea (cooperation), not this one.

**My early view:** variation 2 as the backbone, built on variation 5's reuse of the tank, with variation 4 as the closing experiment. I'd leave out 1, 3, 6 and 7.

Answer whatever you can, especially Q1 and Q5, and I'll narrow to 2–3 directions and run the fit check.
