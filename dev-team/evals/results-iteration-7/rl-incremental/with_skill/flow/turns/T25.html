## Summary
A small lab where hand-built reinforcement-learning agents train on small 2D environments that share one core: an environment interface (one or many agents, discrete or continuous actions), a training loop, logging, evaluation and a replay viewer. Snake teaches value learning: a lookup-table Q-learning agent, then a neural-network Q-learning agent (DQN), over three levels. A round fish tank teaches policy gradients and continuous control: REINFORCE, then PPO, over four levels from one fish with compass headings to a school dodging a chasing ball. Every agent is judged against random and scripted baselines and taken apart by ablation experiments. A toy corridor sanity-checks every agent. Why: see the [brief](brief.md) and [ideas/fish-tank.md](ideas/fish-tank.md).

## Users
| User | What they need to do |
|---|---|

## Scope
**In scope:** Environment interface (`CORE`), Snake environment (`SNAKE`), Toy corridor environment (`CORR`), Fish tank environment (`TANK`), Baseline agents (`BASE`), Tabular Q-learning agent (`TAB`), DQN agent (`DQN`), Policy-gradient agents (`PG`), Training & logging (`TRAIN`), Evaluation & comparison (`EVAL`), Ablation experiments (`ABL`), Replay viewer (`VIEW`).

**Out of scope:**
- Agents from established reinforcement-learning libraries — comparison comes after the hand-built agents work.
- Policy gradients in Snake, and Rainbow-style add-ons to DQN — one concept per environment.
- Observations from raw pixels or the whole environment — compact features or local rays only.
- Hyperparameter-search tooling, dashboards and experiment-tracking services.
- Packaging, documentation sites, leaderboards or polish for other users.
- GPU training and parallel or distributed training.
- A reward-shaping ablation.
- Environments beyond Snake, the toy corridor and the fish tank.
- A ball that learns to hunt — multi-agent competition is its own concept.
- Fish that cooperate, communicate or share a team reward — the school only shares one policy.
- Tank physics beyond simple bounces and wall sliding (no currents, inertia or fish collisions).

## Features

  - Given the ball seen straight ahead on T2, when the flee fish acts, then it takes full turn and full throttle.
  - Given the ball seen straight behind, then it takes no turn and full throttle.
  - Given the ball seen on ray 2 on T1, then the flee fish takes action 6.

### Evaluation & comparison (`EVAL`)
**Serves:** Goal 2, Goal 3
**Behavior:** Learned agents and baselines are scored by the same fixed procedure, compared with a proper significance test, and shown as learning curves and a results table.

**Requirements**
- **EVAL-01** Evaluating an agent plays 100 greedy episodes on a fixed set of evaluation seeds, separate from all training seeds, and reports mean, median and maximum score, mean length, and a breakdown of end causes.
  - Given any two agents evaluated on level L3, then both played the same 100 evaluation seeds, and none of those seeds was used in training.
- **EVAL-02** Comparing an agent with a baseline or a variant uses the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill. It is given each training seed's mean evaluation score for the agent (5 values) and the matching evaluation scores for the other side. Its null hypothesis is "no difference in mean score". A comparison passes only if the null is rejected at the 5% level after correction across all comparisons in the same report, and the agent's mean score is higher.
  - Given the DQN-vs-random and DQN-vs-greedy comparisons on L3, then the report shows each one's test result, corrected significance, and pass or fail.
- **EVAL-03** A learning-curve plot shows evaluation score against training steps, with one faint line per seed, a bold mean line, and the random and greedy baselines' scores as horizontal reference lines. It is made following the `dataviz` skill.
  - Given 5 finished L3 DQN runs, when the plot is made, then it shows 5 seed lines, 1 mean line, and 2 labeled baseline lines.
- **EVAL-04** A results table for each level lists every agent and baseline with its mean score ± standard deviation across seeds and each comparison's pass or fail.
  - Given evaluations on L3, then the table lists random, greedy and DQN, with DQN's two comparison results.

- **VIEW-07** The viewer plays tank episodes with the same controls, showing how many fish are still in play; the panel of VIEW-03 follows one selected fish, and its whisker rays can be shown or hidden.
  - Given a recorded T3 episode, when fish 2 is selected and rays are shown, then fish 2's 8 rays and its policy readout are drawn at every step.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A recorded episode file is missing or corrupt → an error naming the file; the viewer doesn't crash.
- A non-learning agent (random, greedy or flee) → the panel shows "n/a".

## Assumptions
- Actions are relative to the snake's heading (straight, left, right).
- The agent sees 11 yes/no features, not the full grid.
- Rewards are +1 for food, −1 for death and 0 otherwise; reward shaping is off by default.
- An episode ends on death, on a win, or after as many steps without food as the grid has cells.
- Food never spawns on the snake; the same seed gives the same game.
- Evaluation uses greedy play, with no exploration; for policy agents, the most likely or mean action.
- The tank's sizes and speeds are in tank radii per step; the fish is slower than the ball, so surviving takes anticipation, not just speed.
- A fish's reward is its own: +0.01 per step survived, −1 when caught.
- Each run stores its outputs separately; runs never overwrite each other.
- The system produces plots, tables and ablation comparisons; the written explanation of why the agent works is the learner's own, outside the system.

I've written the spec update as a draft. Lint and the hygiene check are clean, and the architecture now flags 26 new and 5 changed requirements, as expected.

**New features**
- **`FORAGE-01`–`FORAGE-12`, the foraging tank:**
  - **Bodies:** 3 prey (radius 0.03, top speed 0.015) and 1 predator (radius 0.04, top speed 0.02). They swim the same way and slide along the wall the same way as in the tank.
  - **Pellets:** 10 pellets. A prey within 0.05 eats one for +1, and it respawns at least 0.2 from every fish. The predator can't eat them.
  - **Catches:** a prey within 0.07 of the predator is caught: −1 for the prey, +1 for the predator. The predator also pays −0.01 every step.
  - **Ending:** an episode ends when all prey are caught ("cleared") or at 1,000 steps ("time").
  - **What each fish sees:** 50 numbers. That's 8 rays, each with distance, kind (wall, pellet, prey or predator) and closing speed; plus own speed and a predator flag.
  - **Teams and scoring:** rows 0–2 are labelled "prey" and row 3 "predator". The team scores are mean pellets per prey and catches by the predator.
  - **Starts:** prey begin at least 0.5 from the predator, and a seed always repeats exactly.
  - **Stretch:** the predator's speed can be set for a balance sweep that's reported, not gated.
- **`SELF-01`–`SELF-06`, self-play training:**
  - a two-team agent routes each team's rows to its own policy: learning PPO, a scripted baseline, or a frozen snapshot;
  - four schedules: **scripted**, **simultaneous**, **alternating** (10 updates per side, prey first) and **pool** (each episode, the opponent is drawn from up to the 20 newest snapshots plus the current policy);
  - snapshots are saved every 50,000 steps.
- **`XPLAY-01`–`XPLAY-06`, co-evolution measurement:**
  - **Reference curves:** every snapshot scored against the fixed scripted opponent.
  - **Cross-play:** a matrix of every prey snapshot against every predator snapshot (20 episodes per cell), drawn as a heatmap.
  - **Forgetting score:** on average, how far the final snapshot falls below the best earlier one against each opponent. Hand-checked example: [5, 3] vs [4, 4] gives 0.5.
  - **Bars** (`null-hypothesis-testing`, 5 seeds):
    - the pool schedule shows **lower prey forgetting** than simultaneous self-play;
    - the final pool-trained predator **beats the chaser** at catching flee prey;
    - the final pool-trained prey **beat flee prey** at eating pellets against the chaser.

**New requirements in existing features**
- **`BASE-06`, the scripted chaser predator:** it turns at full rate toward the nearest prey it sees.
- **`EVAL-05`, team evaluation:** score one named side against a named fixed opponent.

**Changed in place:**
- **`BASE-05`:** a foraging prey flees the predator, otherwise heads for the nearest pellet it sees.
- **`TRAIN-03`:** the training log records each team's score.
- **`VIEW-07`:** the viewer also plays foraging episodes, with pellets and team colours.
- **`QR-01`:** each self-play schedule gets the 15-minute budget.
- **`QR-03`:** the foraging tank must run at 2,000 steps per second or more.

**Scope:** "a ball that learns to hunt" is now out of scope only for the T1–T4 tank. Newly out of scope: several predators, populations, league-style training, and different bodies per side.

**The core is unchanged,** so **M1 is still ready for `/build`.**

**Open questions:** none. All the sizes above are my proposals; tweak any you like.
