There is also a synthetic one-factor demo under `__main__`.
- **In → Out:** In: a `pd.DataFrame` of simple daily returns, T dates x N tickers. Out: residuals (T x N), loadings (N x k), betas (N x k), factors (T x k), explained (array of length k), and a scalar giving the mean absolute off-diagonal correlation.
- **Use for:** The PCA market-removal step of the proposed return-structure stage. It also gives a sanity test: mean absolute residual correlation should drop sharply compared with raw returns, and PC1 should explain a large share of variance.
- **Caveats:**
  - **Look-ahead:** the whole panel is used to fit the demeaning, standardisation, eigenvectors and betas, including the last row. The docstring admits this. It is not usable as is for out-of-sample work and needs a rolling or expanding fit.
  - **Silent data handling:** columns with fewer than 60 observations are dropped silently (`min_obs` is hard-coded at 60). Remaining NaNs are filled with a 0 return, so delisted or not-yet-listed names get fake flat days, which biases correlations and loadings.
  - **Zero-variance columns:** a column with zero variance becomes all zeros after `standardise`. `np.corrcoef` then gives NaN for it and `eigh` breaks or returns garbage. There is no guard or error handling.
  - **Scale mismatch:** factors are projections of the standardised returns, but `residualize` regresses the raw demeaned returns on them. The betas are therefore in mixed units. The residuals are fine; the betas are not comparable to market betas.
  - **Redundancy:** the data is demeaned and then fitted again with an intercept, which is redundant but harmless.
  - **Sign convention:** loadings are sign-fixed by their column sum. That is arbitrary for k > 1.
  - **Not included:** no Marchenko–Pastur or denoising logic, so no q or bound issues apply here.
