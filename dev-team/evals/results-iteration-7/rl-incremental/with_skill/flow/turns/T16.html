# Idea: Fish tank (policy gradients)

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds a second environment, a round tank where fish learn to dodge a bouncing ball, so that I can learn policy-gradient methods and continuous control by building them, on the same shared core as Snake.

## The problem
Snake teaches value learning: estimate how good each of a few moves is, then pick the best. Many problems don't have a few moves. They have a steering angle and a speed. Value learning doesn't stretch to that naturally; policy gradients do, and PPO is the method most people actually use. I know neither yet. Reading about them hasn't stuck, for the same reason Q-learning didn't.

## Who it's for
Me, the same learner as the brief, once Snake works. I'll know DQN, the core and the honest-comparison routine, and I'm ready for the next concept.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 1 (explain why it works), Goal 2 (honest baselines), Goal 3 (see learning happen), Goal 4 (reuse the shared core)
- **Touches:** the shared core, which assumes integer actions, one agent, one reward and a grid-shaped frame (CORE-01–CORE-07; AD-02, AD-10). The training loop, evaluation, run storage, comparison and viewer requirements (TRAIN, EVAL, VIEW) carry over. Snake and its tests (SNAKE, TC-01–TC-29) must keep passing unchanged.
- **Brief changes:** None. It's the next stage of the same project, and the brief rules out policy gradients only in Snake.
- **Stage:** New S2 on acceptance. The core generalization lands in S1 before M1 is built, because M1 hasn't started.

## Recommended direction
**The tank.** A circular tank with one ball bouncing off the wall at constant speed. Fish swim with continuous steering and speed. A fish touched by the ball is out. An episode ends when every fish is out, or at a time limit. Each fish earns a small reward for every step it survives, plus a penalty when it's caught. Fish pass through each other, and each fish's reward is its own. That keeps it a dodging problem, not a cooperation problem.

**What a fish sees.** A fixed ring of 8 "whisker" rays. Each reports the distance to the nearest wall, ball or other fish within a set radius, and which kind it hit. Beyond the radius, a fish sees nothing. The observation is a fixed size, and "only sees nearby things" is literal.

**One concept at a time, as a curriculum:**
1. One fish, 8 fixed compass headings: discrete policy gradients. REINFORCE first, then PPO, to see what PPO's clipping and value baseline buy over the raw gradient.
2. One fish, continuous steering and speed: PPO with a Gaussian policy. This is the stage's main concept.
3. A school of several fish sharing one policy network, each acting on its own observation.
4. Stretch: a ball that chases the nearest fish instead of bouncing.

**The shared core is generalized now.** Before M1 is built, the core interface changes:
- actions become arrays;
- every step returns one observation and one reward per agent;
- frames become lists of shapes (circles and squares), not grid cells.

Snake becomes the special case: one agent, one integer action, square shapes. The training loop, logging, comparisons and viewer then serve the tank without a rewrite. PPO is hand-written in the same style as DQN. A library implementation (for example Stable-Baselines3) is used later, only as a comparison.

## Data
None external: experience comes from the tank. Evaluation mirrors Snake: fixed, held-out evaluation seeds, 5 training seeds, and mean survival time as the metric. The baselines are a random fish and a scripted "flee" fish that swims directly away from the ball. Significance comes from the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.

## Alternatives considered
- A school from day one — four new concepts at once, and no way to tell which one broke.
- Nearest-objects list instead of rays — a variable-size observation needs padding or attention, which is a lesson of its own.
- Separate interfaces for Snake and the tank — two training loops and two viewers, which defeats Goal 4.
- A ball that learns to hunt — real multi-agent RL; see Not doing.

## What success looks like
- PPO's survival-time curve clearly beats random and the flee fish across 5 seeds, at each curriculum level.
- REINFORCE vs PPO, and the PPO ablations (no clipping, no value baseline, no advantage normalization), show what each piece is for.
- I can watch a replay of the school dodging the ball in the same viewer as Snake.
- Snake's tests still pass after the core change.

## What we're betting on
- A small hand-built PPO learns to dodge within the one-hour CPU budget.
- The flee fish is beatable: it gets pinned against the wall, and a learned fish can anticipate bounces.
- Generalizing the core before M1 is built costs less than adapting it afterwards.

## Not doing
- A learning predator ball — multi-agent competition is its own concept.
- Fish that cooperate, communicate or share a team reward — keeps the school a parameter-sharing exercise.
- Library PPO before the hand-built version works — comparison comes later, as in the brief.
- Physics beyond simple bounces (no currents, no inertia tuning) — dodging is the lesson, not fluid dynamics.

## Open questions
- Exact sizes: tank radius, ball speed, fish speed limits, whisker radius and time limit, and how many fish in the school (spec-writing proposes numbers).
- Reward sizes: survival bonus per step vs the caught penalty.
- Whether REINFORCE vs PPO is a requirement or an optional comparison.
- The project name "Grid RL Lab" no longer fits a round tank; a rename is cosmetic and can wait.
