full text
**Mode: Extend.** The brief exists, and the only idea so far, the fish tank, is Specced and frozen, so this gets its own idea file. Nothing is written until we agree on a direction.

**Restated in project terms:** this adds Environment 3 so that you can learn **co-evolution and self-play**: two populations that learn against each other, where each side's environment is the other side's moving policy. It touches Goal 1 (one named concept), Goal 3 (beats baselines), Goal 4 (replay), Goal 5 (the shared core) and, awkwardly, Goal 2 (the library comparison).

### Honest pushback first
1. **It reverses a scope decision we made deliberately, and that's fine, but it should be a deliberate reversal.** The approved spec excludes "competitive or cooperative multi-agent learning" and "a learned or adaptive ball." The fish-tank idea called multi-agent learning "a separate lesson." This *is* that lesson, so the line narrows to cooperative learning, with your agreement. The brief itself doesn't change.
2. **Naive simultaneous learning usually fails in boring ways.** Both sides learning at once tends to **cycle** (predator learns A, prey counter, predator forgets A…), **collapse** (one side wins outright and the other gets no learning signal) or **chase noise**. That makes the experiments frustrating rather than educational. What turns this into a lesson is a fix: training against a **pool of frozen past opponents** rather than only the latest one. If we don't plan for it, you'll get the failure without the lesson.
3. **"Is it learning?" gets hard to answer.** Survival time against a predator that keeps improving can stay flat while both sides get better. Goal 3's "beats random and scripted, measured over many games" still works, but only against **fixed** opponents. To see co-evolution itself, you need a **cross-play table**: generation *i* of prey against generation *j* of the predator.
4. **Goal 2 breaks here.** Stable-Baselines3 has no simultaneous two-sided self-play. The honest options are:
   - compare a single side trained against a *frozen* opponent with library PPO (a fair, narrow comparison), or
   - accept that this environment has no library comparison.
5. **The core changes again, but less this time.** After S2, the core carries several agents, but they all share **one** policy. A predator and prey need **two policies with different views and maybe different action limits**, on one environment. That's a "teams" layer: agents mapped to policies. It's smaller than the S2 revision, but it's real.
6. **It's built on S2, which isn't built yet.** Reusing the tank physics and your PPO is the right call, but this stage can't start until M2 lands.

### Sharpening questions
1. **What's the lesson's centre?** (a) watching co-evolution dynamics (arms races, cycling); (b) the self-play *fix* (opponent pools from past snapshots); (c) both, as a ladder. My guess is (c): naive first, to see the failure, then the pool, to see it fixed.
2. **How should "learning" be shown?** My guess:
   - each side beats fixed scripted opponents;
   - a cross-play table across saved generations;
   - replays of generation *i* vs generation *j*.
3. **Goal 2:** compare the library on one side against a frozen opponent, or no library comparison for this environment? I'd lean toward the frozen-opponent comparison, to keep the goal honest.
4. **Scale and shape:** one predator against a school of prey (say 6), pellets that respawn? Does the predator learn with the same PPO you wrote in S2?
5. **Constraints:** still under an hour of laptop CPU per training run, and $5 of cloud per stage at most? Self-play runs often need longer. Is 2 hours acceptable here, or should it stay 1?

### Variations
1. **A ladder:**
   - rung 1: prey learn against a scripted predator, and the predator learns against scripted prey (fixed opponents);
   - rung 2: both learn at once;
   - rung 3: both learn against pools of past snapshots.
2. **Simplification, pure chase:** one predator against one prey with no pellets: pursuit and evasion in its simplest form.
3. **Inversion, symmetric self-play:** a game of tag where *one* policy plays both roles. It's the cleanest self-play, but less like what you described.
4. **Expert lens, a league with ratings:** keep every Nth checkpoint, then rate generations against each other with an Elo-style score, so "who's winning over time" becomes one chart.
5. **A generation replay gallery:** pick any prey generation and any predator generation and watch them play. This directly serves "watch the two sides adapt."
6. **The 10x version:** several predators and herding prey, with emergent group tactics. It's spectacular, but it's two concepts.
7. **Foraging as the tension:** pellets force the prey to take risks, so hiding forever isn't optimal. That's what makes the predator's job interesting. I'd keep it.

**My lean:** **1 + 4 + 5 + 7**, built on S2's tank and PPO, with one predator against a school of prey foraging pellets. Snapshot pools come in at rung 3, and a cross-play rating plus a generation replay gallery show the adaptation.

Answer the questions, especially 1, 3 and 5, and I'll converge on a direction.
