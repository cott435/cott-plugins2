I committed the index and the two files as `3b28f66`; RF-02 and RF-03 stay unassigned. One correction to my last message: RF-02's stamped hash is `fdf31b69`, not the placeholder I wrote.

Next is refining the Stage 4 idea. Nothing gets written until the questions below are settled.

This adds **neural return and volatility forecasts, the portfolios they drive, and reinforcement-learning allocation agents**. The goal is for you to learn whether learned forecasting beats S2's classical portfolios out of sample, tested in paper trading. RF-02 and RF-03 are prior art for the forecasting part.

**How might we** tell whether neural forecasts and learned agents really beat simple, classical portfolios, when the data has already been used to choose what goes into them?

## How it fits, and where it collides
- **It serves Goal 4** (later stages on the same trusted data). The brief's "paper trading only, and not in this first stage" already allows it, so the brief probably doesn't change.
- **The spec's out-of-scope list blocks three parts, and you'd be reversing each:**
  - "deep learning and automated feature search";
  - "trading on forecasts: return-seeking backtests, portfolios built from forecasts, and paper trading";
  - "transaction costs and turnover limits".
- **Package boundary.** A neural-network library stack (PyTorch and an RL library) is the kind of dependency that gets its own package, like S2 and S3 did.

## Honest pushback (three things that decide whether this is worth building)
1. **There's no clean out-of-sample period left.** "Built on the features that survived S3" means features chosen *using* S3's one holdout run on 2021 to 2026. Testing S4 on those same years would let that choice leak into the result, and every comparison against S2 there would look better than it is. The only untouched data is the future. I'd make **forward paper trading the deciding test**, with the backtest years used only for development.
2. **"Beat S2" needs a metric.** S2's portfolios were judged on realized variance; forecast portfolios and agents chase return. Comparing them fairly means one shared metric, such as Sharpe ratio after costs, tested against chance with `null-hypothesis-testing`. Months of paper trading give little statistical power, so the decision may take a long time.
3. **PPO agents overfit backtests notoriously.** They need transaction costs, or they learn to churn. They also need many seeds and simple baselines to beat: equal weight, S2's minimum variance, and a forecast-ranked portfolio. I'd treat the agents as a separate, later idea, after the forecasts show they're worth anything.

## Variations
1. **Baselines first:** historical-mean and momentum return forecasts, and an exponentially weighted volatility forecast. Every neural model has to beat these.
2. **Sequence model** (the temporal-fusion-transformer style in RF-02 and RF-03) on S3's surviving features, for next-month return and volatility.
3. **Volatility only:** volatility is far more forecastable than return, and better volatility forecasts can improve S2's risk-parity portfolios directly.
4. **Forecast-ranked portfolio:** long-only, top quintile by forecast and weighted by inverse forecast volatility. That's the simplest forecast-driven portfolio.
5. **PPO allocation agent** trained in a backtest environment with costs, against the same baselines.
6. **Zero-survivor fallback (inversion):** if no S3 feature survives, S4 runs on price history alone, or stops.

**My leaning:** two separate ideas. **S4: Forecasts and forecast portfolios** (1, 2, 3 and 4), then **S5: Learned allocation agents** (5) once S4 shows forecasting adds anything.

## Questions
1. **Split:** two ideas and two stages (forecasting first, then agents), or keep them as one stage?
2. **The deciding test:** forward paper trading only, with backtest years for development? If so, how long a paper-trading period before a verdict? My guess is 12 months, knowing that's low power.
3. **Paper trading mechanics:** simulated inside the project on each daily refresh (no broker, stays within the $50 budget)? Or a broker's paper account (a new external service, which would need its own check before the architecture relies on it)?
4. **Costs and the metric:** a fixed cost per trade, say 5 basis points of turnover, and Sharpe ratio after costs as the shared metric against S2?
5. **Compute:** does your machine have a GPU, or Apple-silicon acceleration? And is there a limit on training time per run?
