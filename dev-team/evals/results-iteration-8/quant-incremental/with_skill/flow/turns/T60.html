# Idea: Allocation Agents

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-17 · **Brief:** [../brief.md](../brief.md)

## In one sentence
This adds reinforcement-learning allocation agents trained with PPO in a point-in-time backtest environment, first choosing weights across a few portfolios and only later across stocks, so I learn whether a learned policy beats the variance-first portfolios net of costs, out of sample and forward in paper trading.

## The problem
A learned trading agent is the easiest way to fool myself. A policy trained over and over on one history of about 180 monthly decisions can memorise that history. Its backtest then looks brilliant and means nothing. Seeds alone move PPO's results a lot, costs punish the churn agents love, and an environment that leaks one day of the future makes any agent look skilled. The question is worth asking only if the environment, the baselines and the count of attempts are as strict as the variance-first and feature stages.

## Who it's for
Me, after the forecast stage, with the variance-first portfolios, the forecast portfolios and their net-of-cost results in hand.

## How it fits
- **Serves:** Goal 8 (forecasts, forecast portfolios and agents judged net of costs against the variance-first portfolios)
- **Touches:** PORT-01–PORT-05 (the portfolios the agent allocates across), WALK-01–WALK-06 (the monthly loop and holding rules the environment follows), EVAL-04 (the significance pattern), and the S4 forecast portfolios, net-of-cost evaluation, model log and forward paper trading
- **Brief changes:** None (Goal 8 already covers agents)
- **Stage:** New S5 (roadmap stage on acceptance), after S4

## Recommended direction
**The environment comes first.** A backtest environment steps one month at a time over the point-in-time S&P 500. Its observation at each rebalance holds only what was known at the previous close: recent returns and volatility of each sleeve, the S4 forecasts, market-wide volatility and the surviving features. The agent's weights are held through the month under the variance-first holding rules, charged 10 bp per side on turnover, and rewarded on the month's net return with a variance penalty.

**Planted environments before real data.** The agents must learn a known optimal policy in a synthetic market. For example, one sleeve earns a planted premium only when an observable regime flag is on. The agent must reach a set share of the oracle policy's Sharpe ratio. On a synthetic market with no edge, it must not beat equal weight beyond chance. No real-data training starts until both pass.

**A meta-allocator first.** The first agent chooses long-only weights across a handful of sleeves: the variance-first minimum-variance, risk-parity and hierarchical risk-parity portfolios, equal weight, and the best S4 forecast portfolio. Its action space is small enough to learn from 180 months and to inspect. It must beat three baselines: equal weight across sleeves, the best single sleeve, and an inverse-volatility rule across sleeves.

**A per-stock agent only if the meta-allocator wins.** If, and only if, the meta-allocator beats the best variance-first portfolio under the full test, a second agent allocates across the top-100 universe under the long-only 5% cap.

**Judged as S4 judges.** The verdict is net-of-cost Sharpe ratio against the best variance-first portfolio, with a paired Sharpe-difference test and a deflated-Sharpe correction over every agent configuration and seed ever trained. Results are reported across at least 5 training seeds, never the best one alone. The null-hypothesis-testing skill owns the tests. Development is walk-forward on 2007–2021, and 2022 onward is reported as already seen. The deciding test is forward paper trading with the policy frozen and fingerprinted.

## Data
- The S1 snapshot, the S2 portfolios and windows, the S3 surviving features and the S4 forecasts and forecast portfolios; no new data or spending.
- About 180 development months, one decision each. Training resamples episodes from development months only (blocks of months, never single months), so the agent sees varied orderings without seeing the future.
- Evaluation: walk-forward with the policy retrained on an expanding window; net-of-cost Sharpe against the best variance-first portfolio and the three sleeve baselines; then forward paper trading.

## Alternatives considered
- A per-stock agent from the start — 100 to 500 actions learned from 180 decisions; kept only behind the meta-allocator's gate.
- Other reinforcement-learning algorithms (soft actor-critic, deep Q-learning) — the user chose PPO; one algorithm keeps the count of attempts small.
- Daily rebalancing — more decisions to learn from, but costs and noise grow faster than the signal.

## What success looks like
- In the planted environment, the agent reaches the target share of the oracle's Sharpe; in the no-edge environment, it doesn't beat equal weight beyond chance.
- The meta-allocator's net-of-cost Sharpe beats every baseline and the best variance-first portfolio after the deflated correction, or the report says it doesn't.
- Every seed's result is reported, and the median is the headline.
- The forward paper-trading record matches the frozen policy's fingerprint.

## What we're betting on
- A regime signal exists that tells sleeves apart, such as crisis versus calm, and the observation can carry it.
- Block-resampled episodes give PPO enough variety without leaking the future.
- PPO training on a few sleeves fits on one laptop in hours, not days.

## Not doing
- Real money, broker orders or live automation — paper trading only, recorded by hand
- Shorting, leverage, options or intraday trading
- Retraining during forward paper trading — the policy stays frozen
- Language-model agents or multi-agent setups
- The per-stock agent before the meta-allocator passes

## Open questions
- Reward: net return minus a variance penalty (which weight?), or a differential Sharpe ratio?
- How high must the planted environment's oracle share be: 80% of the oracle Sharpe?
- What exactly opens the per-stock gate: the development test alone, or also the forward record?
- How many seeds and configurations may be tried before the deflated correction makes a pass unreachable?
