# Quant Research Platform — Spec

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-10-18 · **Brief:** [brief.md](brief.md)

## Summary
A personal research platform for US stocks. It builds a trusted daily dataset of S&P 500 members as they were on each date. On that data it studies risk structure and classical portfolios, then tests candidate signals, neural forecasts and reinforcement-learning agents against those portfolios on one shared out-of-sample scoreboard. A browser app shows the data and results. Everything is research and paper trading; see the [brief](brief.md) for why.

## Users
| User | What they need to do |
|---|---|
| Owner (sole researcher) | Build and refresh the dataset, run each study, compare strategies on the scoreboard, follow a forward paper book, and explore everything in the browser app |

## Scope
**In scope:** Data ingest & validation, Look-ahead check, Exploratory analysis, Explorer app, Market-factor removal, Covariance estimates, Classical portfolios, Shared scoreboard, Feature discovery, Neural forecasts, Forecast-driven portfolios, Paper book, Reinforcement-learning agents.

**Out of scope:**
- Company fundamentals (earnings, balance sheets); features use price and volume only.
- Real money, real orders and any broker connection.
- Intraday data; daily bars only.
- Markets outside US stocks, and stocks never in the S&P 500.
- Other users, accounts or hosting.
- Learned models of any kind in the S2 studies (factor removal, covariance estimates, classical portfolios).

## Shared terms
- **Walk-forward:** on the first trading day of each month, every model and estimate is refit using only the trailing 252 trading days that ended the previous trading day, then held fixed for that month.
- **Portfolio rules:** long-only, fully invested, no stock above 5% weight, rebalanced monthly on the walk-forward dates, holding only stocks that are index members on the rebalance date.
- **Trading cost:** 10 basis points (0.10%) of traded value per side, charged on every rebalance's turnover. The same cost applies to every strategy.
- **Scoring period:** from the first trading day of 2002 to the latest close.
- **Holdout:** the most recent 3 years of the scoring period (1,095 calendar days back from the latest close at the time the holdout is fixed).
- **Purge gap:** for any target that looks h trading days ahead, training data stops at least h trading days before the first test date.

## Features

### Data ingest & validation (`LOAD`)
**Serves:** Goal 1
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** The owner runs one command to build the dataset from scratch, and another to add the latest trading day. Each run downloads from the primary source, cleans the data, cross-checks it against a second, independent source, stores it, and writes a validation report. Research code loads the data as it was known on any chosen date.

**Requirements**
- **LOAD-01** A full build downloads every stock that was an S&P 500 member at any time since 2000-01-03, with its prices, corporate actions and membership dates.
  - Given an empty store, when the owner runs the full build, then the store holds data for every stock in the primary source's membership history from 2000-01-03 to the latest close.
- **LOAD-02** A daily update adds the latest trading day without rebuilding earlier days.
  - Given a store complete to day D-1, when the owner runs the update after day D's close, then day D is added and no stored value for an earlier day changes, unless a corporate action was announced; that case is listed in the run's report.
- **LOAD-03** Cleaning removes or flags impossible values instead of filling them silently.
  - Given a raw row with a price ≤ 0, a high below the low, or negative volume, when cleaned, then the row is excluded from returns and listed in the validation report with its stock, date and reason.
  - Given a missing trading day for a stock that was a member that day, when cleaned, then the day stays missing (never filled with a zero return) and is counted in the report.
- **LOAD-04** Returns are total returns: they include dividends and are corrected for splits.
  - Given a stock with a 2-for-1 split on date S and no price move, when daily returns are computed, then the return on S is 0%, not -50%.
  - Given a stock paying a dividend of 1% of its price on its ex-date, with a close-to-close price fall of 1%, when daily returns are computed, then the return on the ex-date is 0% (± 0.01%).
- **LOAD-05** Every stock's closes are cross-checked against a second, independent source.
  - Given both sources cover a stock, when validated, then the report gives, per stock, the share of days whose split-adjusted closes agree within 0.5%, and the stock passes only if that share is ≥ 99%.
  - Given any day whose closes differ by more than 0.5%, when validated, then that stock, date and both values are listed in the report.
- **LOAD-06** The owner's list of 10 delisted former S&P 500 members is checked on every full build.
  - Given the list of 10 stocks with their membership dates, when the full build finishes, then each one is present, its membership start and end dates match the list, and it has prices up to its last trading day; any mismatch fails the build's validation.
- **LOAD-07** Data is stored once and reloaded without contacting any source.
  - Given a completed build, when research code loads data with no network connection, then it gets the same values the build stored.
- **LOAD-08** The loader returns the data as it was known on a chosen date.
  - Given an as-of date T, when the owner loads the index members, prices and returns, then only members on T are returned as current members, and no value dated after T is returned.
  - Given a stock that left the index on date E, when loading as of a date before E, then the stock is included; when loading as of a date after E, it is not a current member but its history up to E stays available.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A source is unreachable or rejects the credentials → the run stops with a message naming the source; the store is left as it was before the run.
- The second source lacks a stock (common for delisted names) → that stock is marked "not cross-checked" in the report; it does not fail the build.
- A stock changes its ticker → its history is kept as one stock across the change.
- A stock leaves and later rejoins the index → both membership periods are kept.
- The update is run on a non-trading day → it reports "no new trading day" and changes nothing.

### Look-ahead check (`LEAK`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** Any step in the platform that produces values by date can be run through one shared check. The check runs the step on the full data and again on data cut off at a chosen date, and reports any output on or before that date that changed. Every later step that fits or estimates anything is required to pass it.

**Requirements**
- **LEAK-01** The check reruns a step on data cut off at a date and confirms that outputs up to that date don't change.
  - Given a step and a cut-off date T, when checked, then every output dated on or before T is identical (to 1e-10 relative) whether the step saw data to T or to the latest close.
- **LEAK-02** The check reports which outputs moved.
  - Given a step that uses one future value, when checked at any T before that value's date, then the check fails and names the first output date and column that changed.
- **LEAK-03** Every step that fits, estimates or scores anything by date passes the check at 3 or more cut-off dates spread across the scoring period before it is used in a study.
  - Given a study run, when any step it uses has not passed the check at 3 or more cut-off dates, then the study refuses to run and names the step.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A step whose output has no date (a single summary number) → the check is run on the dated inputs it consumes, and the step must declare the date its output is as of.
- A cut-off date that is not a trading day → the previous trading day is used, and the report says so.

### Exploratory analysis (`EXPL`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 5
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** After a build, the owner runs an exploratory analysis of the dataset with the `data-analysis` skill and gets a report on its coverage, return distributions and gaps, used to judge whether the data is fit for the later studies.

**Requirements**
- **EXPL-01** Coverage is summarised over time.
  - Given a built dataset, when the analysis runs, then it reports per year the number of index members, the number with prices on every trading day, and the number of missing member-days.
- **EXPL-02** Return distributions and tails are summarised, using the `data-analysis` skill.
  - Given a built dataset, when the analysis runs, then it reports per year the mean, standard deviation, skew, excess kurtosis and 1st/99th percentiles of daily returns across members.
  - Given any daily return beyond ±50%, when the analysis runs, then it lists the stock, date and value for review.
- **EXPL-03** Correlation structure is summarised.
  - Given a built dataset, when the analysis runs, then it reports the average pairwise correlation of daily returns among members per year.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A year with fewer than 400 members with prices → flagged in the report as low coverage.

### Explorer app (`VIEW`)
**Serves:** Goal 5
**Stage:** S1
**Behavior:** A browser app on the owner's machine to explore the dataset. The owner picks a date to see the index members on it, opens any stock's price and return history, and reads the latest validation report. Each later feature adds its own views to the same app.

**Requirements**
- **VIEW-01** The owner sees the index members on any chosen date.
  - Given a date from 2000-01-03 to the latest close, when chosen, then the app lists exactly the members the loader returns for that date.
- **VIEW-02** The owner sees one stock's history.
  - Given a stock, when opened, then the app shows its daily closes and total returns over a chosen date range, with its membership periods marked.
- **VIEW-03** The owner sees the latest validation report.
  - Given a completed build or update, when the owner opens the report view, then it shows the cross-check pass rate, the 10-name check result, and the listed problem rows.
- **VIEW-04** The app shows progress of a running build or update without a page reload.
  - Given a build or update is running, when the owner has the app open, then its progress (stocks done out of total) updates at least every 5 seconds.
- **VIEW-05** The app reads only stored data and stored results.
  - Given the network is down, when the owner opens any view, then it still shows the stored data.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A stock with no data in the chosen range → the view says "no data in range" instead of showing an empty chart.
- No build has been run yet → every view says so and points to the build command.

### Market-factor removal (`FACT`)
**Serves:** Goal 2
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Each month, the platform finds the dominant common movements of member returns over the trailing window and removes them, leaving residual (market-removed) returns. The factors are the principal components of the standardised returns. The owner sees how many components were removed and how much the correlation between stocks fell.

**Requirements**
- **FACT-01** Factors are the top k principal components of the members' standardised daily returns, fit on the walk-forward window only.
  - Given a refit date, when factors are fit, then they use only returns from the trailing 252 trading days, and the step passes the look-ahead check.
- **FACT-02** Residual returns are each stock's returns minus their fitted exposure to the k factors, with exposures from the same window.
  - Given a month after a refit, when residuals are computed for each day of that month, then they use the exposures fixed at the refit date.
- **FACT-03** The number of factors k is chosen from the window's eigenvalues, using only that window.
  - Given a refit date, when k is chosen, then k is the number of eigenvalues of the window's correlation matrix above the random-matrix noise edge (defined in COV-03), with at least 1.
- **FACT-04** Removing the factors lowers the correlation between stocks.
  - Given the scoring period, when residuals are computed, then the mean absolute pairwise correlation of residual returns is reported per year beside that of raw returns, and is lower in every year.
- **FACT-05** Stocks with short or broken histories are handled without biasing the panel.
  - Given a member with fewer than 200 of the 252 window days, when factors are fit, then it is left out of that refit and listed, and its gaps are never filled with zero returns.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A stock joins the index mid-month → it gets residuals from the next refit on which it has 200 or more window days.
- A refit window shows near-equal top eigenvalues → k is still chosen by the noise edge; the refit is listed as unstable in the report.

### Covariance estimates (`COV`)
**Serves:** Goal 1, Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Each month, the platform builds three covariance estimates for the members from the trailing window: the plain sample estimate, a shrinkage estimate, and a noise-filtered estimate. It compares how well each predicts next month's realised risk. The comparison is studied with the `data-analysis` skill and tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.

**Requirements**
- **COV-01** The sample estimate is the plain sample covariance of the window's daily returns.
  - Given a refit date, when estimated, then it equals the sample covariance of the trailing 252 days' returns for the members kept by FACT-05.
- **COV-02** The shrinkage estimate pulls the sample estimate toward a structured target, with a shrinkage intensity chosen from the window's data.
  - Given a refit date, when estimated, then the intensity is between 0 and 1, is reported, and is chosen using only the window.
- **COV-03** The noise-filtered estimate replaces the correlation eigenvalues that fall inside the random-matrix noise band with their average.
  - Given a window of T days and N stocks, when estimated, then eigenvalues of the correlation matrix below the upper edge (1 + √(N/T))² of the noise distribution expected for pure noise of that shape are replaced by their average, the trace is kept, and the result is turned back into a covariance with the sample variances.
- **COV-04** Every estimate is usable by the optimisers.
  - Given any estimate, when checked, then it is symmetric and positive definite (smallest eigenvalue > 0).
- **COV-05** The three estimates are compared on how well they predict the next month's realised covariance, using the `data-analysis` skill.
  - Given the scoring period, when compared, then the report gives each estimate's monthly error against the next month's realised covariance, its eigenvalue spectrum, and its stability from month to month.
- **COV-06** A shrinkage or noise-filtered estimate counts as better only if it lowers out-of-sample variance significantly against the sample estimate.
  - Given the minimum-variance portfolios built on each estimate (PORT-01) over the scoring period, when compared with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill, then the result states, for shrinkage and for noise-filtered, whether realised variance is lower than with the sample estimate at 5% significance (after correcting for the two comparisons).

**Edge cases & errors**
- More stocks than window days (N > T) → the sample estimate is singular; it is still reported, and the portfolios that need an invertible estimate use the smallest stable fix (a tiny ridge, reported) for the sample estimate only.

### Classical portfolios (`PORT`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** Each month, the platform builds three classical portfolios on each covariance estimate: minimum variance, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity. That gives nine portfolios, all following the portfolio rules, all scored on the shared scoreboard. None uses return forecasts.

**Requirements**
- **PORT-01** The minimum-variance portfolio has the lowest predicted variance allowed by the portfolio rules.
  - Given an estimate, when optimised, then no other weight vector meeting the portfolio rules has a lower predicted variance (to solver tolerance 1e-8).
- **PORT-02** The risk-parity portfolio gives every stock an equal share of predicted portfolio risk, within the portfolio rules.
  - Given an estimate, when built without hitting the 5% cap, then each stock's share of predicted risk is within 1% (relative) of 1/N.
- **PORT-03** The hierarchical risk-parity portfolio groups stocks by correlation into a tree and splits weight down the tree in inverse proportion to each branch's predicted risk.
  - Given an estimate, when built, then the stock order follows the tree's leaves, and at each split the two branches' weights are in inverse proportion to their predicted variances.
- **PORT-04** Every portfolio follows the portfolio rules.
  - Given any rebalance, when weights are produced, then they are ≥ 0, sum to 1 (± 1e-9), are ≤ 5% each, and cover only members on that date.
- **PORT-05** No portfolio uses return forecasts or any learned model.
  - Given the inputs to any classical portfolio, when inspected, then they are the covariance estimate and the member list only.

**Edge cases & errors**
- The optimiser fails to converge → that month falls back to the previous month's weights, renormalised over current members, and is flagged on the scoreboard.
- A held stock leaves the index mid-month → it is held to the next rebalance (or sold at its last price if delisted), and the trade is charged the trading cost.

### Shared scoreboard (`SCORE`)
**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
**Stage:** S2
**Behavior:** One scoreboard scores every strategy in the project the same way: walk-forward, out of sample, same period, same trading costs. It reports after-cost Sharpe ratio and realised variance, and tests claimed differences against chance. The app shows the scoreboard and each strategy's history.

**Requirements**
- **SCORE-01** Every strategy is scored on the same dates with the same costs.
  - Given two strategies on the scoreboard, when compared, then both cover the same trading days and both are charged the same trading cost.
- **SCORE-02** The scoreboard reports after-cost Sharpe ratio for each strategy.
  - Given a strategy's daily after-cost returns, when scored, then the annualised Sharpe ratio is the mean over standard deviation times √252, using after-cost returns in excess of zero.
- **SCORE-03** The scoreboard reports realised variance for each strategy.
  - Given a strategy's daily after-cost returns, when scored, then annualised realised variance (daily variance × 252) is reported for the whole period and per year.
- **SCORE-04** The scoreboard reports turnover and total cost paid.
  - Given a strategy, when scored, then average monthly turnover and total trading cost paid are reported.
- **SCORE-05** Any claim that one strategy beats another is tested with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
  - Given two strategies and a metric, when compared, then the result gives the test used, the p-value after correction for the number of comparisons made in that study, and whether it is significant at 5%.
- **SCORE-06** "Beats the classical portfolios" has one fixed meaning.
  - Given a candidate strategy and the best S2 portfolio by after-cost Sharpe ratio over the same period, when compared, then the candidate beats it only if its after-cost Sharpe ratio is higher at 5% significance (SCORE-05) and its realised variance is ≤ 1.25 × that portfolio's.
- **SCORE-07** The holdout is used once.
  - Given the holdout, when any S3, S4 or S5 study selects features, tunes or chooses a model, then no holdout day is used; when a final scoreboard run on the holdout is made, it is recorded with its date, and a second run on the same holdout for the same strategy is refused unless the owner marks it as a deliberate rerun, which the scoreboard then shows.
- **SCORE-08** The app shows the scoreboard.
  - Given scored strategies, when the owner opens the scoreboard view, then it shows each strategy's after-cost Sharpe ratio, realised variance, turnover and cumulative after-cost return chart, filterable by stage.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A strategy missing days in the period → it is not ranked; the scoreboard lists the missing days.

### Feature discovery (`FEAT`)
**Serves:** Goal 4
**Stage:** S3
**Behavior:** The owner builds candidate features from past prices and volume, such as momentum, reversal, volatility and volume changes. Each feature is tested for whether it adds predictive information about future residual returns, using a learned model. Only features that pass a significance test with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill survive to S4.

**Requirements**
- **FEAT-01** Every candidate feature uses only past prices and volume.
  - Given a feature value for stock s on date t, when its inputs are traced, then they are prices and volume of stocks up to and including t only, and the feature passes the look-ahead check.
- **FEAT-02** Targets are future residual returns over 1, 5 and 21 trading days.
  - Given date t and horizon h, when the target is built, then it is the sum of the stock's residual returns over days t+1 to t+h, and never includes day t's return.
- **FEAT-03** A feature's information gain is the improvement in out-of-sample prediction of the target when a learned model is given the feature, compared with the same model without it.
  - Given a feature, a horizon and the walk-forward with a purge gap of at least h days, when tested, then the gain is reported per refit and on average.
- **FEAT-04** A feature survives only if its information gain passes a test against chance with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
  - Given all candidate features and horizons tested, when assessed, then a feature survives only if its gain is significant at 5% after correction for the number of features × horizons tested, against a null where the feature carries no information.
- **FEAT-05** Feature studies never use the holdout.
  - Given the holdout, when features are built, tested or chosen, then no holdout day is in any training or test set.
- **FEAT-06** The app shows each feature's result.
  - Given a completed feature study, when the owner opens the feature view, then each feature shows its gain over time, its corrected p-value and whether it survived.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A feature undefined for a stock on a date (too little history) → that stock-date is left out of that feature's test, never filled with zero; the count left out is reported.
- Two features nearly identical (correlation > 0.95) → both are tested, and the pair is listed so a survivor can be read with care.

### Neural forecasts (`FCST`)
**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Neural network models that read a sequence of past days for each stock forecast its future return and its volatility over 1, 5 and 21 trading days. They use only the features that survived S3. The forecasts are compared with simple baselines on data never trained on.

**Requirements**
- **FCST-01** Forecast models are neural networks that read a sequence of past days of each stock's inputs.
  - Given a stock and an origin date t, when forecast, then the model's input is a window of that stock's surviving features (and past returns) for days up to and including t.
- **FCST-02** Forecasts cover future return and future volatility over 1, 5 and 21 trading days.
  - Given an origin date t, when forecast, then the model outputs the return and the realised volatility over days t+1 to t+h for each h in 1, 5, 21.
- **FCST-03** Models use only features that survived S3.
  - Given a forecast model, when its inputs are listed, then every feature on the list is marked as survived in the S3 study.
- **FCST-04** Training and testing follow the walk-forward with a purge gap of at least 21 trading days, and never use the holdout until the final run.
  - Given any training run, when its data is checked, then the last training target ends before the first test date, and no holdout day is used.
- **FCST-05** Return forecasts are compared with simple baselines.
  - Given the test periods, when scored, then the forecast's error and rank correlation with realised returns are reported beside a zero-return baseline and a trailing-mean baseline, and the forecast counts as better only if the difference is significant at 5% with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
- **FCST-06** Volatility forecasts are compared with simple baselines.
  - Given the test periods, when scored, then the forecast's error against realised volatility is reported beside a trailing-volatility baseline and an exponentially weighted volatility baseline, and the forecast counts as better only if the difference is significant at 5% with the `null-hypothesis-testing` skill.
- **FCST-07** Training is reproducible.
  - Given the same data and the same random seed, when a model is trained twice, then its forecasts match to 1e-6.
- **FCST-08** The app shows forecasts against outcomes.
  - Given completed forecasts, when the owner opens the forecast view for a stock, then it shows forecast and realised return and volatility per horizon over time.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A stock delisted before t+h → its target uses returns up to its last trading day (including the delisting return), and it is never dropped from the test because it left.
- Training fails to improve on the validation part of a window → that refit uses the baseline forecast, flagged in the report.

### Forecast-driven portfolios (`FPORT`)

**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** Portfolios whose weights come from the S4 forecasts, under the same portfolio rules as the classical portfolios. They are scored on the shared scoreboard against the best S2 portfolio.

- Forecasts missing for some members on a rebalance date → those members get zero weight that month, and the count is reported.

### Paper book (`PAPER`)
**Serves:** Goal 3
**Stage:** S4
**Behavior:** After each daily update, every strategy the owner has added to the paper book records the trades it would make and tracks its simulated after-cost profit and loss from that day forward. No real order is ever sent.

**Requirements**
- **PAPER-01** The owner adds or removes a strategy from the paper book.
  - Given a scored strategy, when the owner adds it, then its paper record starts on the next trading day with a stated starting value of 1,000,000 simulated dollars.
- **PAPER-02** After each daily update, each paper strategy records its simulated trades and value.

- **PAPER-03** Paper results are recorded forward only.
  - Given a past day's paper record, when any later run happens, then that record never changes.
- **PAPER-04** The app shows the paper book.
  - Given paper strategies, when the owner opens the paper view, then each shows its value over time, its trades, and its after-cost Sharpe ratio and realised variance since it started.

**Edge cases & errors**
- A daily update is missed for some days → the next update records the missing days in order; it never skips them.
- A paper strategy holds a stock that is delisted → it is sold at its last price on its last trading day, and the cost is charged.

### Reinforcement-learning agents (`AGENT`)
**Serves:** Goal 3, Goal 4
**Stage:** S5
**Behavior:** Trading and allocation agents learn by trial and error in a backtest environment built from the historical data. Training uses proximal policy optimisation, which limits how far each update can move the policy away from the previous one. The agents are scored on the shared scoreboard against the best S2 portfolio.

**Requirements**
- **AGENT-01** The backtest environment replays history one trading day at a time and gives the agent only what was known that day.
  - Given day t, when the agent observes, then it sees members, prices, returns and features up to t only, and the environment passes the look-ahead check.
- **AGENT-02** The agent's actions are portfolio weights that follow the portfolio rules.
  - Given any action, when applied, then the resulting weights satisfy PORT-04 and the trading cost is charged on their turnover.
- **AGENT-03** The agent's reward is its after-cost return, with risk counted.
  - Given a step, when the reward is computed, then it is the after-cost portfolio return minus a variance penalty, whose weight is fixed before training and reported.
- **AGENT-04** Agents are trained with proximal policy optimisation: each policy update is limited by clipping the ratio of new to old action probabilities.
  - Given a training update, when it is applied, then the clipping range (default 0.2) is recorded with the run.
- **AGENT-05** Agents are trained and tuned on the walk-forward, never on the holdout.
  - Given any training or tuning run, when its data is checked, then no holdout day is used.
- **AGENT-06** Each agent is judged against the best S2 portfolio on the holdout.
  - Given the final holdout run, when compared by SCORE-06, then the scoreboard states "beats" or "does not beat" for each agent.
- **AGENT-07** Training is reproducible.
  - Given the same data and seed, when an agent is trained twice, then its holdout after-cost returns match to 1e-6.

**Edge cases & errors**
- Training diverges (reward not finite) → the run stops, is reported as failed, and no agent from it reaches the scoreboard.
