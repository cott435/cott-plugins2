### RF-02: Sequence-model window builder (`references/tft_dataset.py`)
- **Purpose:** turns a (ticker, date) panel into the fixed-length windows a Temporal Fusion Transformer expects. Inputs are grouped as static, known in advance, and observed only, and samples are split by forecast-origin date.
- **Key parts:** `WindowSpec`, `add_calendar_features`, `encode_static`, `normalise_observed`, `build_windows`, `split_by_origin`.
- **Use for:** the dataset step of a sequence forecaster.
- **Caveats:**
  - **No purge or embargo** in the split, so training targets reach into validation, and stride-1 windows overlap heavily.
  - **It drops data without saying so:** rows without a target, which cuts delisted names' last days, and short tickers.
  - **It assumes consecutive rows are consecutive trading days.**
  - **Static categories leak and go stale:** the vocabulary is built on the full sample, and each stock keeps its first sector forever.
  - **Missing values become zeros:** NaN and warm-up rows are filled with 0.
  - **Smaller issues:** calendar features ignore US holidays, parameters are hard-coded, and it uses pandas.

### RF-03: Forward return and volatility targets (`references/tft_targets.py`)
- **Purpose:** builds forward log-return and forward realized-volatility labels over several horizons.
- **Key parts:** `log_returns`, `forward_return`, `forward_volatility`, `make_targets`, `demean_cross_section`, `drop_incomplete`.
- **Use for:** the label step of a forecaster. Its (t, t+h] window alignment is a good pattern for a golden test.
- **Caveats:**
  - **Horizons count rows, not trading days,** so a gap in a stock's dates silently stretches the window.
  - **Survivorship bias:** `drop_incomplete` throws away delisting returns.
  - **Not S3's residual target:** `demean_cross_section` only subtracts the equal-weight mean.
  - **It looks ahead:** it's built on adjusted closes, which carry later splits and dividends (AD-05).
  - **Smaller issues:** volatility uses raw squared returns, there are no input checks, and it uses pandas.
